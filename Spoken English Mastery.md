## 1. Course Introduction & Philosophy

- The course promises a **complete transformation** — not just sentence memorization, but learning **how to think in English**, speak naturally, and show confidence through body language, tone, and vocabulary — together.
- **Communication ≠ only speaking.** It is built from five pillars taught across this course:
  1. **Vocabulary** — the range of words you command
  2. **Grammar** — just enough to build correct, natural sentences
  3. **Listening** — active, attentive listening (most people skip this step)
  4. **Practical Application** — using what you learn in real conversations, not just notebooks
  5. **Personality & Body Language** — confidence, tone, expressions, posture
- **Core teaching philosophy:** Most spoken-English courses only make you memorize sentences. Real communication requires learning _how to think_ in the language.
- **Key mindset instruction:** Be kind to yourself while learning. Humans are naturally harsh on themselves — don't quit after a few unsuccessful minutes. Give yourself a genuine, patient chance, the same way you'd want a teacher to give you one.
- You may go through the course in one sitting or chapter-wise. Chapter-wise is recommended — cramming 12 hours in one day can lead to "saturation" (boredom/burnout) rather than retention.
- If you take breaks, **note down where you stopped**, revise that point briefly, and then continue — don't just restart randomly.
- **Highest-paid skill of 2025 (per job postings/LinkedIn/Naukri.com listings):** Good communication skills — almost every job listing explicitly asks for it.
- **English as an "interlinking language":** India (and South Asia generally) has dozens of native languages. English acts as the common bridge between speakers who don't share a native tongue — e.g., a Hindi speaker traveling to a Tamil- or Bangla-speaking region can still communicate basic needs in English.
- Improving communication does **not** mean disrespecting or abandoning your mother tongue. It is presented purely as an _additional skill_, not a replacement for your identity or language pride.

## 2. Self-Assessment Quiz

Before starting, test yourself honestly (don't Google the answers):

1. What does the word **"benevolent"** mean? _(Correct: kind and generous — Option A)_
2. Fill in the blank: "He \_\_\_ to the gym every morning." (he go / he goes / he gone / he going) _(Correct: "goes")_
3. How would you politely ask someone for help?
   - ❌ "Help me" (too demanding)
   - ❌ "I need help" (still demanding, no politeness marker)
   - ❌ "You should help me" (sounds like advice/order)
   - ✅ **"Could you help me, please?"** (correct — uses "please" and a polite modal)
4. Which sentence is grammatically correct?
   - ❌ She don't like apples.
   - ❌ She doesn't likes apples.
   - ✅ **She doesn't like apples.**
   - ❌ She not like apples.

## 3. Foundations of Communication

### Common Mistakes People Make

1. **Resistance** — "I don't need English, why should I learn it?" This attitude is described as an _automatic killer_ of motivation. It also creates a false sense of security that you'll never need the skill — which is risky, because life circumstances (business expansion, relocation, new opportunities) can change quickly.
2. **Myth: Communication = Speaking only.** This is called out as _the_ biggest misconception. Real communication is about **clearly conveying your ideas AND understanding what the other person is saying.**
   - **Correct priority order: Understanding → Listening → Speaking**
   - Even if you can only respond in one word initially, respond — but make sure you deeply understood first.
3. Communication has **two types**:
   - **Verbal communication** — what comes out of your mouth
   - **Non-verbal communication** — gestures, body language, confidence, facial expressions, everything communicated _without_ speaking
   - Example given: Someone standing with an unintentionally arrogant posture can create a bad impression before they even speak a word.
4. **Mindfulness** is called out as a _very important_ 3rd element of good communication:
   - Example: A boss returns from a stressful meeting, sees an unfinished task, and immediately yells at the employee — without asking why. A mindful person would instead ask _"What happened? Why is this incomplete?"_ first. This single habit prevents most unnecessary conflicts/disputes.
   - Today's world has very little patience for follow-up explanations — mistakes are often punished immediately, so being mindful _before_ reacting protects your relationships.
5. **The 4th pillar — showing "brainpower" through impactful information:** Occasionally dropping a fact, statistic, an old saying/proverb, or a well-placed joke pulls a disengaged listener's attention back in. This is referred to as "shock value" or **"protsahit karna"** (encouraging/invigorating the conversation) using data, quotes, or unexpected information.

### Steps to Improve Communication

| Step                            | Description                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1. Decide**                   | Commit clearly: _"I have to do this — it's important for my future growth."_ Without clear intent, motivation collapses at the first obstacle.                                                                                                    |
| **2. Work on the Basics First** | Don't skip to advanced content just because you're "excited." Go step-by-step; it's a gradual process.                                                                                                                                            |
| **3. Be Consistent**            | Don't quit after 1, 10, or 15 days. The instructor mentions learning conversational English herself in about **30 days** through daily, consistent effort — but leveling up (new vocabulary, grammar refinement) is an ongoing, lifelong process. |
| **4. Practice Actively**        | You don't need another person to practice with — practice alone: talk to yourself, record voice notes, record videos of yourself speaking, and review them.                                                                                       |
| **5. Appreciate Yourself**      | Acknowledge small improvements out loud — self-motivation is essential since no one else will consistently do it for you.                                                                                                                         |

> **Key belief system reinforced throughout:** _"The universe does not revolve around you."_ Nobody is scrutinizing your small mistakes as intensely as you fear. Give yourself permission to fail and learn — embarrassment fades fast; growth compounds.

### Practical Application Tip

Don't just memorize 1,000 sentences and expect fluency — "cramming doesn't work; you'll forget within three days." Learn a small set of words/structures and immediately try to **apply them in real conversations** — with family, friends, coworkers, or even a mirror/camera.

## 5. Grammar Foundations

### 5.1 Parts of Speech

| Part of Speech   | Function                                                                    | Examples                                          |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| **Noun**         | Naming word — person, place, thing, concept, emotion, weather, relationship | teacher, Lucknow, book, education, anger, monsoon |
| **Pronoun**      | Replaces a noun to avoid repetition                                         | he, she, it, they, we, I, you                     |
| **Verb**         | Shows action, time (tense), or mood                                         | run, is, can, teach                               |
| **Adverb**       | Describes/modifies a verb (the _quality_ of an action)                      | sings **well**, runs **quickly**                  |
| **Adjective**    | Describes/modifies a noun or pronoun (the _quality_ of a person/thing)      | **tall** man, **intelligent** teacher             |
| **Preposition**  | Shows time, place, or direction relative to a noun/pronoun                  | on, in, at, near, up                              |
| **Conjunction**  | Joins words, phrases, or clauses                                            | and, but, so, for                                 |
| **Interjection** | Expresses sudden emotion                                                    | Wow! Oh! Hurray! Alas!                            |
| **Article**      | Specifies a noun                                                            | a, an, the                                        |

> **Full worked example sentence:** _"Wow! The tall man swiftly climbed up the steep hill and he reached the top."_

| Word    | Part of Speech                               |
| ------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Wow     | Interjection (sudden emotion)                |
| The     | Article                                      |
| tall    | Adjective (describes "man")                  |
| man     | Noun                                         |
| climbed | Verb                                         |
| up      | Preposition (shows direction)                |
| swiftly | Adverb (describes _how_ he climbed)          |
| the     | Article                                      |
| steep   | Adjective (describes "hill")                 |
| hill    | Noun                                         |
| and     | Conjunction                                  |
| he      | Pronoun (replaces "man" to avoid repetition) |
| reached | Verb                                         |
| the     | Article                                      |
| top     | Noun                                         |

**Why pronouns matter:** Repeating a name over and over ("Vasim said this, Vasim did that, Vasim's class...") sounds clunky ("clunky sentence"). Pronouns (he/she/it/they) eliminate that repetition, exactly the way you'd naturally do in Bangla/Hindi conversation.

### 5.2 Nouns in Detail

**Definition:** Naming words — can name a person, a place, a thing, a relationship, a gender, a concept, an emotion, weather, or anything with an identity (even abstract ones like "education," "money," "justice," "equality," "anger," "love," "jealousy").

> Even abstract, non-physical things are nouns: _"You can't see the color, height, or shape of anger — but it has a name, so it is a noun."_

#### Number: Singular vs Plural

| Rule                                                           | Example                                                                     |
| -------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Add **-s** for most nouns                                      | teacher → teachers, pen → pens                                              |
| Add **-es** for words ending in **ch, ss, sh, z, x**           | bench → benches, glass → glasses, bush → bushes, buzz → buzzes, fix → fixes |
| Words ending in **consonant + y** → remove "y," add **"-ies"** | baby → babies, lady → ladies, city → cities, hobby → hobbies                |
| Words ending in **vowel + y** (like "boy," "day")              | Simply add -s (no rule change needed here per general English rule)         |
| **Irregular plurals (no change)**                              | sheep → sheep, fish → fish, hair → hair                                     |

#### Countable vs Uncountable Nouns

| Type            | Can it be counted (1, 2, 3...)? | Can be Plural? | Examples                                                                                               |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Countable**   | Yes                             | Yes            | pen(s), finger(s), boy(s), dog(s), place(s)                                                            |
| **Uncountable** | No                              | Never          | water, money, sugar, oil (as a general substance), stars (technically an "innumerable" case), currency |

> ❌ "I have a lot of moneys" — Incorrect
> ✅ "I have a lot of money" — Correct
> ❌ "There is a lot of waters in my bottle" — Incorrect
> ✅ "There is a lot of water in my bottle" — Correct

**Important distinction:** "Money" is a concept and uncountable, but "currency" units (rupees, taka, dollars, pounds, yen) ARE countable: _"I have 500 taka"_ is fine, but _"I have a lot of moneys"_ is wrong.

### 5.3 Verbs & Verb Forms

**Definition:** Verbs show action, and they change form to indicate **time (tense)** and **mood**.

#### The 5 Forms of a Verb

| Form   | Formal Name            | Example (EAT) | Example (READ)            | Usage                                              |
| ------ | ---------------------- | ------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **V1** | Base Form / Infinitive | eat           | read                      | Simple Present / Simple Future                     |
| **V2** | Past Form              | ate           | read _(pronounced "red")_ | Simple Past                                        |
| **V3** | Past Participle        | eaten         | read _(pronounced "red")_ | Perfect tenses (after has/have/had), Passive Voice |
| **V4** | Present Participle     | eating        | reading                   | Continuous tenses                                  |
| **V5** | Third Person Singular  | eats          | reads                     | Simple Present with a singular subject (he/she/it) |

> ⚠️ **Irregular verbs — memorize as exceptions:**
>
> - cut → cut → cut (same in all 3 forms)
> - read → read → read (**spelling** stays the same, but **pronunciation** changes from "reed" to "red" in V2/V3)
> - come → came → come
> - go → went → gone
> - see → saw → seen
> - sing → sang → sung
> - write → wrote → written

#### Types of Verbs

1. **Action Verbs** — physical/mental actions (eat, run, think, teach)
2. **Primary Auxiliary Verbs** — show TIME: is/am/are, was/were, do/does/did, has/have/had
3. **Modal Auxiliary Verbs** — show MOOD/attitude: can, could, will, would, shall, should, may, might, must, ought to

#### Third Rule for Verbs — Showing Feeling/Ability (Mood)

Beyond action and time, verbs (specifically modal auxiliaries) also express **capability, willingness, or obligation**:

- "I **can** sing" → shows ability
- "I **will** sing" → shows intention/willingness
- "I **should** sing" → shows a sense of obligation/duty

### 5.4 Sentence Structure Hierarchy

**Hierarchy:** Letter → Word → Phrase → Clause → Sentence

| Unit         | Definition                                                                                                               | Example                                        |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **Word**     | The smallest independent meaningful unit                                                                                 | "TV"                                           |
| **Phrase**   | A group of words that **cannot** contain both a verb and a noun together; functions as ONE single unit within a sentence | "my new TV" (adjective + noun combo — no verb) |
| **Clause**   | A group of words that **can** contain a verb + noun/pronoun combination                                                  | "Tim likes coffee"                             |
| **Sentence** | A group of words conveying a **complete thought and clear meaning**, and it **must** mandatorily contain a verb          | "Ram goes to office."                          |

- **Independent Clause** = complete on its own → CAN function as a full sentence.
  - Example: _"Ram goes to office."_ — complete, makes sense alone.
- **Dependent Clause** = incomplete, needs more information to make sense → CANNOT be a sentence on its own.
  - Example: _"But when Ram goes to office..."_ — leaves the listener asking "then what?"

> **Golden Rule:** A sentence = Group of words **mandatorily carrying a verb** + able to convey a complete thought.

#### Sentence Formula (Basic)

**Subject + Verb + Object**

- **Subject** = the doer of an action / the topic of the sentence
- **Verb** = the action being performed
- **Object** = the receiver of the action

Example: _"I baked a cake."_ → "I" = subject (doer), "baked" = verb (action), "cake" = object (receiver of the baking action).

### 5.5 Types of Sentences

#### A) Positive (Affirmative/Declarative) Sentence

> Used to pass on a piece of information to another person. Contains no "no/not/never."

**Formula:** Subject + Verb + Object _or_ Subject + Auxiliary Verb + Verb + Object

| Example                     | Breakdown                                   |
| --------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| Vinay works hard.           | S + V + Adverb (describes the verb "works") |
| Rashmi is learning English. | S + Aux (is) + V-ing + Object               |

#### B) Negative Sentence

> Contains no/not/never.

**Formula:** Subject + Auxiliary Verb + NOT + Verb + Object

| Example                                                           |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Aman does **not** talk to Harry. _(NOT "Aman not talk to Harry")_ |
| I **do not** scold.                                               |
| They **do not** party.                                            |

> ⚠️ **Critical rule:** Never use **NOT** alone without an auxiliary verb (do/does/did/is/am/are/was/were). This is one of the most common learner mistakes.

#### C) Interrogative Sentence (Questions)

Two types:

1. **Yes/No Questions** — answerable with a simple "yes" or "no"
   **Formula:** Auxiliary Verb + Subject + Verb + Object?
   - _Does Harish eat ice cream?_

2. **Wh- Questions (Explanatory)** — require a detailed answer
   **Formula:** Wh-word + Auxiliary Verb + Subject + Verb + Object?
   - _What does Vikram eat?_
   - Common Wh-words: **What, Which, When, Where, Who, Whom, Whose, Why, How**

#### D) Imperative Sentence

Commands, requests, or instructions (e.g., "Close the door.", "Please sit down."). Mentioned as the 4th sentence type in the course's classification, to be explored further at advanced levels.

## 6. Tenses — Complete Guide (All 12)

### What is "Tense"?

> **Tense = A VERB-BASED method to show the time of an action.** Tense itself doesn't show time directly — the **verb's form change** is what communicates the time.

**Formal definition taught:** _"Tense is a verb-based method to show the time of an action."_

### Main Categories (3 × 4 = 12 Tenses)

| Category    | Sub-types                                       |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| **Past**    | Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous |
| **Present** | Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous |
| **Future**  | Simple, Continuous, Perfect, Perfect Continuous |

### Quick Meaning Guide

| Type                        | Meaning / When to Use                                                                              | Hindi/Bangla-style Identifier        |
| --------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| **Simple** (aka Indefinite) | Habitual actions, repeated actions, universal truths — no exact/precise time is being communicated | "-ता है / -ती है" pattern (habitual) |
| **Continuous**              | An ongoing/in-progress action                                                                      | "-रहा है / रही है" pattern ("-ing")  |
| **Perfect**                 | A completed action, with a connection/impact reaching into the reference time period               | "चुका है / चुकी है" pattern          |
| **Perfect Continuous**      | Action that started earlier and is STILL ongoing (partially complete, ongoing duration)            | "से...रहा है" (since...continuing)   |

### Full 12-Tense Translation Example ("I eat food")

| Tense                      | Example Sentence                                                                        |
| -------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Simple Present             | I eat food.                                                                             |
| Present Continuous         | I am eating food.                                                                       |
| Present Perfect            | I have eaten food.                                                                      |
| Present Perfect Continuous | I have been eating since morning.                                                       |
| Simple Past                | I used to eat food. _(habitual past)_                                                   |
| Past Continuous            | I was eating food.                                                                      |
| Past Perfect               | I had eaten food (before he came).                                                      |
| Past Perfect Continuous    | I had been eating food (before he came).                                                |
| Simple Future              | I will eat food.                                                                        |
| Future Continuous          | I will be eating food.                                                                  |
| Future Perfect             | I will have eaten food.                                                                 |
| Future Perfect Continuous  | _(Rarely used in natural speech — flagged as an "almost never occurring" construction)_ |

### 6.1 Simple Present Tense

**Uses:** Habits, repeated/routine actions, universal truths (e.g., "The bird chirps," "Water boils at 100°C").

| Sentence Type                           | Formula                                    | Example                               |
| --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Positive (plural / I / You / We / They) | Subject + V1 + Object                      | They play a game.                     |
| Positive (singular he/she/it)           | Subject + V5 (verb+s/es) + Object          | She eats food.                        |
| Negative (plural)                       | Subject + do not + V1 + Object             | They do not eat mangoes.              |
| Negative (singular)                     | Subject + does not + V1 + Object           | He does not teach English on Tuesday. |
| Yes/No Question                         | Do/Does + Subject + V1 + Object?           | Does Riya eat ice cream?              |
| Wh- Question                            | Wh-word + Do/Does + Subject + V1 + Object? | What does Vikram eat?                 |

> ⚠️ Never add -s/-es to the verb when "does" is already used in the sentence (❌ "Does she eats" → ✅ "Does she eat").

---

### 6.2 Present Continuous Tense

**Uses:** An action happening RIGHT NOW (ongoing in present time).
**Identifiers:** "-रहा है / रही है / रहे हैं" (-ing pattern)

| Sentence Type     | Formula                                         | Example                        |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Positive          | Subject + is/am/are + V-ing + Object            | I am playing football.         |
| Negative          | Subject + is/am/are + NOT + V-ing + Object      | She is not eating an apple.    |
| Yes/No Question   | Is/Am/Are + Subject + V-ing + Object?           | Is Radhika studying?           |
| Wh- Question      | Wh-word + is/am/are + Subject + V-ing + Object? | What topic is Udisha teaching? |
| Negative Question | Is/Am/Are + Subject + NOT + V-ing + Object?     | Is Ram not playing football?   |

**Auxiliary choice:** am (I) | is (he/she/it/singular noun) | are (we/you/they/plural noun)

---

### 6.3 Present Perfect Tense

**Uses:** An action completed within the present timeline, with an impact/connection that continues to now.
**Identifiers:** "चुका है / चुकी है" or "कर दिया है"

| Sentence Type   | Formula                                     | Example                            |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| Positive        | Subject + has/have + V3 + Object            | She has sung a song.               |
| Negative        | Subject + has/have + NOT + V3 + Object      | They have not finished their work. |
| Yes/No Question | Has/Have + Subject + V3 + Object?           | Have you seen the movie?           |
| Wh- Question    | Wh-word + has/have + Subject + V3 + Object? | Where has Dinesh gone?             |

**Auxiliary choice:** has (singular he/she/it) | have (plural / I / You)

---

### 6.4 Simple Past Tense

**Uses:** A completed action in the past with no ongoing relevance to the present; past habits (with "used to").

| Sentence Type            | Formula                                | Example                                |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Positive (action)        | Subject + V2 + Object                  | He danced at the party.                |
| Positive (quality/state) | Subject + was/were + Adjective         | Palak was angry.                       |
| Past habit               | Subject + used to + V1                 | I used to watch TV when I was a child. |
| Negative                 | Subject + did not + V1 + Object        | Udisha did not teach English.          |
| Yes/No Question          | Did + Subject + V1 + Object?           | Did Suresh eat food?                   |
| Wh- Question             | Wh-word + Did + Subject + V1 + Object? | What did you eat yesterday?            |

> ⚠️ **"Did/Did not" always take V1 (never V2/V3).** This is called "the world's best rule" in the course because it removes any confusion about which verb form to use with "did."

---

### 6.5 Past Continuous Tense

**Uses:** An action that was ongoing at a specific point in the past.
**Identifiers:** "-रहा था / रही थी"

| Sentence Type   | Formula                                        | Example                         |
| --------------- | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| Positive        | Subject + was/were + V-ing + Object            | She was reading a book.         |
| Negative        | Subject + was/were + NOT + V-ing + Object      | They were not playing football. |
| Yes/No Question | Was/Were + Subject + V-ing + Object?           | Were you reading history?       |
| Wh- Question    | Wh-word + was/were + Subject + V-ing + Object? | What were you studying?         |

**Important Distinction — "Read" vs "Study":**

- **Reading** = word-by-word engagement with something (a book, novel, article, newspaper) — _"I am reading a novel."_
- **Studying** = overall academic learning/preparation (school, college, exam prep) — _"I am studying for my exams."_
- ❌ "I am studying a comic" — Incorrect
- ✅ "I am reading a comic" — Correct
- ✅ "I am studying in class 5" — Correct (overall schooling)

---

### 6.6 Past Perfect Tense

**Uses:** ONLY when there are **TWO past actions**, and you need to show which one happened FIRST. The older action = Past Perfect; the next/later action = Simple Past.

> ⚠️ **Critical rule:** Do NOT use "had" just because something is in the past. If there is only ONE past action, use **Simple Past**, not Past Perfect. Past Perfect exists specifically to sequence two past events.

**Formula:** Subject + had + V3 + Object

| Example                                           | Sequence                                      |
| ------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| She **had finished** her work before the meeting. | 1) Work finished → 2) Meeting started         |
| The patient **had gone** before the doctor came.  | 1) Patient left → 2) Doctor arrived           |
| Before he came, I **had eaten** food.             | 1) I ate → 2) He came                         |
| They **had not left** when I reached.             | 1) (Negative) They hadn't left → 2) I reached |

- Negative: Subject + had + NOT + V3 + Object
- Yes/No Question: Had + Subject + V3 + Object?
- Wh- Question: Wh-word + had + Subject + V3 + Object?

---

### 6.7 Simple Future Tense

**Uses:** Willingness/voluntary actions, offers, promises, plans/intentions, and predictions.

**Formula:** Subject + will/shall + V1 + Object

| Use Case                                     | Example                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Willingness/Voluntary action                 | I will go to the market.                                       |
| Promise                                      | I will always support you. / I will always take care of you.   |
| Plan/Intention                               | We will meet at 9 o'clock. / We will all wake up at 5 o'clock. |
| Prediction                                   | India will win the match. / It will rain tomorrow.             |
| Offer (formal, with "Shall," typically I/We) | Shall we go for a walk?                                        |

- Negative: Subject + will not (**won't**) + V1 + Object
- Yes/No Question: Will + Subject + V1 + Object?
- Wh- Question: Wh-word + Will + Subject + V1 + Object?

**Note on "Shall":** Described as a somewhat old-fashioned/regal-sounding word (associated with old royal courts — "Shall we dance?"). "Will" is generally sufficient for beginners; "Shall" becomes more useful at advanced levels of speaking.

---

### 6.8 Future Continuous Tense

**Uses:** An action that WILL BE ongoing at a specific future time; planned actions with duration; time-sensitive statements.

**Formula:** Subject + will be + V-ing + Object

| Example                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------- |
| She will be singing at the concert.                       |
| Tomorrow at 5 PM, the meeting will be going on.           |
| She will be presenting at the conference when you arrive. |
| I will be studying at this time tomorrow.                 |

- Negative: Subject + will **NOT be** + V-ing + Object _(NOT "will be not")_
- Yes/No Question: Will + Subject + **be** + V-ing + Object?
- Wh- Question: Wh-word + will + Subject + **be** + V-ing + Object?

---

### 6.9 Future Perfect Tense

**Uses:** An action that WILL BE COMPLETED by a specific point in the future.

**Formula:** Subject + will have + V3 + Object

| Example                                                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------- |
| He will have gone by then.                              |
| They will have completed the project by next Friday.    |
| I will have learned English by 2026.                    |
| She will not have submitted the report by the deadline. |

- Negative: Subject + will **NOT have** + V3 + Object _(NOT "will have not")_
- Yes/No Question: Will + Subject + **have** + V3 + Object?
- Wh- Question: Wh-word + will + Subject + **have** + V3 + Object?

> _(Future Perfect Continuous exists grammatically but is explicitly flagged in the course as almost never used in natural, practical speech.)_

## 7. Auxiliary & Modal Verbs — Full Breakdown

### 7.1 Primary Auxiliary Verbs (show TIME)

| Verb        | Forms                                                             | Used For                              |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| **To Be**   | is, am, are (present) → was, were (past) → been (V3) → being (V4) | State of being, continuous tenses     |
| **To Do**   | do, does (present) → did (past) → _(no "V3" auxiliary use)_       | Questions, negatives in simple tenses |
| **To Have** | have, has (present perfect) → had (past perfect)                  | Perfect tenses                        |

**Auxiliary + Tense mapping:**
| Auxiliary | Tense | Number |
|---|---|---|
| is / am / are | Present | is=singular, am=I, are=plural |
| was / were | Past | was=singular, were=plural |
| has | Present Perfect | Singular |
| have | Present Perfect / Future Perfect | Plural / I / You |
| had | Past Perfect | Singular & Plural both |
| do | Present Simple (Q/Neg) | Plural / I / You |
| does | Present Simple (Q/Neg) | Singular |
| did | Past Simple (Q/Neg) | Singular & Plural both |
| will / shall | Future | Both (will = general, shall = mostly I/We, formal) |

> ⚠️ **"Doing" and "Having" are verb forms, NOT auxiliary verbs.** Don't confuse V4 (doing/having as -ing forms) with the auxiliary role of "do/have."

### 7.2 Modal Auxiliary Verbs (show MOOD)

| Modal        | Past/Related Form | Primary Use                                 |
| ------------ | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Will**     | Would             | Future intention/prediction                 |
| **Would**    | —                 | Polite request, hypothetical situation      |
| **Can**      | Could             | Ability, possibility (present)              |
| **Could**    | —                 | Past ability, polite request/suggestion     |
| **Shall**    | Should            | Offers/suggestions (esp. with I/We), formal |
| **Should**   | —                 | Advice, obligation/necessity                |
| **May**      | Might             | Permission, possibility                     |
| **Might**    | —                 | Smaller/weaker possibility than "may"       |

| **Can**      | "It **can** rain tomorrow."                                         | Shows possibility                                               |

## 10. Hacks to Become a Confident Speaker

1. **Slow & relaxed movements** — Confident speakers move deliberately and calmly, not in agitated/jerky bursts, even under pressure or provocation. When someone tries to trigger/annoy you, staying visibly calm ("holding your ground") is a strong non-verbal confidence signal.
2. **Hold eye contact** — 3–5 seconds minimum. If you need to break it, do so gently (a soft blink, or briefly glancing sideways) and then return your gaze — don't look around the room randomly, and don't stare at someone's shoes/clothes instead of their face.
3. **Big hand gestures** — Confident, high-status speakers use the space around them; their hand movements are visible and wide, not tiny/closed-off gestures kept close to the body. Presenters who physically move and gesture toward what they're discussing appear more capable than those who stay frozen in place.
4. **The "Carrot and Stick" trick** (negotiation technique) — When persuading or negotiating with someone, you generally need to give:
   - **The Carrot** = the _benefit_ you're offering them
   - **The Stick** = the _consequence or expectation_ tied to it
   - **Worked example:** Instead of directly demanding a raise ("Why am I not getting a raise?"), you sit with your boss and say: _"I've been involved in multiple roles here over the past year, and I feel I've learned enough in this position. I'd like to discuss what you see me doing over the next three months to help this company grow — and if I'm able to achieve those targets, I would also expect to be considered for a raise."_ This offers value first (carrot), then states the expectation (stick) — much more effective and mature than a blunt demand.

### 11.1 Starting a Meeting

| Purpose                   | Phrase                                                                          |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

### 11.2 Introducing Yourself & Stating the Meeting's Purpose

- "I am [Name]. I will keep this meeting brief, as I know you all are very busy people."
- "I am [Name] and I arranged this meeting because I've noticed a lot of confusion around this project, and I wanted all of us to sit down together and finalize the flow of events in one go."
- "I have called this meeting in order to [purpose]..."
- "We are here today to discuss [topic]."

### 11.3 Inviting Introductions from Others

- "Let's go around the table and introduce ourselves." → then direct a specific person: _"[Name], would you like to start?"_
- "Let's introduce ourselves quickly. Please state your name, job title, and why you are here."

### 11.4 Setting the Agenda

- "There are two/five items on the agenda today."
- "Today, I would like to outline our plans for [project/product/client]..."

### 11.5 Defining Action Points

- "Before the next meeting, I want [X] completed so we can discuss the results."
- "By the next meeting, we will have [X] in progress."

### 11.6 Interjecting / Clarifying / Adding a Point

| Situation                 | Phrase                                                                                 |
| ------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

### 11.7 Asking Questions in a Meeting

- "Could you repeat that, please?"
- "Can you run that by me one more time?"
- "Are there any more comments / questions / feedback?"
- "What do you think about this proposal / idea / client / issue?"

### 11.8 Holding the Floor / Returning to a Point

- "Can we come back to that point later? Let me just finish what I was saying."
- "Can I just finish making my point?"
- "I hear what you are saying; however, our senior manager is very clear on this one."

## 15. Tone in Communication — Same Sentence, 12 Registers

> **Key insight repeated throughout the course:** The SAME message can land completely differently depending on tone, word choice, and body language. A teacher who opens class with an exaggerated "Hiiii guyyys!" instantly damages their credibility — even before teaching anything — purely due to tone.

### Example: "I don't want to talk to you right now" — 12 Ways to Say It

| Register                       | Phrase                                                                             |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Formal (to a colleague)**    | "I would prefer not to engage in conversation with you at this time."              |
| **Informal (to a friend)**     | "I am not really in the mood to chat with you right now."                          |
| **Professional (to a client)** | "I believe it would be best if we refrain from further discussion at this moment." |
| **Rude**                       | "I really don't feel like talking to you. So just back off."                       |
| **Friendly**                   | "Hey, I hope you understand, but I am not up for a chat right now."                |
| **Authoritative**              | "It is necessary that we cease communication at this point."                       |
| **Direct**                     | "I don't want to talk to you."                                                     |
| **Indirect**                   | "I guess I will take a break from conversing for a while."                         |
| **Assertive**                  | "I am choosing not to talk to you right now."                                      |
| **Passive**                    | "Talking to you is not something I'm interested in at this point."                 |
| **Casual**                     | "I'm good, thanks, but I'd rather not talk right now."                             |
| **Hesitant**                   | "I think it might be better if we don't have a conversation right now."            |

> **Takeaway:** Your choice of words AND your tone both shape the final impact — practice consciously varying both.

This is described as genuinely useful not just for pronunciation clarity but also as a **jaw/cheekbone toning exercise** that can make your voice deeper and reduce a "grating"/unpleasant vocal tone.
- **Deepen your voice** by projecting from the abdomen rather than just the throat, especially if your natural voice feels thin or piercing.

### 🇧🇩 Special Note for Bangla Speakers: S vs SH

This exact confusion was called out repeatedly in the course (originally for Hindi speakers, but it is **equally — arguably even more classically — a Bangla speech pattern**, since colloquial Bengali often uses "sh"-like sounds where standard English needs a clean "s"):

| Sound                      | Tongue Position                                                                                                              | Example              |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **S** (सरौता/স-type sound) | Tongue tip touches just behind the lower front teeth, air is pushed out in a straight, narrow stream                         | _sorry, sun, glass_  |
| **SH** (शेर/শ-type sound)  | Tongue stays in the middle of the mouth, sides of the tongue touch the upper palate/side teeth — tip does NOT touch anywhere | _shirt, shine, wish_ |

> **Practice tip from the course:** Say "shair" (शेर) and "sorota" (सरौता) repeatedly and feel the physical tongue-position difference. Many learners default to SH for every S-sound (e.g., saying "muskaan" as "mushkaan") — this is flagged directly as a common, fixable error with deliberate practice.

### V vs B Sound

| Sound | Lip Position                                         | Example      |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------ |
| **V** | Lips slightly apart, upper teeth touch the lower lip | _very, verb_ |
| **B** | Lips fully close together, then release              | _boy, big_   |

> Common error: Saying "verb" as "berb," or "vry/bhery" instead of "very" — practice keeping lips apart with teeth-on-lip contact for V-sounds.

## 18. Body Language & Non-Verbal Confidence

### Core Principles

1. **Slow, controlled movements** signal confidence; jerky, rushed movements signal nervousness — even when the content of your speech is fine.
2. **Hold eye contact** — described as essential; "eyes are the window to the soul." Constantly looking away, at someone's shoes, or anywhere except their face makes people feel you're hiding something or being disrespectful.
3. **Avoid closed/small body language** — hunched shoulders (a classic "startled/threatened" reflex response), crossed arms, or fidgeting/touching your ears/hair repeatedly all signal fear/anxiety.
4. **Enhanced/exaggerated lip movements while speaking** — deliberately over-articulating slightly conveys energy, seriousness, and importance. ("If you don't take what you're saying seriously, nobody else will either.")
5. **Match your inner energy to your body language** — the energy you feel internally directly shows up in your tone and posture; low internal energy = flat, unconvincing delivery, regardless of vocabulary.
6. **"Fake it till you make it"** — Act confident even before you feel it internally; consistent "faking" (in a healthy sense — projecting composure) builds genuine confidence over roughly 30 days of consistent practice, per the instructor's own experience.
7. **Don't sit "on both hands" or use restrictively small, closed hand gestures** when explaining ideas — big, open gestures (used appropriately, without disrespect) signal confidence and command of the room.

### Hygiene & Grooming Matter

- Clean clothes, trimmed nails, pleasant scent — all subconsciously impact how people perceive your credibility and trustworthiness, even in professional settings.
- Even with only 2 sets of clothes, you can stay clean by rotating: wear, wash, dry, iron, repeat.
- **Colour combinations recommended:**

| Combination       | Occasion                  |
| ----------------- | ------------------------- |
| Blue + White      | Classic, works for anyone |
| Beige + Navy Blue | Sharp/smart               |
| Brown + Beige     | Elegant                   |
| Brown + Golden    | Formal occasions          |
| Pastels           | Summer wear               |

## 19. Personality Development & Mindset Shifts

### The "Two Circles" Illusion (Key Teaching Concept)

- Two visually identical circles can _appear_ different sizes purely based on what surrounds them (larger surrounding shapes make the inner circle look smaller, and vice versa) — a classic perception illusion (similar to the Ebbinghaus illusion).
- **The lesson mapped onto self-worth:** Two people with the exact same talent/capability can end up feeling completely different about themselves purely because of their **surrounding environment and the comparisons they grew up with** — NOT because of any objective difference in ability.
- **Worked example:** One girl grows up as the "least accomplished" among 5 highly successful sisters and constantly feels inadequate. Another girl grows up as the only daughter among brothers, constantly told "you're the best," and grows up supremely confident — **despite potentially similar actual ability level.**
- **Conclusion:** Attraction, intelligence, and success are all **relative** — shaped heavily by your environment and the comparisons you were raised with, not by any fixed, objective truth about you.

### Reframing Self-Talk

| Instead of...                      | Say...                             |
| ---------------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| "I haven't done anything in life." | "I haven't done much **yet**."     |
| "Nobody supports me."              | "Nobody has supported me **yet**." |
| "I can't do this."                 | "I can't do this **yet**."         |

> Adding the word **"yet"** shifts your mindset from a permanent, fixed failure state to an active growth trajectory — described as a small change with major psychological impact.

### 3 Common Mistakes That Hinder Growth

1. **Fear of judgment / fear of making mistakes** — Give yourself explicit permission to make a set number of mistakes (e.g., "I will make at least 2,000 mistakes over the next 2 years") — reframe mistake-making as a _target to hit_, not something to avoid.
2. **Lack of practical implementation** — Many people (both working professionals and homemakers) attend classes, take excellent notes, but never actually practice speaking. Learning without speaking practice doesn't build fluency — you must **use** what you learn, immediately and repeatedly.
3. **Lack of observation** — Failing to notice: (a) your own internal thought process before speaking, (b) your body language/expressions while speaking, and (c) your tone. True communication requires actively thinking through a sentence — subject, tense, sentence type — _before_ speaking it, not just blurting words out.

### The Value of Pausing

- It's completely acceptable to **pause for 5–10 seconds** before answering a difficult question — this is framed as "an intentionally taken pause," not a weakness.
- A useful stalling phrase: _"Thank you for your question."_ — This buys you a moment to breathe, think, and construct a clear answer before responding.

### On Attractiveness/Confidence (addressing "I don't feel attractive")

- The course explicitly repositions "I'm not attractive enough / not confident enough" as a symptom of comparison-driven thinking, not objective truth.
- Practical intervention discussed: Physical activity/exercise (e.g., committing to a gym routine) was shared as a real example of how consistent small actions changed the instructor's _internal_ feeling of confidence within about a week — even before any visible physical change occurred. The takeaway: **taking action itself changes your internal state**, often faster than external results do.

| Concept (Hindi-style thought)                                        | English Structure/Phrase                        | Example                                                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |

## 27. The 5-Second Rule & 7-Second Rule

### 5-Second Rule

> When an idea or thought comes to your mind, **say it out within 5 seconds** — don't overthink, don't suppress it. The longer you hold a thought inside, the more your anxiety, nervousness, and self-doubt ("second-guessing yourself") builds up.
> Be respectful, but otherwise: **just say it.** Two people might laugh — that's fine, let them.

### 7-Second Rule (attributed to Thomas Corley)

> People form a first impression of you within the **first 7 seconds** of meeting you. In those 7 seconds, you can consciously control:

1. **A confident smile** — a genuine smile that involves your eyes (not a forced, closed-off expression)
2. **Good overall body language** — not slouched, not closed off, not fidgety
3. **Eye contact** — while greeting/shaking hands
4. **A solid, clear self-introduction** — e.g., _"Hi, I'm [Name]. I look after the English vertical of the channel. I hope you're doing well."_

## 28. Common Grammar & Usage Mistakes (Full Corrected List)

| ❌ Incorrect                                                                        | ✅ Correct                                                                              | Rule / Explanation                                                                                                                                                               |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The meeting **is** postponed since next week                                        | The meeting **has been postponed until** next week                                      | Use "has/have been" for this passive-present-perfect meaning; use "until," not "since," when referring to a future point                                                         |
| I have to submit the project **till** Monday                                        | I have to submit the project **by** Monday                                              | "Till" = continuously up to that point in time (implies repetition); "By" = a firm deadline                                                                                      |
| She is my **cousin sister**                                                         | She is my **cousin**                                                                    | No "cousin sister/brother" construction in standard English — gender is shown via the pronoun (he/she)                                                                           |
| I will **revert back** to you soon                                                  | I will **reply to** you soon / I will **get back to** you soon                          | Never add "back" after revert/reply/reverse — it's redundant                                                                                                                     |
| She is looking **so much** tired today                                              | She looks **very** tired today                                                          | "So much" pairs with uncountable nouns (e.g., "so much love"), NOT with adjectives — use "very" for adjectives                                                                   |
| The food is very tasty, **no**?                                                     | The food is very tasty, **isn't it**?                                                   | Use proper tag questions; don't literally translate "na/no"                                                                                                                      |
| The children are so cute, **no**?                                                   | The children are so cute, **aren't they**?                                              | Tag question must match the auxiliary verb used earlier in the sentence (are → aren't)                                                                                           |
| My house is **near to** the park                                                    | My house is **near** the park                                                           | No "to" needed after "near"                                                                                                                                                      |
| I have to go **outside** _(meaning: fully leave the location)_                      | I have to go **out**                                                                    | "Out" = fully outside the current location; "Outside" = just beyond the immediate room/space (e.g., "outside my house" = just past the front door)                               |
| Please come **fastly**                                                              | Please come **fast**                                                                    | "Fastly" is not a standard English word                                                                                                                                          |
| She speaks English very **good**                                                    | She speaks English very **well**                                                        | "Good" = adjective (describes nouns); "Well" = adverb (describes verbs/actions)                                                                                                  |
| I **read** in class 5 _(meaning "study")_                                           | I **study** in class 5                                                                  | "Read" = word-by-word reading (books/articles); "Study" = overall academic learning                                                                                              |
| My earrings got tangled **with** my hair                                            | My earrings got tangled **in** my hair                                                  | Use "tangled in" for something entangled within hair; use "tangled with" when two separate objects get entangled together (e.g., "my charger got tangled with my earphone wire") |
| Stop behaving **childlely**                                                         | Stop behaving **like a child** / Stop behaving **childishly**                           | "Childlely" is not a real word                                                                                                                                                   |
| Are you **mad or what**?                                                            | Are you **mad**?                                                                        | Don't literally translate "क्या" as "or what" — it's redundant and incorrect                                                                                                     |
| The child is studying **downstairs** _(when meant as "under" someone specifically)_ | Use "downstairs" correctly for location, "upstairs" for the opposite                    | Basic location prepositions — "upstairs"/"downstairs," no literal translation needed                                                                                             |
| I need to do **household chores**                                                   | I need to do **household chores** (correct spelling: **chores**, not "coures"/"course") | Pronounced like "chors" — commonly mis-said                                                                                                                                      |
| I am good **in** cooking                                                            | I am good **at** cooking                                                                | Always use "good AT [skill]," never "good IN [skill]"                                                                                                                            |
| Is she not good **in** dancing?                                                     | Is she not good **at** dancing?                                                         | Same "good at" rule applies in questions too                                                                                                                                     |

## 29. Quick-Reference Cheat Sheet

### Tense Formula Summary

| Tense              | Positive Formula          |
| ------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Simple Present     | S + V1(+s/es) + O         |
| Present Continuous | S + is/am/are + V-ing + O |
| Present Perfect    | S + has/have + V3 + O     |
| Simple Past        | S + V2 + O                |
| Past Continuous    | S + was/were + V-ing + O  |
| Past Perfect       | S + had + V3 + O          |
| Simple Future      | S + will/shall + V1 + O   |
| Future Continuous  | S + will be + V-ing + O   |
| Future Perfect     | S + will have + V3 + O    |

### Auxiliary Verb Cheat Sheet

| Use                  | Singular | Plural / I / You |
| -------------------- | -------- | ---------------- |
| Present (state)      | is       | am (I) / are     |
| Past (state)         | was      | were             |
| Present Perfect      | has      | have             |
| Past Perfect         | had      | had              |
| Simple Present Q/Neg | does     | do               |
| Simple Past Q/Neg    | did      | did              |

### Rule Reminders

- **Did / Did not / Does / Do** → ALWAYS followed by **V1**
- **Has / Have / Had / Has not / Have not / Had not** → ALWAYS followed by **V3**
- **All modal verbs** (will, would, can, could, shall, should, may, might, must, ought to) → ALWAYS followed by **V1**
- **Continuous tenses** → ALWAYS use **V-ing (V4)**

## 30. Final Takeaways & Study Plan

1. **Communication is not just about English** — it's about mastering tone, clarity, listening, body language, and confidence, and these principles transfer to ANY language, including Bangla.
2. **Practice > Theory.** Passive learning (just watching/reading) doesn't create fluency — active daily practice does. Speak out loud, even alone.
3. Give yourself **real timelines** (e.g., 30 days) and track small improvements rather than expecting overnight fluency.
4. **Mistakes are part of the process** — reframe them as evidence you're actively trying, not proof of failure.
5. Read regularly — start with beginner-level materials (children's storybooks are fine to start) and gradually build up to editorials, novels, and complex topics, always making sure you understand the _context/background_ first.
6. Use **root words and mnemonics** to retain vocabulary long-term (e.g., linking "Ambi-" root to "both," giving you "ambivert," "ambiguous," and "amphibian" all at once).
7. **Teach what you learn** — even to an imaginary audience, or to a younger sibling/friend — teaching cements retention far better than passive re-reading.
8. **Your inner energy directly shapes your outer communication** — confidence starts from within, and body language/tone will always reflect how you actually feel internally.
9. Suggested **daily practice routine:**
   - Pick 5 new words a day (from reading or this document) and _actively use_ them in conversation within 24 hours.
   - Practice one tense type per day using your own real-life example sentences.
   - Record yourself speaking for 2–3 minutes daily and review it.
   - Read one short article/story and try to identify sentence types, tenses, and unfamiliar vocabulary in context.
