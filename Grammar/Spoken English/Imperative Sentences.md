# Imperative Sentences — Spoken English Notes
## (Day 2 of 30-Day Spoken English Series)

---

## What Are Imperative Sentences?

Every conversation starts with one of three things:
- You **request** something from someone
- You **instruct** or **guide** someone
- You **command** or **order** someone

The sentences you use for all three are called **Imperative Sentences.**

**Used for:**
- Giving instructions (Do this, Go there, Write your name)
- Giving commands / orders (Stop, Sit down, Come here)
- Making requests (Please help me, Kindly listen)
- Giving advice (Study hard, Don't give up)
- Giving warnings (Don't touch that, Be careful)

---

## The Cross Method — Core Sentence-Building Technique

The most important rule to remember when forming imperative sentences in English:

**In English, the action (verb) always comes FIRST.**
**The object (what or who the action is about) comes AFTER.**

This is the opposite of how many other languages work, where the subject or object often comes first.

**Formula:** Verb + Object

**Examples:**

| Instruction | Verb First | Object After | Full Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Close the door | Close | the door | Close the door. |
| Bring water | Bring | a glass of water | Bring a glass of water. |
| Do your studies | Do | your studies | Do your studies. |
| Eat vegetables | Eat | your vegetables | Eat your vegetables. |
| Open the window | Open | the window | Open the window. |

**Key Rule:** In imperative sentences, there is NO subject. You do not say "You close the door." You just say "Close the door." The subject (you) is understood — it does not need to be said.

---

## 3 Main Types of Imperative Sentences

### Type 1 — Positive (Do something)
**Structure:** Verb + Object
> Close the door.
> Eat your food.
> Study for your exams.

### Type 2 — Polite Request (Please do something)
**Structure:** Please / Kindly + Verb + Object
> Please close the door.
> Kindly submit your form.

### Type 3 — Negative (Don't do something)
**Structure:** Don't + Verb + Object
> Don't close the door.
> Don't be late.
> Don't touch that.

## Making Requests — Please and Kindly

When you want to ask politely, add **Please** or **Kindly.**

**Please** — most common, works in all situations, spoken and written.
**Kindly** — slightly more formal, used in professional or written contexts.

**Structure:** Please / Kindly + Verb + Object

**With Please:**
- Please sit down.
- Please listen to me.
- Please pass the salt.
- Please tell me your name.
- Please send me the notes.
- Please wait outside.
- Please be on time.
- Please speak slowly.
- Please help me with this.
- Please keep the door closed.
- Please call me when you arrive.
- Please stay calm.
- Please hold on a moment.
- Please repeat that.
- Please come in.

**With Kindly (formal / written):**
- Kindly submit your application by Monday.
- Kindly be seated.
- Kindly refrain from using mobile phones.
- Kindly note the change in timings.
- Kindly inform us of your decision.
- Kindly follow the instructions below.
- Kindly arrive 10 minutes before the scheduled time.
- Kindly maintain silence in the library.
- Kindly carry a valid ID.
- Kindly return the books before the due date.

**Please at the end (also correct):**
- Close the door, please.
- Pass the salt, please.
- Say that again, please.
- Be quiet, please.
- Come early, please.

## Close vs. Shut — An Important Difference

Both mean to make something not open, but they are NOT always interchangeable.

**Close** — gentle, polite, standard, used in everyday conversation
- Close the door.
- Close the window.
- Close your eyes.
- Close your book.

**Shut** — more forceful, implies firmness or finality
- Shut the door! (stronger, more urgent)
- The shop is shut. (closed for business)
- Shut down the system. (technical context)

**Rule:** Use **Close** in polite conversation. Use **Shut** only when force or urgency is intended.

**Never say:** Shut up — this is considered rude.
**Instead say:** Please be quiet. / Please lower your voice. / Stay calm.

---

## Stay Calm vs. Keep Quiet — Choosing the Right Words

The same message can sound kind or rude depending on your word choice.

| Intended meaning | Rude version | Polite version |
|---|---|---|
| Be quiet | Shut up. / Keep quiet. | Please be quiet. / Stay calm. |
| Go away | Get lost. | Please give me some space. |
| Leave | Get out. | Please step outside. |
| Stop talking | Shut your mouth. | Could you please lower your voice? |
| Be calm | Calm down! | Please stay calm. / Take a deep breath. |
| Wait | Just wait. | Please hold on a moment. |
| Move faster | Hurry up! | Could you be a little quicker, please? |

Always consider **tone.** The same instruction can build or break a relationship.

## Emphasis with "Do" — Warm, Earnest Requests

Adding **Do** before the verb makes a request stronger and warmer — it shows you genuinely mean it.

**Structure:** Do + Verb + Object

- Do come. (Please do come — I really want you to)
- Do try.
- Do let me know.
- Do eat something.
- Do take care of yourself.
- Do think about it.
- Do come to the party.
- Do read this — it's really good.
- Do give it a chance.
- Do visit us sometime.
- Do call me when you're free.
- Do bring your friends along.
- Do ask if you have any questions.
- Do rest well tonight.

This form sounds natural, warm, and encouraging — very common in spoken English.

## Softening Commands — Making Them Sound Polite

Sometimes a direct command sounds too harsh. Here are ways to soften the same instruction:

| Direct Version | Softer / Polite Version |
|---|---|
| Give me that. | Could you give me that? |
| Sit down. | Please have a seat. |
| Be quiet. | Could you please keep it down? |
| Don't do that. | Please refrain from doing that. |
| Close the door. | Could you close the door, please? |
| Wait here. | Please wait here for a moment. |
| Come on time. | Please make sure you are on time. |
| Don't be late. | Please try to be punctual. |
| Hurry up. | We are a little short on time. |
| Leave me alone. | Could you give me some space, please? |
| Tell me the truth. | I would really appreciate your honesty. |
| Stop talking. | Could you please keep your voice down? |
| Do it now. | Whenever you get a chance, please take care of this. |
| Don't argue. | Let's try to find a solution together. |

## Question Tags with Imperatives

A question tag softens a command and makes it sound like a polite request.

**Structure:** Imperative sentence + will you? / won't you? / can you? / could you?

- Close the door, will you?
- Pass me the salt, could you?
- Be quiet, can you?
- Come early tomorrow, won't you?
- Help me with this, would you?
- Don't forget, will you?
- Don't be rude, will you?
- Send me the file, can you?
- Keep this safe, could you?
- Let me know, will you?

**Note:** For negative imperatives (Don't...), the tag is almost always **will you?**

## Real-Life Conversations Using Imperatives

### Conversation 1 — Morning Routine (Parent and Child)
> **Parent:** Wake up! It is already 7.
> **Child:** Five more minutes, please.
> **Parent:** No. Get up right now. Brush your teeth and wash your face.
> **Child:** Okay, okay.
> **Parent:** Don't forget your lunch box. Hurry up — don't miss the bus.
> **Child:** Don't worry.
> **Parent:** Call me when you reach school. Take care.

**Imperatives:** Wake up, Get up, Brush your teeth, Wash your face, Don't forget, Hurry up, Don't miss, Call me, Take care.

---

### Conversation 2 — Classroom
> **Teacher:** Good morning, everyone. Please sit down.
> **Students:** Good morning, ma'am.
> **Teacher:** Open your books to chapter 5. Read the first paragraph silently.
> **Student:** I don't have my book today.
> **Teacher:** Don't worry. Share with the person next to you. Please don't make noise while others are reading.
> **Teacher:** Stop reading now. Pay attention to the board. Write this down. Don't copy from each other — do your own work.

**Imperatives:** Sit down, Open, Read, Share, Don't make noise, Stop, Pay attention, Write, Don't copy, Do your own work.

---

### Conversation 3 — Friends Making Plans
> **Friend A:** Come to the market with me today.
> **Friend B:** I am tired. Don't force me.
> **Friend A:** Come on, don't be lazy. Get ready in 10 minutes.
> **Friend B:** Fine. But don't take too long at every shop.
> **Friend A:** Don't worry. Just come. Bring your wallet.
> **Friend B:** Wait for me outside.
> **Friend A:** Hurry up then!

**Imperatives:** Come, Don't force, Don't be lazy, Get ready, Don't take too long, Don't worry, Come, Bring, Wait, Hurry up.

---

### Conversation 4 — Doctor and Patient
> **Doctor:** Please lie down here. Relax and stay still.
> **Patient:** Yes, doctor.
> **Doctor:** Don't tense up. Take a deep breath. Breathe in... breathe out. Good.
> **Doctor:** Take this medicine twice a day. Don't skip any doses. Avoid oily food. Drink plenty of water. Come back after a week.
> **Doctor:** Don't worry too much. Get proper rest.

**Imperatives:** Lie down, Relax, Stay still, Don't tense up, Take a deep breath, Breathe in, Breathe out, Take this medicine, Don't skip, Avoid, Drink, Come back, Don't worry, Get proper rest.

---

### Conversation 5 — Office (Manager and Employee)
> **Manager:** Please come in and have a seat.
> **Employee:** Thank you.
> **Manager:** Kindly submit the project report by Thursday. Don't forget to include the budget summary.
> **Employee:** Should I send it by email?
> **Manager:** Please do. And don't share the details outside the team.
> **Manager:** Attend tomorrow's meeting at 10. Don't be late.

**Imperatives:** Come in, Have a seat, Kindly submit, Don't forget, Please send, Don't share, Attend, Don't be late.

## Common Mistakes and Corrections

| Wrong | Correct | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Don't to touch that. | Don't touch that. | No "to" after Don't |
| Please to sit down. | Please sit down. | No "to" after Please |
| You close the door. | Close the door. | No subject in imperatives |
| Don't be late come. | Don't come late. | Wrong word order |
| Kindly do not to enter. | Kindly do not enter. | No "to" after do not |
| Open you the window. | Open the window. | No subject needed |
| Don't to forget. | Don't forget. | No "to" after Don't |
| Please don't to go. | Please don't go. | No "to" after don't |
| Bring me the water a glass. | Bring me a glass of water. | Object order |
| Do your study. | Do your studies. / Study. | Correct form |

## Summary — 5 Things to Remember

1. **No subject needed** — Just start with the verb. "Close the door" not "You close the door."
2. **Positive imperative** — Verb + Object → *Close the door.*
3. **Polite request** — Please / Kindly + Verb + Object → *Please close the door.*
4. **Negative imperative** — Don't + Verb + Object → *Don't touch that.*
5. **Tone matters** — "Stay calm" is kinder than "Keep quiet." "Please have a seat" is better than "Sit down."
