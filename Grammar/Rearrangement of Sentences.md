## 1. Session Overview

**Topic:** Rearrangement of Sentences and Parts  
**Exam Target:** CUET English, SSC, Banking, and other Competitive Exams  
**Session Promise:** By the end of this session, Rearrangement will become your favourite and most confident topic.

> **Why this topic is easy:** Rearrangement does not test your Grammar. It does not test your Vocabulary. It tests your **common sense** — your ability to put ideas in a logical order. Use common sense, never leave it at home.

---

## 2. What Is Rearrangement?

Rearrangement questions give you a sentence (or a group of sentences) that has been **broken into jumbled parts**. Your task is to put those parts back in the correct order to form a meaningful, logical sentence or paragraph.

This is also called **Jumbled Words / Sentences** — the word *jumbled* itself means mixed up, disordered.

---

## 3. Two Types of Rearrangement Questions

### Type 1 — Rearrangement of Parts (Words)
**What it is:** Individual words or short phrases are given. You must arrange them to form **one complete, meaningful sentence**.

**How it appears in CUET:** This is the most common type in CUET. It is also the easier of the two types.

**Example format:**
> Parts given: P. *that would be a good enough reason*  Q. *by itself*  R. *to switch to*  S. *this system*
> Question: Arrange P, Q, R, S to form a meaningful sentence.

### Type 2 — Rearrangement of Sentences
**What it is:** Multiple complete sentences are given (usually 4–5). You must arrange them in the correct sequence to form a coherent **paragraph or passage**.

**Where it appears:** More common in Banking exams, SSC CGL, and higher-level competitive exams. Less frequent in CUET, but the same logic applies.

---

## 4. The Core Strategy

The single most important principle:

> **Every sentence — no matter how long or complex — has a natural starting point. Find that starting point first, and the rest of the answer falls into place.**

### What is the natural starting point?

Any sentence must begin with its **Subject**. The Subject is always a **Noun** or **Pronoun**.

| Subject Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Pronouns | I, We, You, He, She, It, They |
| Names (proper nouns) | Hamlet, Wilhelm Röntgen, Copernicus, Anshul |
| Common nouns | The boy, The goal, A school child, The administration |
| Conditional openers | If, When, Although, Since (introducing a clause) |
| Time/Place openers | In 1895, Now a days, In India (when introducing context) |

---

## 5. The Introductory Element — The Key Trick

This is the central technique for all Rearrangement questions.

> **Find the Introductory Element first.**

The **Introductory Element** is the word, phrase, or sentence that naturally opens the complete statement. Once you identify it, look at the answer options and find whichever option *begins* with that element. That is your answer — or at minimum narrows you to 1–2 options immediately.

### For Type 1 (Parts → One Sentence):
- Find the part that contains a **noun or pronoun** or a **conditional opener** (if, when).
- That part is most likely the opening of the sentence.

### For Type 2 (Multiple Sentences → Paragraph):
- Find the sentence that **introduces the topic** — the one that sets the scene, announces a subject, or presents a general statement that the rest of the sentences elaborate on.
- This **Introductory Sentence** is the first sentence of the paragraph.

## 6. What Can Start a Sentence (and What Cannot)

### Can start a sentence:
| Word Type | Examples | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Pronoun | I, He, She, They, We | Natural subject |
| Proper noun | Hamlet, The Centre, Wilhelm | Names = subjects |
| Common noun | The boy, A school child | Nouns = subjects |
| Conditional opener | If all the nations... | Introduces a condition |
| Time/place phrase | In 1895, Now a days, In India... | Provides context |
| Interrogative word | What view does... | Forms a question |

### Cannot start a sentence (eliminate immediately):
| Word/Type | Examples | Why it cannot open |
|---|---|---|
| Prepositions | *for, of, in, on, with, by, at* | Always attach to something before them |
| Conjunctions | *and, but, or* | Always connect two existing clauses |
| Helping verbs alone | *is, was, had, does* | Come after subjects |
| Adverbs describing actions | *beautifully, intelligently* | Modify verbs, not start sentences |
| Adjectives alone | *good, clever* | Need a noun before or after |
| Middle-of-sentence phrases | *by itself, for the common good* | These are clearly continuations |

> **The Elimination Principle:** If you cannot immediately identify the correct opener, start by **eliminating** the options that cannot possibly be the opening. You will always narrow down to 1–2 correct choices very quickly.

---

## 7. Step-by-Step Method

### Step 1 — Read all parts quickly
Do not attempt to build the sentence yet. Just scan through the parts.

### Step 2 — Identify the Introductory Element
Ask: which part has a noun, pronoun, or conditional opener that could logically begin this sentence or paragraph?

### Step 3 — Eliminate impossible openers
Cross off any option where the opening word is a preposition, conjunction, adjective alone, adverb alone, or a helping verb alone.

### Step 4 — Go to the answer options
Look at which option **starts with** your identified Introductory Element. This is most likely your answer.

### Step 5 — Verify with common sense
Read the full sequence formed by that option. Does it make a coherent, logical sentence or paragraph? Does the meaning connect smoothly from start to finish?

### Step 6 — Confirm
If two options both start with your element, read both complete sequences and pick the one that makes better logical sense.

## 8. Solved Practice Questions (PYQs)

---

### Question 1
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *then the world will avoid all future wars*
> B. *and agree to obey all the laws*
> C. *if all the nations work together*
> D. *for the common good of mankind*

**Elimination approach:**
- A starts with *then* → continuation word → eliminate
- B starts with *and* → conjunction → eliminate
- C starts with *if* → conditional opener → **possible opener**
- D starts with *for* → preposition → eliminate

**Answer options check:** Find the option that begins with C.
**Correct order: C → D → B → A**
*If all the nations work together for the common good of mankind and agree to obey all the laws, then the world will avoid all future wars.*

---

### Question 2
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *no part of this publication may be*
> B. *in any form without the written permission of*
> C. *of the publisher of the book*
> D. *reproduced, stored and transmitted*

**Elimination approach:**
- B starts with *in* → could work only if introducing context like "In India…" — here "in any form" is clearly a middle phrase → eliminate
- C starts with *of* → preposition → eliminate
- D starts with *reproduced* — 3rd form verb appearing without a subject or helping verb → eliminate
- A starts with *No part* → noun phrase → **possible opener**

**Correct order: A → D → B → C**
*No part of this publication may be reproduced, stored and transmitted in any form without the written permission of the publisher of the book.*

---

### Question 3
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *for vehicles carrying food items*
> B. *the Centre has made it mandatory*
> C. *to display all details on the*
> D. *windshield and the rear screen*

**Elimination approach:**
- A starts with *for* → preposition → eliminate
- C starts with *to display* → infinitive mid-phrase → eliminate
- D starts with *windshield* → clearly a continuation → eliminate
- B starts with *The Centre* → proper noun / institution → **opener**

**Correct order: B → A → C → D**
*The Centre has made it mandatory for vehicles carrying food items to display all details on the windshield and the rear screen.*

---

### Question 4
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *a consumer protection drive to educate*
> B. *against using fraudulent means*
> C. *the people about their rights and to warn traders*
> D. *the administration will soon launch*

**Elimination approach:**
- A starts with *a consumer protection drive* — noun phrase → **possible opener**, but it's incomplete without a verb
- B starts with *against* → preposition → eliminate
- C starts with *the people* — but this is a continuation after "to educate" → eliminate
- D starts with *The administration* → institution/noun → **strong opener**

**Correct order: D → A → C → B**
*The administration will soon launch a consumer protection drive to educate the people about their rights and to warn traders against using fraudulent means.*

---

### Question 5
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *that would be a good enough reason*
> B. *by itself*
> C. *to switch to*
> D. *this system*

**Elimination approach:**
- B starts with *by itself* → prepositional phrase continuation → eliminate
- C starts with *to switch to* → infinitive continuation → eliminate
- D starts with *this system* → demonstrative, needs prior reference → unlikely opener

**Verification:** A starts with *that* → relative clause but also works as a standalone opener here. Check: **A → C → D → B**: *That would be a good enough reason to switch to this system by itself.* ✓

**Correct Answer: A → C → D → B**

---

### Question 6
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> P. *proud of my new*   Q. *after them very well*   R. *family and looked*   S. *I was very*

**Elimination approach:**
- P starts with *proud* → adjective → eliminate
- Q starts with *after them* → continuation → eliminate
- R starts with *family and looked* → noun + conjunction → eliminate
- S starts with *I was very* → pronoun → **opener**

**Correct order: S → P → R → Q**
*I was very proud of my new family and looked after them very well.*

---

### Question 7
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> P. *can help you*   Q. *must know*   R. *before I*   S. *all the facts*

**Elimination approach:**
- P starts with *can help you* — helping verb before subject → eliminate
- Q starts with *must know* — modal before subject → eliminate
- R starts with *before I* — conditional/time opener → **possible opener**
- S starts with *all the facts* — noun phrase continuation → eliminate

**Correct order: R → Q → S → P**
*Before I must know all the facts, can I help you?* → Verify: **R → Q → S → P** gives: *Before I can help you, I must know all the facts.* ✓

**Correct Answer: R → P → Q → S** *(Before I can help you, I must know all the facts.)*

---

### Question 8
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *the goal of public distribution system*
> B. *is to create an infrastructure for the distribution of food*
> C. *to the poor and the needy*

**Elimination approach:**
- B starts with *is to create* → helping verb → eliminate
- C starts with *to the poor* → prepositional continuation → eliminate
- A starts with *The goal* → noun phrase → **opener**

**Correct order: A → B → C**
*The goal of public distribution system is to create an infrastructure for the distribution of food to the poor and the needy.*

---

### Question 9
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *intoxicating drinks*
> B. *are bad for health*
> C. *and should be avoided*
> D. *at all costs*

**Elimination approach:**
- B starts with *are bad* → verb without subject → eliminate
- C starts with *and should be* → conjunction → eliminate
- D starts with *at all costs* → prepositional phrase → eliminate
- A starts with *Intoxicating drinks* → noun phrase → **opener**

**Correct order: A → B → C → D**
*Intoxicating drinks are bad for health and should be avoided at all costs.*

---

### Question 10
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *in 1895*
> B. *Wilhelm Röntgen*
> C. *discovered a new type of ray that could penetrate objects including human tissue*
> D. *round the sun*

**Elimination approach:**
- D starts with *round the sun* → prepositional phrase → eliminate immediately
- A starts with *In 1895* → time context opener → **possible opener**
- B starts with *Wilhelm Röntgen* → proper noun → **possible opener**
- C starts with *discovered* → 2nd form verb without subject → eliminate

**Key decision:** A (time) + B (subject) → natural order is *In 1895, Wilhelm Röntgen discovered...*

**Correct order: A → B → C**
*In 1895, Wilhelm Röntgen discovered a new type of ray that could penetrate objects including human tissue.*

---

### Question 11
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *now a days*
> B. *the attitude of most parents*
> C. *is different*

**Analysis:** *Now a days* = time phrase → opener. *The attitude of most parents* = noun phrase → subject.

**Correct order: A → B → C**
*Now a days, the attitude of most parents is different.*

---

### Question 12
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *hut*   B. *what view*   C. *of poverty*   D. *does the author express*

**Question type:** Interrogative sentence.
- C starts with *of poverty* → preposition → eliminate
- A starts with *hut* → not a logical opener here → eliminate
- B starts with *what view* → interrogative → **opener**
- D starts with *does the author* → interrogative helper, comes after "what view"

**Correct order: B → D → C → A** *(What view of poverty does the author express?)*

---

### Question 13
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *a school child*   B. *should be taught*   C. *to think*   D. *for himself*

**Elimination approach:**
- B starts with *should be taught* → modal passive → eliminate
- C starts with *to think* → infinitive → eliminate
- D starts with *for himself* → preposition → eliminate
- A starts with *A school child* → noun → **opener**

**Correct order: A → B → C → D**
*A school child should be taught to think for himself.*

---

### Question 14
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *it was Copernicus*   B. *round the sun*   C. *that the earth revolves*   D. *who first said*

**Elimination approach:**
- B starts with *round the sun* → preposition → eliminate
- C starts with *that the earth* → relative clause continuation → eliminate
- D starts with *who first said* → relative pronoun continuation → eliminate
- A starts with *It was Copernicus* → pronoun → **opener**

**Correct order: A → D → C → B**
*It was Copernicus who first said that the earth revolves round the sun.*

---

### Question 15
**Rearrange to make a meaningful sentence:**

> A. *the boy*   B. *cut his*   C. *hand*   D. *with a knife*

**Elimination approach:**
- B starts with *cut his* → verb without subject → eliminate
- C starts with *hand* → noun but fragment → eliminate
- D starts with *with a knife* → preposition → eliminate
- A starts with *The boy* → noun → **opener**

**Correct order: A → B → C → D**
*The boy cut his hand with a knife.*

## 9. Exam Tips & Common Traps

### Tip 1 — Trust your Introductory Element
The moment you find a noun, pronoun, or conditional opener, that is most likely your first element. Do not second-guess yourself unnecessarily.

### Tip 2 — Use frequency of options
If one letter (say B) appears in two answer options, there is a higher probability the answer is one of those two. This narrows your focus immediately.

### Tip 3 — Time openers are valid but need context
Phrases like *In 1895*, *In India*, *Now a days* can open sentences — but only when they introduce a **setting or context**. A phrase like *in any form* is mid-sentence and cannot open.

### Tip 4 — Do not read every option fully first
Read just the **first word** of each part. If the first word is a preposition, conjunction, or adverb, eliminate immediately. This saves enormous time.

### Tip 5 — Verify with meaning
After identifying your probable answer, read the full sequence aloud in your head. Does it make logical sense from start to end? If yes, confirm. If not, reconsider.

### Tip 6 — Watch for grammatical consistency
When building the sequence, check if verb forms are consistent. A sentence like *The author express* (without -es) is grammatically wrong and should be eliminated.

### Common Traps
| Trap | How to avoid it |
|---|---|
| Preposition looks like a subject | A preposition always needs a noun after it — *of the publisher* cannot start a sentence |
| Conjunction opens the sentence | *And, but, or* always connect two existing parts |
| Time phrase in the middle | *In any form, at all costs, by itself* are always mid-sentence phrases |
| Interrogative question mark confusion | If there is a question mark, the opener must be an interrogative word (*what, why, how*) or an inverted helping verb |
| Two options with same opening | Verify both by reading the full sequence; only one will make complete sense |

## 10. Quick Reference Summary

### The Golden Rule
> Find the **Introductory Element** (noun/pronoun/conditional opener) → match it to the answer option → verify with meaning.

### Elimination Checklist
Before spending time reading every option fully, eliminate any part whose **first word** is:

- [ ] A preposition: *for, of, in, on, with, by, at, to* (as a standalone)
- [ ] A conjunction: *and, but, or, nor*
- [ ] A helping verb alone: *is, was, has, had, does, did, will*
- [ ] An adverb alone: *beautifully, quickly, intelligently*
- [ ] An adjective alone: *good, clever, strong*
- [ ] A clear continuation phrase: *by itself, for the common good, at all costs*

### What Is Likely the Opener?
- [ ] **Pronoun:** I, We, You, He, She, It, They
- [ ] **Proper noun (name):** Wilhelm, Copernicus, Hamlet, The Centre
- [ ] **Common noun:** The boy, A school child, The goal, Intoxicating drinks
- [ ] **Conditional opener:** If, When, Although
- [ ] **Interrogative opener:** What, Why, How, Who (for questions)
- [ ] **Time/Place context:** In 1895, Now a days, In modern India

### The Shortcut for Type 2 (Multiple Sentences)
For paragraph-level rearrangement:
1. The **first sentence** must **introduce** the topic (a general statement, announcement, or setting).
2. The **last sentence** must **conclude or result** from the preceding ideas.
3. Sentences with *this, these, such, therefore, however, then* are **middle sentences** — they always refer to something mentioned before.
