## 2. Strategy for Learning Idioms & Phrases

### The Literal Meaning Trick
The most effective approach to understanding idioms is to first derive their **literal (direct) meaning** in Hindi or English. The literal meaning often gives a strong hint about the figurative/idiomatic meaning.

**Example:**
- *Apple of my eye* → Literal: apple of the eye → Meaning: someone very dear (the most precious person)

### Key Points
- **Do NOT simply rote-learn** idioms.
- Use logical association — connect the literal image to the meaning.
- In MCQ exams, **options themselves are a hint**. Even a vague memory of the idiom can help you identify the correct option.
- Cover **all topics broadly** rather than mastering only a few — in objective tests, partial recall + options = correct answer.

---

## 3. What Are Idioms & Phrases?

Idioms are **fixed expressions whose meaning cannot be understood from the individual words alone** — just like proverbs and sayings in any language, their figurative meaning is completely different from the literal meaning of the words.

**Parallel Examples from Other Languages (for understanding the concept):**

| Expression (Translated Literally) | Actual Idiomatic Meaning |
|---|---|
| "Star of the eye" | Someone very dear; the most precious person |
| "To sleep after selling the horse" | To sleep without a care in the world |
| "To turn a grain of mustard into a mountain" | To make a mountain out of a molehill; to exaggerate |
| "Thieves are cousins to each other" | Birds of a feather flock together |

Just as every language has its own set of figurative expressions, English has its own idioms — and mastering them is essential for competitive exams.

## 4. Complete Idioms & Phrases Reference

### A

#### An Eye Wash
**Meaning:** A pretence; doing something with false motives; pulling the wool over someone's eyes  
**Memory Tip:** Literally "washing the eyes" — blinding someone to the truth with a false show  
**Example:** *All his excuses are but an eye wash — he does nothing without self-interest.*

---

#### An Iron Hand
**Meaning:** To use force or strict authority; to control with great power  
**Memory Tip:** Iron = strength and hardness → a hand of iron = ruling with absolute force  
**Example:** *Indira Gandhi put down the opposition with an iron hand.*

> 💡 **Memory Tip:** IRON = strength/power. Whenever you see "iron" in an idiom, connect it to strength/force.

---

#### At Odds
**Meaning:** To be in dispute or disagreement with someone  
**Memory Tip:** "Odd" = different/out of place → when two parties are "at odds," their views don't match  
**Example:** *The two groups of the society are at odds over the selection procedure.*

---

#### At Sixes and Sevens
**Meaning:** In a state of confusion; having different/conflicting opinions  
**Example:** *Economists are at sixes and sevens on the policy of rationing.*

> 💡 **Note:** Similar in meaning to *At Odds* — both indicate disagreement/disorder.

---

#### Apple of My Eye
**Meaning:** Someone cherished above all others; a very dear person  
**Memory Tip:** The "apple" (pupil) of the eye is the most delicate and precious part — so the idiom means the most precious person in your life  
**Example:** *I just love Jessie — she is the apple of my eye.*

---

#### All Bark and No Bite
**Meaning:** Someone who threatens but does not act; all talk and no action  
**Related Idiom:** *Barking dogs seldom bite*  
**Example:** *Don't worry about him — he's all bark and no bite.*

> 💡 **Connection:** *Actions speak louder than words* — similar concept.

---

#### An Axe to Grind
**Meaning:** To have a dispute or personal grievance with someone; a selfish motive  
**Example:** *Environmentalists have no political axe to grind — they just want to save the planet.*

---

#### At Someone's Beck and Call
**Meaning:** Always ready to serve someone; at their service  
**Example:** *Don't worry — I am at your beck and call.*

> 💡 **Similar Expression:** *I am always at your disposal* — same meaning.

---

#### Achilles' Heel
**Meaning:** A weak point or vulnerability (despite overall strength)  
**Origin:** Greek mythology — Achilles was invulnerable except for his heel  
**Example:** *Her involvement in this scandal has been her Achilles' heel.*

### B

#### Bag and Baggage
**Meaning:** With all one's belongings; completely  
**Example:** *He returned to his village bag and baggage.*

---

#### Back to Square One
**Meaning:** Having to start all over again from the beginning  
**Example:** *They lost everything and found themselves back at square one.*

---

#### Bated Breath
**Meaning:** In a state of anxiety and expectation; holding one's breath  
**Example:** *Every Indian was waiting with bated breath for the outcome of the cricket match.*

---

#### Bank On
**Meaning:** To rely on; to depend on; to count on someone  
**Example:** *You can always bank on her to help in a crisis.*

> 💡 **Similar:** *Count on* = to rely/depend on someone.

---

#### Barking Up the Wrong Tree
**Meaning:** Making a false assumption; blaming the wrong person; pursuing the wrong course  
**Example:** *If you think we don't want another word, you are barking up the wrong tree.*

---

#### Beat a Dead Horse
**Meaning:** To force an issue that has already ended; to bring up old matters unnecessarily  
**Memory Tip:** Beating a horse that is already dead is pointless — similarly, revisiting a closed matter achieves nothing  
**Example:** *He keeps trying to get it published, but I think he is beating a dead horse.*

---

#### Beating Around the Bush
**Meaning:** Avoiding the main topic; not speaking directly about the issue  
**Example:** *Stop beating around the bush and tell me what happened.*

---

#### Between the Cup and the Lip
**Meaning:** On the point of achievement; something that is just about to happen  
**Example:** *Until I got the appointment letter in hand, I was not sure of the posting — there were many slips between the cup and the lip.*

---

#### Black and White
**Meaning:** In writing; in written form  
**Memory Tip:** Black ink on white paper = a written document. That's exactly what "in black and white" means.  
**Example:** *I want it in black and white before I sign.*

---

#### Bird's Eye View
**Meaning:** A general overview; a broad perspective from above  
**Example:** *You can get a bird's eye view of the city by going to the topmost floor.*

---

#### Blow Hot and Cold
**Meaning:** To be inconsistent; to favour something at one time and oppose it at another; having no firm stand  
**Example:** *He can't be relied upon — he blows hot and cold constantly.*

---

#### Body and Soul
**Meaning:** Entirely; with complete dedication  
**Example:** *She devoted body and soul to win the medal.*

---

#### Blank Cheque
**Meaning:** Complete authority; unrestricted freedom of action; a free hand  
**Example:** *The manager was given a blank cheque to restructure the team.*

---

#### Blue Moon
**Meaning:** A rare event or occurrence  
**Example:** *Something which happens rarely is called a blue moon event.*

> 💡 **Related:** *Once in a blue moon* = very rarely.

---

#### Bite Off More Than You Can Chew
**Meaning:** To take on a task that is far too big for you  
**Example:** *I think he has bitten off more than he can chew by taking all those classes.*

---

#### By Hook or by Crook
**Meaning:** By any means possible; by fair means or foul  
**Memory Tip:** A "crook" is a dishonest person — "by hook or by crook" means using either honest (hook) or dishonest (crook) methods to get something done  
**Example:** *By any means, I won't get this licence — by hook or by crook.*

---

#### By Leaps and Bounds
**Meaning:** Very rapidly; making great progress  
**Example:** *The population is increasing by leaps and bounds.*

---

#### By Fits and Starts
**Meaning:** Irregularly; not continuously  
**Example:** *Work done by fits and starts never completes in time.*

> ⭐ **Both are Very Important for Exams** — *By Leaps and Bounds* (speedily) vs. *By Fits and Starts* (irregularly).

### C

#### Calling a Spade a Spade
**Meaning:** To speak plainly and directly; to say things as they are; straight talk  
**Example:** *He is known for calling a spade a spade — he never sugarcoats anything.*

---

#### Cast Iron Stomach
**Meaning:** Someone who can eat or drink anything without ill effects  
**Memory Tip:** Cast iron is an extremely tough, durable metal — a "cast iron stomach" means a stomach as tough and unaffected as iron  
**Example:** *It's too spicy for me, but John will love it — he has a cast iron stomach.*

> 💡 **Iron in Idioms = Strength/Toughness** (Iron hand, Iron stomach, Iron Man — all show strength)

---

#### Cast Aspersions
**Meaning:** To criticize someone's character; to make a negative remark about someone  
**Example:** *His opponents never missed an opportunity to cast aspersions on his professionalism.*

---

#### Chip Off the Old Block
**Meaning:** Someone who looks or behaves like their parents  
**Example:** *Like his brother — he's a chip off the old block.*

> 💡 **Related Phrasal Verb:** *Take after* = to resemble a parent or family member.

---

#### Cock and Bull Story
**Meaning:** A false, unbelievable story; a fabricated tale  
**Memory Tip:** A cock (rooster) and a bull have absolutely nothing in common — so a "cock and bull story" is one that makes no logical sense; it's completely made up  
**Example:** *All his excuses seem a cock and bull story.*

---

#### Cry Over Spilt Milk
**Meaning:** To complain or feel upset about a loss that cannot be undone  
**Example:** *Don't cry over spilt milk — what's done is done.*

---

#### Cross Paths
**Meaning:** To encounter or meet someone by chance  
**Example:** *I believe we will cross paths soon.*

---

#### Cry Wolf
**Meaning:** To give a false alarm; to raise unnecessary panic  
**Example:** *Don't pay attention to Peter — he is only crying wolf.*

---

#### Curiosity Killed the Cat
**Meaning:** Being too inquisitive can lead to trouble or danger  
**Example:** *When he started asking too many questions about his neighbours' whereabouts, they warned him that curiosity killed the cat.*

---

#### Cut and Dried
**Meaning:** Ready-made; already decided; fixed  
**Example:** *There is no cut and dried method of English speaking — you need constant practice.*

---

#### Curtain Lecture
**Meaning:** A reprimand given by a wife to her husband (privately, usually at bedtime)  
**Example:** *My younger brother never pays any attention to his wife's curtain lecture.*

---

#### Close Call
**Meaning:** A narrow escape from danger or disaster  
**Example:** *It was quite a close call, but my father managed to avoid hitting the animal that ran across the highway.*

---

### D

#### Dark Horse
**Meaning:** Someone previously unknown who suddenly becomes prominent  
**Memory Tip:** "Dark" = hidden from light, unknown to others → a dark horse is someone nobody knew about who suddenly rises to prominence  
**Example:** *The movie is a dark horse for the award.*

---

#### Don't Count Your Chickens Before They Hatch
**Meaning:** Don't rely on something until you are sure it will happen  
**Example:** *I am here not to count her chickens before they are hatched.*

---

#### Don't Put All Your Eggs in One Basket
**Meaning:** Don't put all your resources in one place; spread your risk  
**Example:** *His financial adviser urged him to be careful and not put all his eggs in one basket by investing all his money in stock.*

---

#### Dog's Life
**Meaning:** A miserable and unpleasant life  
**Example:** *Mark has been leading a dog's life since he was fired from the job.*

---

#### Dig One's Own Grave
**Meaning:** To do something that has negative consequences for oneself  
**Example:** *She dug her own grave when she started talking behind her boss's back.*

---

#### Drive Someone Up the Wall
**Meaning:** To irritate or annoy someone greatly  
**Example:** *My secretary pops her gum so loudly — it absolutely drives me up the wall.*

### E

#### Every Man Jack
**Meaning:** Every single person without exception  
**Example:** *I would bet every man jack of them are spies.*

---

#### Elephant in the Room
**Meaning:** A difficult, controversial, or obvious problem that no one wants to discuss  
**Example:** *He sat there for the whole morning with this elephant in the room.*

> 💡 **Memory Tip:** In idioms, "elephant" often represents a big problem or something difficult to ignore.

---

#### Every Cloud Has a Silver Lining
**Meaning:** Every difficult situation has something positive; be optimistic  
**Example:** *Even in difficult times, every cloud has a silver lining — better days will come.*

> 💡 **Cloud in idioms** = problems/difficult times. Silver lining = hope/positivity.

**Example:** *The president's racist comments are really going to fan the flames of hatred among the crowd.*

#### First and Foremost

---

#### Fiddle While Rome Burns

#### Foot the Bill

#### Free and Easy

## 6. Exam Strategy & Tips

### For CUET English (MCQ Format)
1. **You always get 4 options** — use them. Even a vague memory + logical elimination = correct answer.
2. **Cover all topics broadly** — do not skip topics hoping they won't appear.
3. **Use literal meaning as a clue** when you don't remember the idiom exactly.
4. **Previous Year Questions (PYQs)** — these are the best practice source. Most coaching material uses PYQs or questions from other competitive exams.

### On Clearing Mental Blocks
- CUET is objective — you don't need to write essays on idioms.
- A partial memory + correct option in front of you = correct answer.
- Grammar topics: Error Detection, Tenses, Modals, Voice, Prepositions, Conjunctions — all follow the same MCQ pattern.

### On Vocabulary (Vocab)
> "Vocab is not something to fear. The only genuine way to remember vocabulary is to **repeat it in different ways** — through questions, fill in the blanks, revision, and recall practice."

- See the word as a question.
- See it in context (sentence).
- Revise it after 2 hours, then next day, then 3 days later.
- The more you **forget and recall**, the stronger the memory becomes.

## 8. Revision Technique

### How to Revise This Sheet

**Day 1 (Today):**
- Go through the list **5 times** — just scan with your eyes, don't re-read deeply.
- Each scan: ~10–15 minutes.
- Total: ~1 hour spread across the day.

**Day 2:**
- Revise **3 times**.

**Day 3 onwards:**
- **2 times** per day until the exam.

### The Forget-Recall Method
> *"The more you forget and then recall, the more it stays in your memory."*

- Learn → let some time pass → try to recall without looking → check → repeat.
- This spaced repetition is the **only genuine method** for long-term retention of vocabulary and idioms.

### Practice Drill
For each idiom, test yourself:
1. **Cover the meaning** — can you recall it from the phrase alone?
2. **Cover the phrase** — can you recall the phrase from the meaning?
3. **Make your own sentence** — can you use it naturally?
