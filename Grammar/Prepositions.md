## 2. What Is a Preposition? (Core Concept)

**Etymology-based understanding:**
- **Pre** = before
- **Position** = place
- So a preposition is a word that comes **before a "position"** in a sentence — i.e., before a noun/pronoun to relate it to the rest of the sentence.

**Formal definition:**
> Prepositions are words which establish a relation between a noun/pronoun and other parts of the sentence.

**Illustrative example:**
- "**The book is the table**" — technically forms a sentence but has no real logical relationship between "book" and "table."
- Add **on**: "**The book is ON the table**" — now there is a clear relationship (position) between the book and the table.
- Similarly:
  - "I live India" → adds no relation. "I live **IN** India" → shows the relation (inside).
  - "This is you" → no relation. "This is **FOR** you" → shows relation (purpose/recipient).

**Conclusion:** A preposition is a word that creates a connecting relationship between a noun/pronoun and the rest of the sentence — showing **direction**, **place**, or **time**.

## 3. The Three Things Prepositions Show

| Category | Meaning | Example Prepositions |
|---|---|---|
| **Direction** | Which way something is moving | to, from, into, onto |
| **Time** | When something happens | at, in, on, since, for, until |
| **Place/Position** | Where something is | at, in, on, over, under, above, below |

## 4. Preposition: AT

**Core idea:** AT is used to point to a very **small/specific point** — whether of time, place, or activity. Think of it as "pointing with a finger" at one exact spot within a larger whole.

### Uses of AT:

1. **Exact point in time** (small units — hours, minutes, precise clock time)
   - *I will meet you at 3 o'clock.*
   - *At 2 PM.*

2. **Small/specific places** (a point within a larger area — house number, small locality, exact spot — NOT big regions like a country/city, which take "in")
   - *I live at Shahdara.*
   - *I live at Amar Colony.*
   - *I am at school.* (at a place/point)
   - Compare: *I am IN school* (emphasizes being physically inside the building).

3. **Activities** (hobbies, skills, things one is good/bad at)
   - *I am good at sports.*
   - *I am good at speaking/dancing/singing/cricket.*

4. **Emotional reactions / pointing gesture** (shock, anger, laughing at someone — like pointing a finger)
   - *He was shocked at the news.*
   - *He shouted at the driver.*
   - *He was laughing at me.*
   - *Don't shout at me.*
   - *He is at [some point in time/place], e.g., "I will come at [time]."*

5. **Rates/prices**
   - *At the rate of...*

**Trick to remember:** AT = pointing to ONE specific point (time, place, or target of an emotion/activity) — never used for large/broad contexts.

## 5. Preposition: IN

**Core idea:** IN means "**inside**" — used when something is enclosed, covered, or contained within something bigger.

### Uses of IN:

1. **Being physically inside something** (a container, box, room, big area)
   - *My clothes are in the box.*
   - *You are in my heart* (= inside, safe, secure).
   - *It is in the pot* (e.g., *dal* cooking inside a pot).

2. **Big places** (as opposed to AT for small/specific places)
   - *I live in India.*
   - *I live in Delhi.*
   (Compare: *I live AT Shahdara* — smaller/specific locality within Delhi.)

3. **Larger time units** (months, years, seasons — as opposed to AT for exact clock time)
   - *It happens in January.*
   - *In the morning.*

4. **Emotional/mental states** (being "inside" a feeling)
   - *I am in trouble.*
   - *I am in love.*
   - *I am in danger.*
   - *I am in a bus* (enclosed inside).

5. **Interest / involvement**
   - *I am interested IN sports/music/you.*
   - *He has a great passion IN classical music* (topic area — inside a field).
   - *Please do not interfere IN my personal affairs.*

**Trick to remember:** IN = INSIDE — bigger enclosed spaces, larger time units, or figuratively "inside" a feeling/state/interest.

---

## 6. Preposition: INTO

**Core idea:** INTO = IN + movement. Used when something **moves and enters/falls inside** something else (motion + entering).

### Uses of INTO:

1. **Physical movement into something**
   - *The cat jumped into the box.*
   - *The mouse fell into the box.*
   - *You need to divide it into 5 equal parts* (cutting so pieces go apart/into groups).

2. **Being "into" something = a hobby / strong interest** (very commonly tested meaning)
   - *Are you into cricket?*
   - *Are you into music/football/painting/literature?*
   - *Are you into iPhones?* (= Do you like/collect iPhones as a hobby/interest?)

**Trick to remember:** INTO = "IN with motion" (something enters/falls inside) OR "deeply absorbed in / a fan of" something (hobby usage — most commonly tested).

---

## 7. Preposition: ON

**Core idea:** ON shows a **surface** (a layer/lepar) — something resting or happening on top of a surface — and also marks **events, days, and dates**.

### Uses of ON:

1. **Surface contact**
   - *The book is on the table.*
   - *The paper is on my desk.*
   - *He is writing on the board.*
   - *The picture is playing on the television* (on the screen's surface).

2. **Days and dates**
   - *He will come on Wednesday.*
   - *They met me on Sunday.*
   - *I was born on 26 January.*

3. **Events/occasions**
   - *On my birthday.*
   - *On my anniversary.*
   - *On Diwali, on Christmas.*
   - *I congratulated her on her promotion.* (an event, not "for" — since "for" would only apply if you're giving something as a gift/purpose.)

4. **Devices/machines that are "active"**
   - *He is on the phone.*
   - *The show will be telecast on television.*
   - *She has been on the computer.*

5. **Starting/continuing something** (used in many phrasal verbs — e.g., carry on, go on)

**Trick to remember:** ON = surface + events/days/dates + active devices. **Never used for interior/interest** — that's IN.

---

## 8. Preposition: ONTO

**Core idea:** Like INTO but for **surfaces** — movement resulting in landing ON TOP of something (not inside).

### Uses of ONTO:

- *Move the books onto the shelf.*
- *The cat jumped onto the table.*
- *She climbed onto the roof.*

**Trick to remember:** ONTO = "ON with motion" (something moves and ends up on top of a surface) — rarer/less tested than INTO.

---

## 9. Preposition: WITH

**Core idea:** WITH shows **accompaniment (togetherness)** or **the tool/instrument** used to do something.

### Uses of WITH:

1. **Togetherness/accompaniment**
   - *I am with you.*
   - *He is with you.*
   - *I am going with my brother.*

2. **Tool/instrument (means of doing something)**
   - *The teacher beat me with a stick.*
   - *I am writing with a pen.*
   - *I am teaching you with happiness.*

**Trick to remember:** WITH = "together with" a person OR "by means of" a tool.

---

## 10. Preposition: BY

**Core idea:** BY shows **agency** (who/what did an action) — most often used for **means of transport** and the **doer of an action** (passive voice).

### Uses of BY:

1. **Transportation**
   - *I came by bus.*
   - *By train, by aeroplane, by car.*

2. **Doer of an action (passive voice)**
   - *This is done by me.*
   - *Made by [name]* (as seen on greeting cards/artwork).
   - *Most of the guests arrived for the concert by bus.*

3. **Deadlines** (a specific point by which something must be completed — contrast with UNTIL, see below)
   - *You need to complete your preparation by 10th of May* (= complete it before/by this date, cannot go beyond).
   - *The manager asked the team to complete the project by the end of the week.*

**Trick to remember:** BY = "through the agency of" (transport/doer) OR "no later than" (deadline).

---

## 11. Preposition: FROM

**Core idea:** FROM = **source/origin** — the starting point from which something emerges (like a river flowing from a source point).

### Uses of FROM:

- *The waves are coming from point X.*
- *The love is coming from my side.*
- *The question is being asked from the sample paper / from my notes.*
- *He graduated from Oxford.*
- *It differs from their last suggestion.*
- *She suffers from a heart disease.*

**Trick to remember:** FROM = the **starting point / source** of something (origin).

---

## 12. Preposition: TO

**Core idea:** TO = **direction/destination** — the point toward which something is moving (opposite of FROM).

### Uses of TO:

- *The waves are coming from X to Y.*
- *He is coming from Delhi to Agra.*
- *I go to Delhi. / I go to school.*
- *A strange thing happened to me.*
- *This is a lesson to them.*
- *Owing to* (fixed phrase — used to show a reason, e.g., *Unfortunately, we had to cancel it owing to [reason]*).
- *He took the criticism to heart* (direction — "to" the heart).

**Trick to remember:** TO = **direction** (towards a destination/target) — meaning "towards."

---

## 13. Preposition: OF

**Core idea:** OF has two main uses — showing **possession/belonging** and showing a **reason/cause**.

### Uses of OF:

1. **Possession / belonging**
   - *He is the brother of Salman Khan.*
   - *He is brother of mine.*
   - *This is made of gold.*
   - *You are a member of my family.*
   - *The world is full of people.*
   - *Milk/paneer is made of...*

2. **Reason/cause** (not written explicitly in grammar books, but a very useful practical trick)
   - *I am proud of you* (= I am proud **because of** you).
   - *He dies of hunger.*
   - *He dies of cholera.*
   - *I am scared of you.*
   - *I am envious of him* (jealous because of him).
   - *He was accused of theft.*
   - *What did you make of the lecture?* (= what did you understand/interpret from it?)

**Trick to remember:** OF = "belonging to / made of" (possession) **or** "because of" (reason) — no "in," "on," or "at" energy in the sentence.

---

## 14. Preposition: FOR

**Core idea:** FOR shows **purpose**, an item **given/meant for someone**, **indefinite duration of time**, or things done **in exchange/replacement** for something.

### Uses of FOR:

1. **Purpose / recipient**
   - *This is for you.*
   - *This pen is for you.*

2. **Indefinite duration** (contrast with SINCE for definite/specific starting points)
   - *He has been learning English for two hours.*
   - *For a long time.*

3. **In place of / in exchange for**
   - *Gold for cash.*
   - *It would be wrong to mistake his diffidence for his arrogance and coldness* (mistaking one thing FOR another = substitution).

4. **Reason for an apology**
   - *I apologize for the inconvenience caused.*

5. **Capability for a task**
   - *He is capable for doing the job well.* (Note: often "capable OF" is also common — see comparison with OF below.)

**Trick to remember:** FOR = purpose/recipient, duration ("for two hours"), or "in place of."

---

## 15. Preposition: ABOUT

**Core idea:** ABOUT means **"regarding" / "on the topic of"** something.

### Uses of ABOUT:

- *I am talking about you.*
- *He is talking about me.*
- *She cares about the environment.*
- *I am worried about the exam.*

**Trick to remember:** ABOUT = "regarding/concerning" a topic — used when the sentence is clearly about discussing/thinking of a subject (not interior, not surface, not reason).

---

## 16. Preposition: SINCE

**Core idea:** SINCE marks a **definite/specific starting point in time** (a fixed point from which something has been continuing).

### Uses of SINCE:

- *Since morning.*
- *Since my childhood.*
- *Since 1998* (a specific year).

**Trick to remember:** SINCE = **definite** starting time point (contrast with FOR = **indefinite** duration, e.g., "for 2 hours").

---

## 17. Preposition: UNTIL

**Core idea:** UNTIL shows **"till/up to" a certain time** — duration continuing up to a point, WITHOUT being a strict deadline.

### Uses of UNTIL:

- *I will work hard until I get success.*
- *I will love you until my death / until my last breath.*
- *She worked until 5 o'clock.*
- *You can sleep until morning.*
- *You can use my phone until 8 o'clock.*

**Difference between UNTIL and BY (important, exam-tested distinction):**
| UNTIL | BY |
|---|---|
| Shows duration/timeline ("till when" an action continues) | Shows a **deadline** (a point by which something must be finished, cannot go beyond) |
| *I worked until evening.* | *Complete the project by the end of the week.* |

**Trick to remember:** UNTIL = ongoing timeline ("till X point"). BY = a strict deadline.

---

## 18. Preposition: UPON

**Core idea:** UPON is a more **formal** version of ON, often used when something is **about to happen** or in formal invitations.

### Uses of UPON:

1. **Something about to happen**
   - *Diwali is upon us.*
   - *My exams are upon us.*
   - *Exams will soon be upon us.*

2. **Formal invitations (especially on stage/ceremonies)**
   - *We would like to invite [Name] to come upon the stage.*

3. **Immediately after doing something**
   - *Sangeeta got married immediately upon passing [college].*
   - *Upon declaration of the result...*

4. **More formal alternative to ON**
   - *I would like to congratulate you upon your journey* (vs. "congratulate on").
   - *My whole future depended upon your decision.*
   - *They congratulated him upon his success.*

**Trick to remember:** UPON = formal ON; used for imminent events or formal/ceremonial contexts.

---

## 19. Preposition: OVER

**Core idea:** OVER shows something **higher up (hovering, not necessarily touching)**, covering/contact, or "**more than**" a quantity.

### Uses of OVER:

1. **Hovering above (in the air, not fixed contact)**
   - *A beautiful white bird is flying over the lake.*
   - *An umbrella over my head.*

2. **Covering / contact**
   - *I will put my hands over my ears.*
   - *He puts the blanket over his body.*
   - *Cover my face over...*

3. **More than (quantity)**
   - *Over 2 million people participated.*
   - *Over 3 million people.*

4. **Practical/informal usage** (commonly used casually in place of ON to sound more polished)
   - *I would love to talk over this issue* (practical usage, not strictly grammatical).

**Trick to remember:** OVER = above without necessarily touching, OR covering something, OR "more than" a number.

---

## 20. Preposition: ABOVE

**Core idea:** ABOVE shows a **higher position/level**, generally **without contact** (unlike OVER, which can involve contact/covering).

### Uses of ABOVE:

- *His house is situated above sea level.*
- *Can you raise your hand above your head?*

**Trick to remember:** ABOVE = higher level (no contact) — mainly about elevation/height comparison.

---

## 21. Preposition: BELOW

**Core idea:** BELOW shows something is **beneath/under** something else.

### Uses of BELOW:

- *The cat is hiding below the table.*

**Trick to remember:** BELOW = opposite of ABOVE — "beneath" something.

## 22. Quick Comparison Table (Master Summary)

| Preposition | Core Meaning | Typical Use |
|---|---|---|
| **AT** | Exact point | Small places, exact time, activities, pointing (emotion) |
| **IN** | Inside | Big places, large time units (months/years), feelings/states |
| **INTO** | In + motion | Physical entry with movement; hobbies/strong interest |
| **ON** | Surface | Days/dates, events, active devices, physical surface |
| **ONTO** | On + motion | Movement landing on a surface |
| **WITH** | Together / tool | Accompaniment or instrument used |
| **BY** | Agency / deadline | Transport, doer of action (passive), deadlines |
| **FROM** | Source | Origin/starting point |
| **TO** | Direction | Destination/target |
| **OF** | Possession / reason | Belonging, made of, or "because of" |
| **FOR** | Purpose / duration | Recipient, indefinite duration, exchange |
| **ABOUT** | Topic | Regarding/concerning something |
| **SINCE** | Definite start | Specific starting point in time |
| **UNTIL** | Duration/timeline | "Till" a point (not a strict deadline) |
| **UPON** | Formal "on" | Imminent events, formal ceremonies |
| **OVER** | Hovering/covering/more than | Above without contact, covering, quantity |
| **ABOVE** | Higher level | Elevation, no contact |
| **BELOW** | Lower level | Beneath something |

## 25. Key Takeaways for the Exam

1. **Don't rote-learn** long lists of rules — instead, build a **logical/visual sense** for each preposition (AT = point, IN = inside, ON = surface, TO = direction, FROM = source, etc.).
2. When multiple options seem "technically fine" in Hindi translation, always fall back to the **English logic/trick**, not the Hindi meaning — many prepositions (AT, ON) translate to the same Hindi word ("पर") but are used very differently in English.
3. **Common confusion pairs to watch for:**
   - AT vs. IN vs. ON (point vs. inside vs. surface)
   - FOR vs. SINCE (indefinite vs. definite duration)
   - UNTIL vs. BY (ongoing duration vs. strict deadline)
   - OF vs. FOR (possession/reason vs. purpose)
   - OVER vs. ABOVE (contact/covering vs. pure elevation without contact)
4. Prepositions mainly test three things: **time**, **place**, and **direction** — always ask "is this about when, where, or which way?"
5. Some verb + preposition combinations are **fixed** (e.g., *agree with*, *look into*, *depend on*, *consist of*) and must simply be memorized as phrases.
6. Practice daily with real sentences — write your own address using AT/IN, your own daily routine using ON/AT, etc., to internalize the logic.
