## 1. Session Overview

**Topic:** Voice — Active Voice and Passive Voice  
**Exam Target:** CUET English, SSC, Banking, and other Competitive Exams  
**Session Promise:** By the end of this session, you will be able to identify and convert any Active/Passive Voice sentence at a glance.

> **Core Connection:** Voice builds directly on Tenses. Before studying this topic, make sure your Tenses concepts are clear — because the tense of a sentence **never changes** when converting between Active and Passive Voice.

---

## 2. What Is Voice?

Every English sentence can be expressed in **two different ways** — not because the meaning changes, but because the **focus** shifts.

**Example:**
> Sentence: *I teach English.*

This can also be said as:

> *English is taught by me.*

The action is the same. The verb is the same. The tense is the same. Only the **style of expression** has changed.

> **Concept:** In any English sentence, the action (verb) stays fixed. What changes is **who or what we focus on** — the doer (Subject) or the receiver of the action (Object).

This shift of focus is what gives us two types of voice:

| Voice | Focus |
|---|---|
| **Active Voice** | Focus is on the **Subject** (the doer of the action) |
| **Passive Voice** | Focus is on the **Object** (the receiver of the action) |

### Why "Active" and "Passive"?
- **Active** — the Subject is energetic, prominent, in the spotlight.
- **Passive** — the Subject is sidelined, less important; the Object takes center stage.

---

## 3. Active Voice vs Passive Voice

### Active Voice
In Active Voice, the Subject comes first and performs the action directly.

**Structure:** `Subject + Verb + Object`

| Active Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| I play cricket. | I am the one doing the playing. |
| Children are flying kites. | Children are performing the action. |
| He taught me English. | He is the active doer. |

### Passive Voice
In Passive Voice, the Object is brought to the front and the Subject is moved to the end (or dropped entirely).

**Structure:** `Object + Helping Verb + Main Verb (3rd Form) + by + Subject`

| Passive Sentence | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cricket is played by me. | Focus is on cricket, not me. |
| Kites are being flown by children. | Focus is on the kites. |
| English was taught to me by him. | Focus is on English. |

> **Key Insight:** The same event, described two ways. Tense does not change. Verb does not change. Only the sentence structure — and the point of emphasis — changes.

---

## 4. The Core Rules of Passive Voice

These three rules apply to **every** Passive Voice sentence, regardless of tense:

### Rule 1 — Object Comes First
In a Passive Voice sentence, the **Object** of the Active sentence becomes the first element (the new "Subject").

### Rule 2 — Main Verb Is Always in the 3rd Form
No matter what the tense, the **main verb in Passive Voice is always the 3rd form** (past participle).

| Verb | 1st Form | 2nd Form | 3rd Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Go | Go | Went | **Gone** |
| Teach | Teach | Taught | **Taught** |
| Play | Play | Played | **Played** |
| Write | Write | Wrote | **Written** |
| See | See | Saw | **Seen** |
| Make | Make | Made | **Made** |
| Eat | Eat | Ate | **Eaten** |
| Speak | Speak | Spoke | **Spoken** |
| Take | Take | Took | **Taken** |
| Come | Come | Came | **Come** |
| Beat | Beat | Beat | **Beaten** |

### Rule 3 — Tense Never Changes
The tense of the Active sentence and the Passive sentence is always the same. The Helping Verb changes to reflect the same tense in passive form — but the underlying time reference stays identical.

> **What actually changes:** The Helping Verb shifts to match the passive structure of the same tense.
>
> - Present → `is / am / are`
> - Past → `was / were`
> - Present Perfect → `has been / have been`
> - Past Perfect → `had been`
> - Future → `will be / shall be`
> - Continuous (any tense) → adds `being`
> - Perfect (any tense) → adds `been`
> - Modals → `Modal + be`

## 5. When Passive Voice Cannot Be Formed

### Condition 1 — No Object
Passive Voice requires an **Object** to move to the front. If a sentence has no object, no Passive Voice can be formed.

**Example:** *He came.* → No object → **Passive Voice cannot be formed.**

### Condition 2 — Intransitive Verbs
Some verbs by nature do not take an object — these are called **Intransitive Verbs**. Their Passive Voice cannot be formed.

**Example:** *He arrived on time.* → "On time" is not a true object → **Passive Voice cannot be formed.**

> **Rule to remember:** Only sentences with **Transitive Verbs** (verbs that act on an object) can be converted to Passive Voice.

### Condition 3 — Perfect Continuous Tenses
**No tense in the Perfect Continuous family has a Passive Voice.**

This means:
- Present Perfect Continuous → ❌ No Passive Voice
- Past Perfect Continuous → ❌ No Passive Voice
- Future Perfect Continuous → ❌ No Passive Voice

## 6. Passive Voice — Tense-by-Tense Structures

---

### 6.1 Present Indefinite

**Active Structure:** `Subject + V1 (+ s/es) + Object`

**Passive Structure:** `Object + is/am/are + V3 + (by + Subject)`

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| I play cricket. | Cricket is played by me. |
| She teaches English. | English is taught by her. |
| We watch movies. | Movies are watched by us. |
| He reads the book. | The book is read by him. |

**Interrogative Passive:** `Is/Am/Are + Object + V3 + (by + Subject)?`

> *Is cricket played by him?*
> *Are movies watched by us?*

**Negative Passive:** `Object + is/am/are + not + V3 + (by + Subject)`

---

### 6.2 Past Indefinite

**Active Structure:** `Subject + V2 + Object`

**Passive Structure:** `Object + was/were + V3 + (by + Subject)`

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| He celebrated his birthday. | His birthday was celebrated by him. |
| She cooked the food. | The food was cooked by her. |
| They built the wall. | The wall was built by them. |
| He taught me English. | English was taught to me by him. |

> **Note on Preposition:** Most sentences use *by*, but some verbs require a different preposition based on meaning. For example, *"known to"* not *"known by"*. Always verify that the final sentence makes logical sense.

**Interrogative Passive:** `Was/Were + Object + V3 + (by + Subject)?`

> *Was the food cooked by her?*

**Negative Passive:** `Object + was/were + not + V3`

> **Important Exam Point:** In negative sentences with *did not*, the main verb returns to its **1st form** because *did* already carries the past marker. Similarly, in Passive Voice negative constructions, *was/were + not* is used and the verb stays in the 3rd form.

---

### 6.3 Present Continuous

**Active Structure:** `Subject + is/am/are + V1+ing + Object`

**Passive Structure:** `Object + is/am/are + being + V3 + (by + Subject)`

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| They are playing football. | Football is being played by them. |
| She is teaching Garba. | Garba is being taught by her. |
| He is making a noise. | A noise is being made by him. |

> **Why "being"?**
> In Active Continuous, the `-ing` ending conveys the sense of "is happening right now." In Passive Voice, the main verb must be in the 3rd form — so `-ing` is lost. To restore the "ongoing" meaning, **being** is added. *Being* = *be* + *-ing* = "is happening."

---

### 6.4 Past Continuous

**Active Structure:** `Subject + was/were + V1+ing + Object`

**Passive Structure:** `Object + was/were + being + V3 + (by + Subject)`

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| He was arresting the criminal. | The criminal was being arrested by him. |
| She was singing a song. | A song was being sung by her. |
| They were building the wall. | The wall was being built by them. |

---

### 6.5 Present Perfect

**Active Structure:** `Subject + has/have + V3 + Object`

**Passive Structure:** `Object + has/have + been + V3 + (by + Subject)`

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| He has done the work. | The work has been done by him. |
| She has completed her homework. | Her homework has been completed by her. |
| They have won the match. | The match has been won by them. |

> **Why "been"?**
> *Been* is the 3rd form of the verb *be*. It conveys the sense of "having happened" — i.e., the work has been completed. It carries the perfect (completed) meaning that the main verb's 3rd form alone cannot fully express in passive structure.

---

### 6.6 Past Perfect

**Active Structure:** `Subject + had + V3 + Object`

**Passive Structure:** `Object + had + been + V3 + (by + Subject)`

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| She had cooked the food. | The food had been cooked by her. |
| He had written the letter. | The letter had been written by him. |

---

### 6.7 Future Indefinite

**Active Structure:** `Subject + will/shall + V1 + Object`

**Passive Structure:** `Object + will/shall + be + V3 + (by + Subject)`

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| I shall play cricket. | Cricket will be played by me. |
| She will cook the food. | The food will be cooked by her. |
| He will do the work. | The work will be done by him. |

---

### 6.8 Passive Voice with Modals

For any sentence containing a **Modal Verb** (can, could, should, would, may, might, must, ought to, etc.):

**Active Structure:** `Subject + Modal + V1 + Object`

**Passive Structure:** `Object + Modal + be + V3 + (by + Subject)`

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| You must do the homework. | The homework must be done (by you). |
| He should take medicine. | Medicine should be taken by him. |
| They can repair the car. | The car can be repaired by them. |
| She might ask some questions. | Some questions might be asked by her. |

> **Rule:** Modal stays unchanged. Add **be** after the modal, then the main verb in **3rd form**.

## 7. Pronoun Case Changes in Passive Voice

When the Subject moves to the end of a Passive sentence (after *by*), and the Object moves to the beginning, their pronoun forms must change accordingly.

### Subjective → Objective (for the new "Subject" at the end, after "by")

| Subjective Case (Active Subject) | Objective Case (After "by" in Passive) |
|---|---|
| I | me |
| We | us |
| You | you |
| He | him |
| She | her |
| They | them |
| It | it |

### Objective → Subjective (Object becomes new front "Subject")

| Objective Case (Active Object) | Subjective Case (New front position in Passive) |
|---|---|
| me | I |
| us | we |
| him | he |
| her | she |
| them | they |
| it | it |

**Example:**
> Active: *I know him.*
> Passive: *He is known to me.* (not *by me* — because "know" is a state verb, not an action verb, so *to* is used instead of *by*)

## 8. Special Cases

### 8.1 When the Subject Is Unknown or Unspecified
If the Active sentence has no clear subject (e.g., uses *somebody*, *anyone*, *nobody*), the Passive sentence is often written **without naming a subject** — or uses *they* (implied).

| Active | Passive |
|---|---|
| Somebody has opened the packet. | The packet has been opened. |
| Nobody has done the work. | The work has not been done. |
| They say he is honest. | He is said to be honest. |

> **Exam Rule:** When converting Passive to Active and no agent is given after *by*, use **they** as the implied subject. In some cases, **we** is appropriate depending on context.

### 8.2 Sentences Already in Passive Voice
Some sentences in English exist only in Passive Voice — they have no Active counterpart, or the Active form is unnatural.

> *He was arrested by police.*  
> *The work is done.*  
> *English is spoken here.*

### 8.3 Preposition Varies by Verb
Not all Passive sentences use *by*. The preposition depends on the verb and meaning:

| Verb | Preposition Used |
|---|---|
| know | *known **to** me* |
| surprise | *surprised **at*** |
| please | *pleased **with*** |
| fill | *filled **with*** |
| cover | *covered **with*** |
| interest | *interested **in*** |

Always verify that the final sentence **makes logical sense** before selecting a preposition.

### 8.4 Sentences That Look Passive But Are Not
Not every sentence with *is* + adjective is a Passive Voice sentence.

> *Sugar is sweet.* → This is **not** Passive Voice. There is no action verb here; *sweet* is an adjective, not a past participle.

True Passive Voice requires a **transitive action verb** in its 3rd form.

## 9. Exam Strategy & Key Tips

### Step 1 — Identify the Tense First
Before anything else, **identify the tense** of the sentence. This tells you which helping verb to use in the Passive.

### Step 2 — Find the Object
The Object of the Active sentence moves to the front. If there is no object, Passive Voice cannot be formed.

### Step 3 — Apply the Right Helping Verb

| Tense | Active HV | Passive HV |
|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | do/does | is/am/are |
| Past Indefinite | did | was/were |
| Present Continuous | is/am/are | is/am/are + being |
| Past Continuous | was/were | was/were + being |
| Present Perfect | has/have | has/have + been |
| Past Perfect | had | had + been |
| Future | will/shall | will/shall + be |
| Modals | modal | modal + be |

### Step 4 — Main Verb Is Always 3rd Form
This never changes. Every Passive Voice sentence uses the 3rd form of the main verb.

### Step 5 — Check the Pronoun Case
When pronouns shift position, their form must change (I→me, he→him, they→them, etc.).

### Step 6 — Verify Logical Sense
Choose the correct preposition (*by*, *to*, *with*, *at*, etc.) based on meaning, not just habit.

### Exam Shortcuts
- If *being* is present → it is a **Continuous** tense Passive.
- If *been* is present → it is a **Perfect** tense Passive.
- If *will/shall + be* → it is **Future** Passive.
- If *Modal + be* → it is a **Modal** Passive.
- If no agent (*by* someone) → the subject is likely **they** or **we** (implied).
- **Perfect Continuous tenses have NO Passive Voice** — eliminate any option suggesting them.

## 10. Solved Practice Questions (PYQs)

---

**Q1.** *I know him.* → Change to Passive Voice.

Options: (A) He is known to me (B) He is known by me (C) He is known with me (D) He is known near me

**Answer: A — He is known to me.**

*Explanation:* "Know" is a state verb, not an action verb. With state verbs, we use *to* instead of *by*. The tense is Present Indefinite → *is known*.

---

**Q2.** *Careless driving causes many accidents.* → Change to Passive.

Options: (A) Many accidents causes careless driving (B) Many accidents are caused by careless driving (C) Many accidents are causing by careless driving (D) Many accidents have caused careless driving

**Answer: B — Many accidents are caused by careless driving.**

*Explanation:* Present Indefinite tense → *are caused*. Main verb in 3rd form (*caused*). "Accidents" is plural → *are*.

---

**Q3.** *All his calculations have been proved wrong.* → Change to Active Voice.

Options: (A) All his calculations proved wrong (B) They have proved all his calculations wrong (C) He has proved all his calculations wrong (D) It has proved wrong all his calculations

**Answer: B — They have proved all his calculations wrong.**

*Explanation:* Present Perfect Passive (*have been proved*) → Active uses *they* as implied subject (no agent given). *They have proved* + Object.

---

**Q4.** *Why was such a letter written by your brother?* → Change to Active Voice.

Options: (A) Why was your brother writing such a letter? (B) Why did your brother write such a letter? (C) Why your brother wrote such a letter? (D) Why has your brother written such a letter?

**Answer: B — Why did your brother write such a letter?**

*Explanation:* Past Indefinite Passive (*was written*) → Active uses *did* + 1st form. "Your brother" moves to Subject position.

---

**Q5.** *The wall is being built by the mason.* → Change to Active Voice.

Options: (A) The mason has built the wall (B) The mason was building the wall (C) The mason is building the wall (D) The mason had built the wall

**Answer: C — The mason is building the wall.**

*Explanation:* *is being* → Present Continuous Passive. Active: *is building* (Present Continuous Active). Tense does not change.

---

**Q6.** *Somebody cleans the room every day.* → Passive Voice.

Options: (A) The room is clean every day (B) The room is being cleaned every day (C) The room has been cleaned every day (D) The room is cleaned every day

**Answer: D — The room is cleaned every day.**

*Explanation:* Present Indefinite (*cleans*) → *is cleaned*. "Every day" hints at routine = Present Indefinite. No *being* (not Continuous), no *been* (not Perfect).

---

**Q7.** *The prime minister was giving the speech.* → Passive Voice.

Options: (A) The speech was given by the prime minister (B) The speech was being given by the prime minister (C) The speech has been given by the prime minister (D) The speech is being given by the prime minister

**Answer: B — The speech was being given by the prime minister.**

*Explanation:* Past Continuous (*was giving*) → *was being given*. Continuous tense → *being* must appear.

---

**Q8.** *My father will write a letter.* → Passive Voice.

Options: (A) A letter was written by my father (B) A letter is written by my father (C) A letter has been written by my father (D) A letter will be written by my father

**Answer: D — A letter will be written by my father.**

*Explanation:* Future Indefinite (*will write*) → *will be written*. Tense stays Future.

---

**Q9.** *The packet has not been opened by anyone.* → Active Voice.

Options: (A) Anyone has not opened the packet (B) No one has opened the packet (C) No one has not opened the packet (D) No one has been opening the packet

**Answer: B — No one has opened the packet.**

*Explanation:* Present Perfect Passive (*has been opened*) → Active: *has opened*. In negative sentences, *no one* is preferred over *anyone* (which is used in questions and conditions).

---

**Q10.** *The soldiers properly obeyed his command.* → Passive Voice.

Options: (A) His command is properly obeyed by the soldiers (B) His command was properly obeyed by the soldiers (C) His command had properly been obeyed (D) His command is being obeyed by the soldiers

**Answer: B — His command was properly obeyed by the soldiers.**

*Explanation:* Past Indefinite (*obeyed* = 2nd form) → *was obeyed*. "Command" is singular → *was*.

## 11. Quick Reference Chart

### Active to Passive — Helping Verb Conversion

| Tense | Active Helping Verb | Passive Helping Verb | Main Verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | do / does | is / am / are | **3rd form** |
| Past Indefinite | did | was / were | **3rd form** |
| Present Continuous | is / am / are | is/am/are + **being** | **3rd form** |
| Past Continuous | was / were | was/were + **being** | **3rd form** |
| Present Perfect | has / have | has/have + **been** | **3rd form** |
| Past Perfect | had | had + **been** | **3rd form** |
| Future Indefinite | will / shall | will/shall + **be** | **3rd form** |
| Modals (can/should/etc.) | Modal | Modal + **be** | **3rd form** |
| Perfect Continuous (any) | — | ❌ **No Passive Voice** | — |

### When to Use "being" vs "been"

| Word | Used In | Signals |
|---|---|---|
| **being** | Any Continuous Passive | Action is *in progress* |
| **been** | Any Perfect Passive | Action is *completed* |
| **be** | Future / Modal Passive | Action *will happen* |

### Common Preposition Exceptions

| Verb | Use This, Not "by" |
|---|---|
| know | **to** (*known to me*) |
| surprise / astonish | **at** (*surprised at*) |
| please | **with** (*pleased with*) |
| fill | **with** (*filled with*) |
| cover | **with** (*covered with*) |
| interest | **in** (*interested in*) |
| marry | **to** (*married to*) |

## Summary in One Paragraph

Voice is the way we **choose to frame** a sentence — focusing either on the doer (Active) or the receiver of the action (Passive). The core rules are simple: in Passive Voice, the **Object comes first**, the **main verb is always in the 3rd form**, and the **tense never changes** — only the helping verb shifts to its passive equivalent. Continuous tenses add *being*; Perfect tenses add *been*; Future and Modal tenses add *be*. Perfect Continuous tenses have no Passive Voice. When no agent is given, the implied subject is *they* (or *we* depending on context). Once these patterns are clear, any exam question on Voice can be solved by first identifying the tense, then matching the structure.
