## 2. What Are Determiners?

**Definition:** Determiners are words placed **before a noun** that give extra information about it. They are also called *clarifying words*.

A determiner tells you three things about a noun:
1. **How many / how much** — quantity (a few, many, much)
2. **Whose** — ownership (my, his, their)
3. **Which one** — specific or general (this, that, the, a)

**Why the name?** *Determine* means "to find out". The word *determiner* finds out the quantity or detail of the noun.

**Key example (from class):**

| Sentence | Correct? | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| I have friends. | ✅ Grammatically correct | No detail about quantity |
| I have **a few** friends. | ✅ Correct | "A few" tells us the quantity — it is a determiner |

**Quantifiers** are the determiners that focus on **quantity** (how many / how much).

## 3. Countable vs Uncountable Nouns

| Type | Meaning | Examples | বাংলা |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Countable** | Can be counted one by one (1, 2, 3…) | friends, pens, books, flowers, questions | গণনাযোগ্য |
| **Uncountable** | Cannot be counted directly | water, milk, love, sugar, salt, help, time, money, rice, information | অগণনীয় |

**Trick:** You can count *bottles of water*, *packets of sugar*, *sacks of rice* — but not *water*, *sugar* or *rice* themselves. Containers are countable; the substance is not.

**Example sentences**
- Countable: *How many friends do you have?*
- Uncountable: *There is not much water in the tank.*

## 4. Quantifiers — Small Quantity (Few, A few, Little, A little)

These four are two pairs:

| Determiner | Used with | Meaning | বাংলা | Tone | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A few** | Countable | Some, a small number (roughly 0–5) | কয়েকটি | Positive | I have a few friends in Delhi. |
| **Few** | Countable | Hardly any — not enough | খুব অল্প / প্রায় নেই | Negative | Few people came to see you when you were ill. |
| **A little** | Uncountable | Some, a small amount | সামান্য | Positive | I need a little help from you. |
| **Little** | Uncountable | Hardly any — not enough | খুবই কম | Negative | They had little money for the party. |

**Rule of thumb**
- With **"a"** → positive (there is something).
- **Without "a"** → negative (almost nothing).
- **Few / a few → countable. Little / a little → uncountable.**

**Extra examples (from class)**
- *A few coconuts fell from the tree.* (countable)
- *A little knowledge is a dangerous thing.* (uncountable)
- *Many were invited, but few came.* (negative — most did not come)
- *I have a few chances / a few hours.* (positive)
- *I have little time for TV.* (very little time)

## 5. Some and Any

| Determiner | Used with | Meaning | বাংলা | Usual place | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Some** | Countable **and** uncountable | An unspecified quantity, more than "a few/a little" | কিছু | Positive sentences, offers/requests | I need some help. |
| **Any** | Countable, uncountable, singular | Any/no specific one | কোনো | Negative and interrogative sentences | Do you have any friends in London? |

**More examples**
- *I had some rice for dinner.* (uncountable)
- *We will read some books.* (countable)
- *Would you like some coffee and a few cookies?* (offer → "some")
- *She does not want any help.* (negative → "any")
- *I don't waste any food.* (negative → "any")

**Some vs few/little (class explanation):** *Few/little* = a small quantity. *Some* = unspecified but usually more than few/little. Both may be grammatically right; the speaker's intended quantity decides.
- *I have some time.* → a decent amount of time.
- *I have little time.* → hardly any time.

**Asking for something:** *Can I borrow some sugar?* — When borrowing, the exact amount is not specified, so **some** is used (not "a little", not "much").

## 6. Much and Many

| Determiner | Used with | Meaning | বাংলা | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Much** | Uncountable | A large quantity | অনেক (পরিমাণ) | There is not much light in this room. |
| **Many** | Plural countable | A large number | অনেক (সংখ্যা) | Many people came to see him. |

**More examples**
- *Thank you very much.* / *I love you so much.* (uncountable feeling)
- *How much time will you take?*
- *I don't know much about it.*
- *Congratulations!* → *Many congratulations* (never "much")
- *I have many friends / many memories / many questions.*
- *He has many cars.*

**Trap:** *Much* is not used with countable nouns. So "much questions" ❌ → "many questions" ✅.

## 7. Large Quantity Phrases

| Determiner | Used with | Meaning | বাংলা | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Several** | Plural countable | More than two, fewer than *many* | কয়েকটি / একাধিক | He gave me several gifts. |
| **A lot of** | Countable + uncountable | A large amount | প্রচুর | That's a lot of money. |
| **A large amount of** | Uncountable | A great quantity | বিপুল পরিমাণ | The project needs a large amount of funding. |
| **A great deal of** | Uncountable | A great quantity | প্রচুর | She spent a great deal of time on it. |
| **Plenty of** | Countable + uncountable | More than enough | পর্যাপ্ত / প্রচুর | We have plenty of time. |

**Example:** *I have a lot of things to do.*

## 8. More and Most

Quantity ladder taught in class (small → large):

`little / few → some → much / many → more → most`

| Determiner | Meaning | বাংলা | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **More** | A greater quantity (comparison) | আরও / বেশি | I need more time. |
| **Most** | The greatest quantity / majority | অধিকাংশ | Most of the candidates pass this exam. |

## 9. Every, Each, Both, Either, Neither, None, All, Enough

| Determiner | Meaning | বাংলা | Noun that follows | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Every** | The whole group, all together | প্রতিটি (সমষ্টি) | Singular noun | Every student must submit the form. |
| **Each** | One by one, individually (usually a small group) | প্রত্যেকে (আলাদা করে) | Singular noun | Each of the two sisters is married. |
| **Both** | The two of them together | উভয় | Plural | Both brothers are engineers. |
| **Either** | One of two | দুটির যেকোনো একটি | Singular | You can take either road. |
| **Neither** | Not one, not the other (of two) | দুটির কোনোটিই নয় | Singular verb | Neither of the deadlines is met. |
| **None** | Not one (of more than two) | কোনোটিই নয় | Plural/singular | None of the students was absent. |
| **All** | Everyone / everything | সবাই / সব | Plural / uncountable | We should inform all of them. |
| **Enough** | Sufficient | যথেষ্ট | Countable + uncountable | They have spent enough for their son's wedding. |

### Each vs Every (class explanation)
- **Every** → you see the group as a **whole** ("every student in this class").
- **Each** → you look at the **individuals** one by one, usually in a **small** group ("each of the two sisters").
- After *every*, a **singular noun** follows: every student, every girl, every boy, everyone.
- **Each of you / each of them** is the common exam pattern.

### Either / Neither vs None
- **Either / Neither** → **only two** options (either/or, neither/nor).
- **None** → **more than two** options.
- *Neither* takes a **singular verb**: *Neither of the deadlines **is** met.*
- **Both** always needs **plural**: "Both of the two sisters is married" ❌ → "Each of the two sisters is married" ✅ (because the verb "is" is singular).

## 10. Articles: A, An, The

Articles are the most common determiners.

### 10.1 "A" — any one of many

| | |
|---|---|
| **Meaning** | Any one, random, non-specific (one from the whole class of things) |
| **Used before** | Singular countable nouns with a **consonant sound** |
| **Trick** | *a = any one* |
| **Example** | I have a car. (any car — brand/model unspecified) |
| **Example** | She is a teacher. (any one of all the teachers in the world) |
| **Example** | He is a boy. / I have a pen. |

### 10.2 "An" — same meaning as "a", used before a vowel *sound*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Rule** | Use **an** when the noun **starts with a vowel sound** (a, e, i, o, u sound) — decided by **sound, not spelling** |
| **Example** | She ate an apple. |
| **Example** | He is an MBBS doctor. (M is pronounced "em" → vowel sound) |
| **Example** | I saw an elephant. |
| **Silent h** | an **h**our, an **h**onour, an **h**eir/heirloom — the "h" is silent, so the word begins with a vowel sound |

**Check:** *It is an heirloom of the family.* ✅ (silent h → "an") | *He will be there in an hour.* ✅

### 10.3 "The" — the specific one

| | |
|---|---|
| **Meaning** | A particular, known, or already-mentioned thing |
| **Trick** | *the = that specific one* (the English of "वो") |

**Example of how it flows:**
> I have **a** student. **The** student is very intelligent.

First mention → *a* (any student). Second mention → *the* (that particular student we just talked about).
> She is **a** teacher. **The** teacher is very strict.

### 10.4 When to use "the"

| Use "the" with… | Example |
|---|---|
| Something already mentioned | I bought a car. The car is red. |
| Unique things | **The** sun, **the** moon, **the** earth, **the** sky |
| Superlatives | He is **the** best student. |
| Ordinal numbers | **The** first chapter |
| Decades | In **the** 1990s |
| Rivers | **The** Ganga, **the** Yamuna |
| Famous buildings/monuments | **The** Taj Mahal, **the** Eiffel Tower |
| Republics, kingdoms, groups of countries | **The** USA, **the** UAE, **the** United Kingdom |
| Newspapers | **The** Hindu, **The** Times of India |
| Something known to both speaker and listener | **The** man that we saw turned out to be a thief. |

> ⚠️ *Note:* the teacher also mentioned continents, but continents (Asia, Africa, Europe) normally take **no** "the". Ranges/groups such as **the Himalayas, the Alps** and oceans (**the** Pacific) do take "the".

### 10.5 When NOT to use "the"

| No "the" with… | Example |
|---|---|
| Names of cities | Delhi, Mumbai (not "the Delhi") |
| Names of (single) countries | India, China (not "the India") |
| Names of languages | I learn English. |
| School subjects | He teaches us biology. |
| Names of persons | Sachin Tendulkar, Virat Kohli |
| General things spoken of in general | I like red colour. |

**"Specific" means:** a thing/person already discussed or known to both speaker and listener. It does *not* mean famous or special — so do not put "the" before Sachin Tendulkar or Shah Rukh Khan.

## 11. Solved Practice Questions

### Set A — Quantifiers

| # | Question | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The teacher asked him ___ questions. (much / a few / any / every) | **a few** | "Questions" is plural countable → *a few*. *Much* is for uncountables; *every* needs a singular noun; *any* is for negatives/questions. |
| 2 | ___ of the two sisters is married. (Both / Any / Each / Every) | **Each** | Singular verb "is" → *both* is out. Two individuals → *each*. |
| 3 | ___ of you should attend the conference. (Both / Each / Every / Many) | **Each** | "Each of you" pattern; individuals addressed one by one. |
| 4 | We should inform ___ of them. (many / every / few / all) | **all** | We should tell every single one of them — *all* fits. |
| 5 | They have spent ___ for their son's wedding. (few / many / enough / all / little) | **enough** | Money is uncountable; *many/few* are for countables; "all" makes no sense; *enough* = sufficient. |
| 6 | ___ of the candidates pass this exam. (Enough / Either / Most / Fewer) | **Most** | The majority pass. |
| 7 | ___ of the deadlines is met. (Neither / None / Much) | **Neither** | Verb "is" is singular; two options → *neither*. |
| 8 | Can I borrow ___ sugar? (a few / a little / some / much) | **some** | Sugar is uncountable; the amount is unspecified when asking → *some*. (*Much* is a large quantity — not natural when borrowing.) |
| 9 | I need ___ help. | **a little / some** | Help is uncountable; "a few" ❌ |
| 10 | There are ___ good teachers on YouTube. | **some / a few** | Teachers are countable |

### Set B — Articles

| # | Sentence | Answer | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 11 | He would make a better statesman than ___ university professor. | **a** | "University" starts with a "yoo" (consonant) sound → *a*, not *an* |
| 12 | ___ man that we saw turned out to be a thief. | **The** | A specific man we saw |
| 13 | He told me that ___ blue was her favourite colour. | **None of the above** | Colour names in general take no article |
| 14 | ___ sun is about to set. | **The** | Unique object |
| 15 | It is ___ heirloom of the family. | **an** | Silent "h" → vowel sound |
| 16 | He will be there in ___ hour. | **an** | Silent "h" → vowel sound |
| 17 | He teaches us ___ biology. | **None of the above** | Subject names take no article |
| 18 | ___ of the people who came have left. | **Most** | The majority had come and left |
| 19 | Shatabdi Express arrives at… | *practice with "the"* | Trains/named services — check context |
| 20 | I learn ___ English. | **(no article)** | Languages take no "the" |

## 12. Quick Revision Sheet

**Determiner = word before a noun that tells quantity / ownership / which one.**

| If the noun is… | Use |
|---|---|
| Countable, small number (positive) | a few |
| Countable, almost none (negative) | few |
| Uncountable, small amount (positive) | a little |
| Uncountable, almost none (negative) | little |
| Countable or uncountable, unspecified | some (positive) / any (negative & questions) |
| Uncountable, large | much |
| Plural countable, large | many |
| Countable or uncountable, large | a lot of, plenty of |
| Majority | most |
| Whole group (singular noun) | every |
| Individuals in a small group | each |
| Two together | both |
| One of two | either |
| Neither of two | neither |
| None of more than two | none |

**Articles in one line:**
- **a / an** = any one (use *an* before a vowel **sound**)
- **the** = that particular one, already known/mentioned
- **No article** with cities, countries, languages, subjects, person names

**Elimination habit for MCQs:** first check (1) countable or uncountable? (2) singular or plural verb? (3) positive, negative or question? — this usually removes 2–3 options immediately.
