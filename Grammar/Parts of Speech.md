## 1. Parts of Speech — Overview

### What is a Sentence?
A sentence is a **group of words** that carries a **meaningful sense**. Every word in an English sentence belongs to one of eight categories called **Parts of Speech**.

> "Parts of Speech" means the different types of words we use when we speak or write.

### Why Do Parts of Speech Exist?
English has millions of words. To understand **where each word goes** in a sentence, we place all words into 8 types. Without the correct positioning of words, sentences become meaningless.

**Example of wrong word order:** "Eats boy the rice" → No sense.
**Correct order:** "The boy eats rice." → Makes sense.

This is why we need Parts of Speech — to know which word goes where.

### The 8 Parts of Speech

| # | Part of Speech | Bengali Meaning | Core Function |
|---|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| 1 | **Noun** | বিশেষ্য (Noun) | Name of any person, place, or thing |
| 2 | **Pronoun** | সর্বনাম (Pronoun) | Replaces a noun |
| 3 | **Verb** | ক্রিয়া (Kriya) | Shows action or state of being |
| 4 | **Adjective** | বিশেষণ (Adjective) | Describes a noun or pronoun |
| 5 | **Adverb** | ক্রিয়া-বিশেষণ (Adverb) | Describes a verb |
| 6 | **Preposition** | অনুসর্গ (Preposition) | Shows relationship between words |
| 7 | **Conjunction** | সংযোজক (Conjunction) | Connects clauses or sentences |
| 8 | **Interjection** | বিস্ময়সূচক (Interjection) | Expresses sudden emotion |

### Role of Each Part in a Sentence

- **Noun / Pronoun** → Acts as Subject or Object; tells us *who* or *what*
- **Verb** → Tells us *what action* is happening and *in which tense*
- **Adjective** → Describes the quality of a Noun or Pronoun
- **Adverb** → Describes *how, when, where, how often* an action happens
- **Preposition / Conjunction** → Provides extra information; connects parts of a sentence
- **Interjection** → Expresses emotion; stands alone grammatically

> **Core Rule:** Every sentence needs at minimum: **Subject (Noun/Pronoun) + Verb + Object**. Other parts add detail but are not always required.

## 2. Nouns

### Definition
A noun is the **name of any person, place, thing, idea, or feeling**.

**Examples:** Dhaka, Bangladesh, Cricket, Book, Chair, Table, Love, Happiness, Fear

### Types of Nouns

| Type | Definition | Examples |
|------|-----------|---------|
| **Proper Noun** | Specific name of a particular person, place, or thing | Dhaka, Shakib Al Hasan, Bangladesh, Islam |
| **Common Noun** | General name shared by a whole category | Boy, City, Book, Car, River |
| **Collective Noun** | Name given to a group of things or people | A class of students, A flock of birds, A team of players |
| **Abstract Noun** | Name of something that cannot be seen, touched, or tasted — feelings and ideas | Love, Happiness, Fear, Knowledge, Freedom |
| **Material Noun** | Name of a material or substance | Gold, Iron, Glass, Wood, Cotton |
| **Countable Noun** | Can be counted individually | Apple (1 apple, 2 apples), Chair, Book |
| **Uncountable Noun** | Cannot be counted individually | Rice, Water, Sugar, Furniture, Money, Milk |

> **Important:** Proper nouns always start with a **capital letter**, not just at the beginning of a sentence.
> Example: **D**haka, **S**hakib, **B**angladesh — always capitalised.

---

## 2. Nouns — Extra Notes

### Countable vs Uncountable — Why It Matters

Uncountable nouns never take "a/an" and never become plural.

**Wrong:** I need a rice. / I need rices. ❌
**Correct:** I need some rice. / I need rice. ✅

**Wrong:** She gave me an information. ❌
**Correct:** She gave me some information. / She gave me a piece of information. ✅

## 3. Pronouns & Cases

### Definition
A pronoun is a word used **in place of a noun** to avoid repeating the noun again and again.

**Without pronouns (unnatural):**
"Rahim is my friend. Rahim is very intelligent. Rahim has two siblings. Rahim lives in Dhaka."

**With pronouns (natural):**
"Rahim is my friend. **He** is very intelligent. **He** has two siblings. **He** lives in Dhaka."

Here, **"He"** is a pronoun replacing the noun "Rahim."

### Personal Pronouns

| Pronoun | Meaning in English |
|---------|--------------------|
| I | Referring to myself |
| We | Referring to a group including myself |
| You | Referring to the person I am talking to |
| They | Referring to other people/things |
| He | Referring to a male person |
| She | Referring to a female person |
| It | Referring to a thing or animal |

> **Key Point:** Whether you say "I", "me", or "my" in English — they all refer to the same person (yourself). The **case** tells you which form to use depending on the role in the sentence.

---

### The Three Cases of Pronouns

#### Case 1 — Nominative (Subjective) Case
Used when the pronoun is the **subject** of the sentence (the one doing the action).
**No change** in the pronoun form.

| Nominative Form |
|----------------|
| I, We, You, They, He, She, It |

**Example:**
- "I like cooking." (I = subject — doing the action of liking)
- "She reads books." (She = subject)

> **Common Mistake:** Many students write "Me like cooking." — This is WRONG. When it is the subject, always use **I**, not **Me**.

#### Case 2 — Objective Case
Used when the pronoun is the **object** of the sentence (the one receiving the action).

| Nominative → Objective |
|------------------------|
| I → **Me** |
| We → **Us** |
| You → **You** (no change) |
| They → **Them** |
| He → **Him** |
| She → **Her** |
| It → **It** (no change) |

**Examples:**
- "He gave **me** money." (me = object — receiving the money)
- "I saw **him** yesterday." (him = object — being seen)
- "She called **them**." (them = object — being called)

#### Case 3 — Possessive Case
Used when the pronoun shows **ownership** — something belongs to someone.

| Nominative → Possessive |
|-------------------------|
| I → **My / Mine** |
| We → **Our / Ours** |
| You → **Your / Yours** |
| They → **Their / Theirs** |
| He → **His** |
| She → **Her / Hers** |
| It → **Its** |

**Examples:**
- "This is **my** book." (my = ownership — the book belongs to me)
- "That car is **his**." (his = ownership)
- "**Their** house is big." (their = ownership)

---

### Complete Pronoun Reference Table

| Base | Nominative (Subject) | Objective (Object) | Possessive (Ownership) |
|------|---------------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| I | **I** | **Me** | **My / Mine** |
| We | **We** | **Us** | **Our / Ours** |
| You | **You** | **You** | **Your / Yours** |
| They | **They** | **Them** | **Their / Theirs** |
| He | **He** | **Him** | **His** |
| She | **She** | **Her** | **Her / Hers** |
| It | **It** | **It** | **Its** |

> **This table is very important** — it directly applies to Passive Voice, Direct-Indirect Speech, and Subject-Verb Agreement questions in exams.

---

### Types of Pronouns

| Type | Examples | Usage |
|------|---------|-------|
| **Personal** | I, We, You, They, He, She, It | Refers to persons or things |
| **Interrogative** | Who, Whom, Which, What, Whoever | Asks questions |
| **Demonstrative** | This, That, These, Those, Such | Points to something specific |
| **Reflexive** | Myself, Himself, Herself, Yourself, Themselves, Ourselves | Refers back to the subject |
| **Relative** | Who, Whom, Which, That, Whoever | Connects clauses |
| **Indefinite** | All, Another, Any, Anyone, Each, Everybody, Few, Many, Nobody, None, Somebody | Refers to non-specific persons/things |

## 4. Verbs

### Definition
Words that show **action** (doing something) or **being** (existing/state) are called verbs.

**Action verbs:** Speak, Go, Learn, Read, Eat, Cook, Run, Write, Think
**Being verbs:** Is, Am, Are, Was, Were — these show a state or existence

**Example:** "We are happy." → "Are" shows our *state of being* → It is a verb.

### Types of Verbs

#### Auxiliary (Helping) Verbs
These support the main verb and cannot carry full meaning alone.

- **Primary auxiliaries:** Do, Does, Did, Is, Am, Are, Was, Were, Has, Have, Had
- **Modal auxiliaries:** Can, May, Should, Would, Might, Ought To, Used To, Must, Will, Shall

#### Main (Action) Verbs
These carry the core meaning of the sentence.
**Examples:** Speak, Go, Learn, Read, Eat, Run, Think, Write, Play

#### Transitive Verbs
These require an **object** to complete their meaning.

**Example:** "Rafiq loves..." → Loves what? Incomplete!
"Rafiq loves cricket." → Now it is complete. (Object = cricket)

#### Intransitive Verbs
These are complete in themselves — **no object needed**.

**Examples:**
- "They jumped." ✅ (complete — no object needed)
- "The bird flew." ✅ (complete)

## 5. Adjectives & Degrees of Comparison

### Definition
Adjectives are words that describe the **quality, quantity, or characteristic** of a noun or pronoun. They tell us what kind, how many, or which one.

**Examples:** Black, Beautiful, Tall, Weak, Strong, Clever, Wise, Unique, Little, Bright

**Example sentences:**
- "He is a **clever** boy." (clever describes the boy)
- "She is an **intelligent** girl." (intelligent describes the girl)

---

### Degrees of Comparison

Every adjective has three degrees:

#### Positive Degree
The base form — used when no comparison is being made.

| Adjective |
|-----------|
| Weak, Strong, Clever, Tall, Big, Short, Beautiful |

#### Comparative Degree
Used to compare **two** things. Add **-er** to short adjectives.

| Positive | Comparative | Use |
|----------|------------|-----|
| Weak | **Weaker** | He is weaker **than** Rahim. |
| Strong | **Stronger** | She is stronger **than** me. |
| Clever | **Cleverer** | Ali is cleverer than Karim. |
| Tall | **Taller** | Dhrubo is taller than his brother. |
| Big | **Bigger** (double final consonant) | This room is bigger than that one. |
| Short | **Shorter** | This road is shorter than the other. |

> The comparative can also compare something to its own past state:
> "He is **wiser** now." (compared to how he used to be — no second person needed)

#### Superlative Degree
Shows the **highest degree** — nothing surpasses it. Add **-est** to short adjectives.

| Positive | Superlative | Use |
|----------|------------|-----|
| Weak | **Weakest** | He is the **weakest** player in the team. |
| Strong | **Strongest** | She is the **strongest** student. |
| Clever | **Cleverest** | He is the **cleverest** boy in class. |
| Big | **Biggest** | This is the **biggest** building in Dhaka. |

---

### Special Rule — Long Adjectives (More / Most)

For adjectives with **more than one syllable** (words that take more than one breath to say), use **More** (comparative) and **Most** (superlative) instead of adding -er/-est.

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|----------|------------|-------------|
| Beautiful | **More** Beautiful | **Most** Beautiful |
| Intelligent | **More** Intelligent | **Most** Intelligent |
| Comfortable | **More** Comfortable | **Most** Comfortable |
| Honest | **More** Honest | **Most** Honest |
| Interesting | **More** Interesting | **Most** Interesting |

**Easy Rule:** If the adjective is a long word (like "beautiful" or "intelligent") → use More/Most.
If the adjective is a short word (like "tall" or "weak") → use -er/-est.

---

### Irregular Adjectives (Must Memorise)

| Positive | Comparative | Superlative |
|----------|------------|-------------|
| Good | Better | Best |
| Bad | Worse | Worst |
| Many / Much | More | Most |
| Little | Less | Least |
| Far | Farther / Further | Farthest / Furthest |

## 6. Adverbs

### Definition
Adverbs are words that provide **additional information about a verb** — they tell us how, when, where, or how often an action happened.

**Example:** "He has done his work **brilliantly**."
→ *Brilliantly* tells us **how** he did the work → Adverb of Manner.

### How Adverbs Are Formed
Most adverbs are formed by adding **-ly** to an adjective:

| Adjective | Adverb |
|-----------|--------|
| Clever | **Cleverly** |
| Beautiful | **Beautifully** |
| Brilliant | **Brilliantly** |
| Quiet | **Quietly** |
| Angry | **Angrily** |
| Quick | **Quickly** |

> **Important:** Not all adverbs end in -ly.
> "He runs **fast**." → *fast* is an adverb here. **Never say "fastly"** — it is wrong!
> "She works **hard**." → *hard* is an adverb here. **Never say "hardly"** — "hardly" means "almost not at all", which is a completely different meaning!

---

### Types of Adverbs

| Type | What it Shows | Examples |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Adverb of Manner** | *How* the action was done | Loudly, Quietly, Angrily, Badly, Well, Fast, Brilliantly |
| **Adverb of Place** | *Where* the action happened | Here, There, Outside, Down, Indoors, Nearby, Everywhere |
| **Adverb of Time** | *When* the action happened | Now, Yesterday, Soon, Later, Tomorrow, Already, Then |
| **Adverb of Frequency** | *How often* the action happens | Always, Sometimes, Often, Usually, Seldom, Never, Generally |
| **Adverb of Degree** | *To what extent* something happened | Extremely, Absolutely, Almost, Just, Completely, Fully, Quite |

---

### Position of Adverbs in a Sentence

- **Adverb of Manner** → Usually at the **end** of a sentence
- **If the object is long** → Adverb goes in the **middle** (between subject and verb)

**Examples:**
- "She ate **quickly**." (adverb at end — short sentence)
- "She **quickly** ate the enormous plate of food she had ordered." (adverb in middle — long object)

## 7. Prepositions

### Definition
Prepositions are words that show the **relationship** of a noun or pronoun with other parts of the sentence. They mainly show **Place, Time, and Direction**.

**Common Prepositions:** On, In, At, By, With, Into, Above, Over, Under, From, For, Since, Until, Till, Through, Towards, During

---

### Key Prepositions — Detailed Usage Rules

#### AT, IN, ON — For Time

| Preposition | Used for | Examples |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| **At** | Specific clock time | At 8 o'clock, At noon, At midnight |
| **In** | Months, years, seasons, parts of day | In January, In 2006, In summer, In the morning |
| **On** | Days and specific dates | On Monday, On 19th February, On my birthday |

#### BY vs UNTIL / TILL

| Preposition | Usage | Example |
|-------------|-------|---------|
| **By** | **Deadline** — "not later than a certain time" | Complete this work **by** evening. I will arrive **by** 9 o'clock. |
| **Until / Till** | **Duration** — "up to a point in time, continuously" | I was working **until** midnight. Wait here **until** morning. |

> **Note:** Until and Till have the same meaning. Both are correct.

**Memory Trick:**
- BY = There is a deadline. Something must happen before that time.
- UNTIL = Something continues up to that point.

**Example:**
- "Submit your assignment **by** Friday." (deadline — must submit before Friday)
- "I waited **until** Friday." (I kept waiting continuously up to Friday)

#### SINCE vs FOR

| Preposition | Usage | Example |
|-------------|-------|---------|
| **Since** | **Specific / definite** point in time — a date, year, or clock time you can see on a calendar or watch | Since 1996, Since 8 PM, Since Monday, Since last year |
| **For** | **Amount / duration** of time — a period, not a specific point | For four hours, For five years, For a long time, For three months |

**Memory Trick:**
- SINCE = You can point to the exact time on a calendar or clock.
- FOR = You are talking about how long something lasted.

**Examples:**
- "She has been studying **since** 2020." (2020 = specific year on calendar → SINCE)
- "She has been studying **for** three years." (three years = amount of time → FOR)
- "I have been waiting **since** 6 PM." (6 PM = specific time on clock → SINCE)
- "I have been waiting **for** two hours." (two hours = amount → FOR)

#### Additional SINCE Usage — Meaning "Because"
SINCE can also mean **"because"** when used at the **beginning** of a sentence:

"**Since** it was raining, we stayed home." (Since = because)
"**Since** I was late, I missed the bus." (Since = because)

#### FROM — Shows Origin or Source
Shows **where something comes from**.

"I am coming **from** Chittagong."
"This letter is **from** my friend."

> Never use FROM for time durations. Use FOR or SINCE instead.

#### DURING — For Events or Periods
"**During** the exam, please stay quiet."
"**During** the holidays, I visited Cox's Bazar."

#### BY — For the Doer (in Passive Voice)
Shows who performed the action:
"This letter was written **by** Karim."
"The match was won **by** Bangladesh."

#### WITH — For Tools or Instruments
Shows the tool used to perform an action:
"I am writing **with** a pen."
"He cut the fruit **with** a knife."
"She hit the ball **with** a bat."

#### FOR — For Purpose or Benefit
"This gift is **for** you."
"I am looking **for** a job."
"He works hard **for** his family."

## 8. Conjunctions

### Definition
Conjunctions are words that **connect** two clauses, sentences, or phrases.

**Examples:** And, But, Or, So, For, Since, Because, Although, Even though, Yet, Still, Therefore, If, Whether, Until, Neither…Nor, Either…Or, Not only…But also

---

### Common Conjunctions & Their Usage

| Conjunction | Usage | Example |
|-------------|-------|---------|
| **And** | Adds two ideas together | I like tea **and** coffee. |
| **But** | Shows contrast or exception | I studied hard **but** I failed. |
| **So / Therefore** | Shows result | It was raining, **so** I took an umbrella. |
| **Although / Even though** | Shows contrast — unexpected result | **Although** he studied, he failed. |
| **Since / Because** | Shows reason | I stayed home **because** I was sick. |
| **Either…Or** | Gives a choice between two options | **Either** study **or** fail. |
| **Neither…Nor** | Negative — rejects both options | **Neither** Rahim **nor** Karim came. |
| **Not only…But also** | Adds emphasis | She is **not only** intelligent **but also** hardworking. |
| **If** | Shows condition | I will come **if** you invite me. |
| **Whether** | Shows a choice in indirect speech | I do not know **whether** he will come. |
| **Until / Till** | Shows time — up to a point | Wait here **until** I return. |

---

### BUT as an Exception Word
"But" can also mean **"except"**:

"I love all subjects **but** Mathematics." = "I love all subjects **except** Mathematics."
"Everyone came **but** Rahim." = "Everyone came **except** Rahim."

---

### So…That vs So That

| Construction | Meaning | Example |
|-------------|---------|---------|
| **So + adjective + That** | Shows a result | He was **so** tired **that** he could not walk. |
| **So That** | Shows purpose — "in order that" | I studied hard **so that** I could pass. |

## 9. Interjections

### Definition
Interjections are words or phrases that express **strong emotion, surprise, or a sudden feeling**. They stand **alone** and are not grammatically connected to the rest of the sentence. They are usually followed by an **exclamation mark (!)**.

| Interjection | Emotion |
|-------------|---------|
| **Wow!** | Surprise or admiration |
| **Alas!** | Sorrow or grief |
| **Hurray!** | Joy or celebration |
| **Oh no!** | Shock or disappointment |
| **Phew!** | Relief |
| **Bravo!** | Appreciation or well done |
| **Ouch!** | Pain |
| **Hey!** | To get attention |

## 10. Direct & Indirect Speech (Narration)

### What is Direct & Indirect Speech?

- **Direct Speech:** The **exact words** someone said — written inside quotation marks (" ")
- **Indirect Speech (Reported Speech):** **Reporting** what someone said to a third person — no quotation marks, tenses change

**Example:**
- Direct: He said, **"I will help you tomorrow."**
- Indirect: He said that he **would** help me **the next day**.

Notice: "will" changed to "would" and "tomorrow" changed to "the next day".

---

### Core Rules for Converting Direct to Indirect

#### Rule 1 — Tense Shifts to Past
When we report speech, the time has already passed. So all tenses shift one step back into the past.

| Direct Speech Tense | Changes to in Indirect |
|--------------------|----------------------|
| **will** | **would** |
| **can** | **could** |
| **may** | **might** |
| **is / am / are** | **was / were** |
| **has / have** | **had** |
| **do / does** | **did** |
| Simple Present (eats) | Simple Past (ate) |
| Present Continuous (is eating) | Past Continuous (was eating) |
| Present Perfect (has eaten) | Past Perfect (had eaten) |
| Simple Past (ate) | Past Perfect (had eaten) |

#### Rule 2 — Time and Place Words Change

| Direct Speech Word | Changes to in Indirect |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| tomorrow | the next day |
| yesterday | the previous day |
| tonight | that night |
| now | then |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| today | that day |
| last week | the previous week |
| ago | before |

---

### Said vs Told — When to Use Which

| Situation | Use | Example |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| **No listener mentioned** in direct speech | **Said** | He said that he was tired. |
| **Listener IS mentioned** in direct speech ("said to me") | **Told** | He told me that he was tired. |

**Examples:**
- Direct: He said, "I am tired." → Indirect: He **said** that he was tired. *(no "to me" — use said)*
- Direct: He said to me, "I am tired." → Indirect: He **told me** that he was tired. *(listener mentioned — use told)*

---

### Question Mark in Indirect Speech
When converting a **question** to indirect speech, the **question mark disappears** and the sentence becomes a statement.

- Direct: He asked, "Are you free tomorrow?"
- Indirect: He asked me **whether** I was free the next day. *(No question mark!)*

**Rules:**
- **Yes/No questions** → Use **if** or **whether**
- **WH-questions** (who, what, where, when, why, how) → Use the same WH-word

---

### CUET Exam Strategy for Direct-Indirect Questions
When you see options in the exam:
1. Check if the **tense has been shifted to past** — cut any option where tense is NOT in past.
2. Check if **time/place words** (tomorrow, tonight, this, here) have been changed.
3. Check if **said/told** is used correctly.
4. The option that satisfies all rules = correct answer.

## 11. Active & Passive Voice

### Definition
- **Active Voice:** The **subject performs** the action → "Rahim wrote the letter."
- **Passive Voice:** The **subject receives** the action → "The letter was written by Rahim."

---

### Key Rules for Making Passive Voice

#### Rule 1 — Object becomes Subject
The object of the active sentence moves to the front as the subject of the passive sentence.

#### Rule 2 — Main Verb becomes V3 (Past Participle)
Always use the **third form** of the verb in passive voice.

#### Rule 3 — Add the Correct Helping Verb
The helping verb (is/am/are/was/were/has been/have been/had been) must match the tense.

#### Rule 4 — Doer is introduced with "BY"
"Karim wrote it." → "It was written **by Karim**."

---

### Pronoun Changes in Passive Voice

When the object becomes the subject (moves to the front), pronoun form changes:

| Objective → Nominative (object becomes subject) |
|------------------------------------------------|
| Me → **I** |
| Us → **We** |
| Him → **He** |
| Her → **She** |
| Them → **They** |

When the subject moves after "by" (becomes object), pronoun form changes:

| Nominative → Objective (subject moves after "by") |
|--------------------------------------------------|
| I → **Me** |
| We → **Us** |
| He → **Him** |
| She → **Her** |
| They → **Them** |

---

### Passive Voice — Tense-by-Tense Structure

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|-------|--------|---------|
| Simple Present | He writes letters. | Letters **are written** by him. |
| Simple Past | He wrote a letter. | A letter **was written** by him. |
| Present Continuous | He is writing a letter. | A letter **is being written** by him. |
| Past Continuous | He was writing a letter. | A letter **was being written** by him. |
| Present Perfect | He has written a letter. | A letter **has been written** by him. |
| Past Perfect | He had written a letter. | A letter **had been written** by him. |
| Simple Future | He will write a letter. | A letter **will be written** by him. |

---

### CUET Strategy for Active-Passive Questions
When checking answer options:
1. Is the **object placed at the beginning**? If not, cut that option.
2. Is the **V3 (third form)** of the verb used? If not, cut that option.
3. These two checks alone will eliminate most wrong options immediately.

## 12. Tenses — Key Rules for Error Detection

### Tense Hint Words in Sentences
In error detection questions, the sentence itself gives you a **hint word** that tells you which tense is correct. Always look for the hint word first.

| Hint Word / Phrase | Tense Required |
|-------------------|---------------|
| Yesterday, Last week, Two days ago, Last month | **Simple Past** |
| Always, Usually, Every day, Generally, Regularly | **Simple Present** |
| Just, Already, Yet, Ever, Never, Recently | **Present Perfect** |
| Since + specific time (since 2020, since Monday) | **Present Perfect Continuous** |
| For + duration (for 3 years, for two hours) | **Present Perfect Continuous** |
| Tomorrow, Next week, Next year | **Simple Future** |

> **Very Important Rule:** If the sentence contains "yesterday" or "last week" — always use **Simple Past**, never Present Perfect.

**Wrong:** The committee **has met** yesterday. ❌
**Correct:** The committee **met** yesterday. ✅
(Reason: "yesterday" is a hint for Simple Past)

---

### Subject-Verb Agreement Rules

| Rule | Correct Example |
|------|----------------|
| Singular subject → singular verb | She **does** not like cricket. |
| Plural subject → plural verb | They **do** not like cricket. |
| **Each / Every** → always singular verb | **Each** of the students **has** submitted the work. |
| **None / Neither** (used alone) → singular verb | **Neither** of the answers **is** correct. |
| **Neither…Nor** → verb agrees with the **nearest** subject | Neither the students nor the teacher **is** present. |
| **One of** → always followed by a **plural noun** | One of my **friends** (not "friend") asked me. |
| Collective nouns (team, jury, committee) | Usually takes a **singular** verb in formal writing. |

---

### "One of" Rule — Very Important for CUET

"One of" is always followed by a **PLURAL noun**, even though we are talking about one person.

**Wrong:** One of my friend asked me. ❌
**Correct:** One of my **friends** asked me. ✅

**Reason:** We are choosing from a group of friends (plural), so the noun must be plural.

---

### Relative Clause After "One of those…"

When a relative clause follows "one of those…", the verb in the relative clause is **PLURAL** (it agrees with the plural noun, not with "one").

**Example:** "She is one of those rare individuals who **go** out of their way to help others."
- "who go" is correct because "who" refers to "individuals" (plural), not to "she".

**Wrong:** "She is one of those rare individuals who **goes**…" ❌

---

## 13. Modal Verbs

### What Are Modal Verbs?
Modal verbs express **mood, ability, possibility, obligation, permission, or habit**. They are always followed by the **base form (V1)** of the main verb — never V2 or V3.

---

### Modal Verbs & Their Uses

| Modal | Main Use | Example |
|-------|---------|---------|
| **Can** | Ability / Informal permission | I **can** speak English. Can I sit here? |
| **Could** | Past ability / Polite request | I **could** swim when I was young. |
| **May** | Formal permission / Possibility | **May** I come in, sir? It **may** rain today. |
| **Might** | Remote possibility / Suggestion | He **might** come later. |
| **Should** | Advice / Recommendation | You **should** study every day. |
| **Must** | Strong obligation / Necessity | You **must** submit the assignment today. |
| **Would** | Polite request / Hypothetical situation / Past habit | **Would** you like some tea? |
| **Shall** | Formal future (first person) | I **shall** return by Monday. |
| **Ought to** | Moral duty or ideal situation | You **ought to** respect your elders. |
| **Used to** | Past habit (no longer done now) | I **used to** play cricket every evening. |
| **Need** | Necessity | You **need** to study harder. |
| **Will** | Simple future | He **will** come tomorrow. |

---

### Modals in the Past — Regret or Missed Opportunity

To express a **past action that did NOT happen** (regret, missed chance, logical deduction), use:

**Formula: Modal (past form) + HAVE + V3**

| Formula | Meaning | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Should + have + V3** | You were supposed to do it but didn't | You **should have studied** harder. |
| **Could + have + V3** | You were able to do it but didn't | I **could have helped** you. |
| **Would + have + V3** | You intended to do it but didn't | I **would have come** if you had called. |
| **Must + have + V3** | Logical conclusion about the past | He **must have left** already — the door is locked. |

**More Examples:**
- "You **should have called** me." (You didn't call — regret)
- "I **could have done** better." (I had the ability but didn't use it)
- "She **must have arrived** by now." (logical conclusion)

> **Exam Clue:** If you see a **V3 (past participle)** right after should/could/would/must, it is a **past modal** construction. Example: "You should have **stood** by him" — "stood" is V3 of "stand".

## 14. Clauses

### What is a Clause?
A clause is a **group of words** that contains both a subject and a verb. It may or may not be a complete sentence on its own.

---

### Types of Clauses

#### Noun Clause
A clause that works as a **Subject or Object** in the sentence — it takes the place of a noun.

**Examples:**
- "I want to know **who came here**." (*who came here* = object of "know")
- "Please tell me **who came to meet me**." (*who came to meet me* = object of "tell")
- "**That he lied** is clear." (*That he lied* = subject of the sentence)

**How to identify:** Ask yourself — Can I replace this clause with a noun/pronoun? If yes → Noun Clause.

---

#### Adjective Clause (Relative Clause)
A clause that **describes or identifies a noun** — answering *which one, what kind, who, where, when*.
Usually introduced by: **who, whom, which, that, where, when**

**Examples:**
- "The boy **who came here** is my brother." (*who came here* describes "the boy")
- "I know the place **where they will meet**." (*where they will meet* describes "the place")
- "I remember the day **when we first met**." (*when we first met* describes "the day")
- "The book **that I borrowed** is interesting." (*that I borrowed* describes "the book")

**Key point:** An adjective clause always comes **right after** the noun it describes.

---

#### Adverb Clause
A clause that describes a **verb** — showing reason, condition, time, place, manner, or contrast.
Usually introduced by: **because, although, since, if, when, as, while, until, as soon as**

**Examples:**
- "I missed the bus **because I was late**." (*because I was late* = reason)
- "I will call you **when I arrive**." (*when I arrive* = time)
- "**Although it was raining**, we went out." (*Although it was raining* = contrast)
- "**If you study hard**, you will pass." (*If you study hard* = condition)

---

### Quick Identification Guide

| Clause Type | Key Signal Words | Function |
|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Noun Clause** | That, who, what, whether, if (after verbs like know, think, say) | Acts as Subject or Object |
| **Adjective Clause** | Who, which, that, where, when — placed after a noun | Describes a noun |
| **Adverb Clause** | Because, although, since, if, when, as, while, until | Describes a verb |

---

## 15. Figures of Speech

### What Are Figures of Speech?
Also called **Poetic Devices**, these are special and creative ways of using language to produce effect, beauty, or a vivid image in the reader's mind.

### Major Figures of Speech

| Figure of Speech | Definition | Example |
|-----------------|-----------|---------| 
| **Simile** | Comparison using **"as…as"** or **"like"** | She sings **like** an angel. / He is **as strong as** a lion. |
| **Metaphor** | **Direct comparison** without using as/like — one thing is said to BE another | "You are the **sunshine** of my life." / "Life is a **journey**." |
| **Personification** | Giving **human qualities** to non-human things | "**The wind whispered** through the trees." / "**The sun smiled** at us." |
| **Hyperbole** | **Extreme exaggeration** — something physically impossible stated for effect | "I have told you **a million times**." / "I am **so hungry I could eat a horse**." |
| **Alliteration** | **Repetition of the same initial consonant sound** in nearby words | "**S**ilent **s**nakes **s**lide **s**lowly." |
| **Onomatopoeia** | Words that **imitate the sound** they describe | Buzz, Hiss, Roar, Splash, Woof, Crack, Sizzle |
| **Imagery** | Language that creates a **vivid mental picture** | "The golden sun melted slowly into the dark sea." |
| **Oxymoron** | Combination of **two contradictory/opposite** words | Deafening silence / Bittersweet / Living death |

---

### Key Identification Tips

- **Personification:** Non-human things (wind, sun, moon, trees) are doing human actions (whisper, smile, speak, cry)
- **Simile:** Always look for the words **"as…as"** or **"like"**
- **Metaphor:** One thing is directly **called** something else — no "as" or "like"
- **Hyperbole:** The statement is physically **impossible** — used for dramatic effect
- **Alliteration:** The **same first sound** repeats in several words close together
- **Oxymoron:** Two **opposite words** placed together to create meaning

---

## 16. Determiners

### What Are Determiners?
Determiners are words that come **before a noun** and tell us which one, how many, or how much of something.

**Examples:** Each, Every, Both, All, Some, Many, Much, Few, Any, None, Either, Neither

---

### Key Rules for Determiners

| Determiner | Rule | Correct Example |
|-----------|------|----------------|
| **Each** | Used for individuals in a group — one by one | **Each** student must submit a form. |
| **Every** | Used for all members of a group collectively | **Every** student must attend the class. |
| **Each / Every** | **Always take a singular verb** | Each of the students **has** submitted. ✅ (NOT "have") |
| **Both** | Refers to two things | **Both** answers are wrong. |
| **None** | Means "not one" → takes a singular verb | **None** of the answers **is** correct. |
| **Few** | Small number — negative feeling (not enough) | **Few** students passed. (most failed) |
| **A few** | Small number — positive feeling (at least some) | **A few** students passed. (some did well) |
| **Much** | Used with **uncountable** nouns | **Much** water was wasted. |
| **Many** | Used with **countable** nouns | **Many** students were absent. |
| **Either** | One of two options | **Either** option is fine. |
| **Neither** | Not one, not the other | **Neither** option is good. |

---

## 17. Articles — A, An, The

### Indefinite Articles: A and AN
Used for **any one** of a kind — not specific, not particular.

**Rule for A vs AN — Based on SOUND, not spelling:**
- Use **AN** before words that **begin with a vowel sound** (a, e, i, o, u sounds)
- Use **A** before words that **begin with a consonant sound**

> The rule is about the **spoken sound**, not the written letter!

| Word | Starts with (sound) | Correct Article | Reason |
|------|--------------------|-----------------|---------| 
| Apple | Vowel sound /æ/ | **An** apple | Starts with vowel sound |
| Umbrella | Vowel sound /ʌ/ | **An** umbrella | Starts with vowel sound |
| Hour | Vowel sound /aʊ/ — 'h' is silent | **An** hour | 'H' is silent, so it sounds like "our" |
| Honest | Vowel sound /ɒ/ — 'h' is silent | **An** honest person | 'H' is silent |
| University | Consonant sound /juː/ — sounds like "you" | **A** university | Starts with "y" sound |
| Unique | Consonant sound /juː/ — sounds like "you" | **A** unique idea | Starts with "y" sound |
| European | Consonant sound /juː/ — sounds like "you" | **A** European country | Starts with "y" sound |

---

### Definite Article: THE
Used for **specific** things — something already known, already mentioned, or one of a kind.

**THE is used before:**

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| Something already mentioned | "I saw **a** dog. **The** dog was barking." (second mention = specific) |
| Rivers, seas, oceans, canals | **The** Padma, **The** Meghna, **The** Pacific Ocean, **The** Nile |
| Mountain ranges | **The** Himalayas, **The** Alps, **The** Andes |
| Newspapers and magazines | **The** Daily Star, **The** Times, **The** Guardian |
| Groups of islands or countries (plural/union names) | **The** USA, **The** UAE, **The** Philippines, **The** Netherlands |
| Historical monuments | **The** Taj Mahal, **The** Eiffel Tower, **The** Colosseum |
| Religious books | **The** Bible, **The** Quran, **The** Gita, **The** Torah |
| Unique objects (only one in existence) | **The** Sun, **The** Moon, **The** Earth, **The** sky |
| Parts of the day | In **the** morning, In **the** evening, In **the** afternoon |
| Superlatives | She is **the** best student. |

**THE is NOT used before:**

| Category | Examples |
|----------|---------|
| Names of people | ~~The~~ Rahim came. / ~~The~~ Fatima is kind. |
| Names of most countries (singular) | ~~The~~ Bangladesh, ~~The~~ India, ~~The~~ France |
| Names of languages | ~~The~~ English, ~~The~~ Bengali, ~~The~~ Arabic |
| Meals | ~~The~~ breakfast, ~~The~~ lunch, ~~The~~ dinner |
| Names of sports | ~~The~~ cricket, ~~The~~ football, ~~The~~ tennis |
| Names of subjects | ~~The~~ Mathematics, ~~The~~ Science, ~~The~~ History |

## 18. Error Detection — Strategy Guide

### The Golden Approach — Follow This Sequence Every Time

**Step 1 — Check Subject-Verb Agreement**
Does the verb match the subject in number (singular/plural)?
- Singular subject → singular verb (has, is, does, was)
- Plural subject → plural verb (have, are, do, were)

**Step 2 — Check the Tense**
Is there a hint word in the sentence? Find it and match the tense:
- "yesterday / last week / two days ago" → Simple Past
- "since / for" → Perfect or Perfect Continuous
- "always / every day" → Simple Present

**Step 3 — Check the Preposition**
Is the correct preposition used?
- By / Until / Since / For / With / In / On / At — each has specific rules (see Section 7)

**Step 4 — Check Articles and Determiners**
If there is an article or determiner, is it correct?
- A / An / The — applied correctly?
- Each / Every / None — followed by singular verb?

**Step 5 — Check Gerund or Infinitive**
Is the verb form (ing vs to + V1) correct?
- "I enjoy **playing**." (enjoy + gerund) ✅
- "I want **to play**." (want + infinitive) ✅

**Step 6 — Check Modal Verbs**
If there is a modal, is it used correctly?
- Modal + V1 (base form) in present
- Modal + have + V3 for past regret

**Step 7 — Elimination (Last Resort)**
If steps 1-6 show the other parts are correct, the remaining underlined part must be the error.

---

### The Most Important Tip for Long Sentences

> **The longer and more complex the error detection sentence, the simpler the error actually is.**

Examiners use long, difficult-looking sentences to create fear. But the error is almost always one of these basics:
- Wrong helping verb (is instead of are, has instead of have)
- Wrong tense form (has met → met with "yesterday")
- Wrong preposition (in → on, by → until)
- Subject-verb number disagreement

**Strategy:** Do not get distracted by difficult vocabulary inside the sentence. Focus only on the underlined part and the hint words.

## 19. Conditional Sentences

### Type 1 — Real / Possible Condition
Something that is **likely to happen** if the condition is met.

**Structure:** If + Simple Present → will + V1
- "**If** you study hard, you **will** pass."
- "**If** it rains, I **will** stay home."
- "**If** she calls, I **will** answer."

---

### Type 2 — Unreal / Hypothetical (Present or Future)
Something **imaginary, unlikely, or hypothetical** — not happening in reality.

**Structure:** If + Simple Past → would + V1
- "**If** I **had** money, I **would buy** a new phone."
- "**If** I **were** rich, I **would travel** the world."
- "**If** she **knew** the answer, she **would tell** us."

> **Important:** In Type 2 conditionals, always use **"were"** (not "was") for all subjects in formal/written English.
> "If I **were** you..." ✅ (not "If I was you")
> "If she **were** here..." ✅ (not "If she was here")

---

### Type 3 — Unreal / Hypothetical (Past — Regret)
Something that **did NOT happen in the past** — expressing regret or imagining a different outcome.

**Structure:** If + Past Perfect (had + V3) → would have + V3
- "**If** I **had known** about the meeting, I **would have attended** it."
- "**If** she **had studied** harder, she **would have passed**."
- "**If** he **had called** me, I **would have helped** him."

---

### Inverted Conditional (Without "If")
Sometimes "If" is dropped and the sentence starts directly with **Had**:

| Normal form | Inverted form |
|------------|--------------|
| If she had come… | **Had** she **come**… |
| If I had known… | **Had** I **known**… |
| If he had tried… | **Had** he **tried**… |

**Exam Example:**
"**Had** I **known** about the meeting earlier, I _______ attended it."
→ Answer: **would have** → "I **would have attended** it."

---

## 20. Exclamatory Sentences

### What Are They?
Exclamatory sentences express **strong emotion** — surprise, joy, sorrow, or admiration. They always end with an **exclamation mark (!)**.

### Formation Patterns

| Emotion | Pattern | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Admiration / Surprise | **What a + adj + noun + subject + verb!** | **What a** wonderful player he is! |
| Joy / Celebration | **Hurray!** | **Hurray!** Bangladesh won the match! |
| Sorrow / Grief | **Alas!** | **Alas!** He lost everything. |
| Surprise | **What! / Oh!** | **What!** You already finished? |

### Converting Statements to Exclamatory

| Statement | Exclamatory |
|-----------|------------|
| She is a wonderful woman. | **What a** wonderful woman she is! |
| It was a beautiful day. | **What a** beautiful day it was! |
| He is very tall. | **How** tall he is! |
| The news was terrible. | **What** terrible news! |
