## 1. Figures of Speech (Poetic Devices)

Figures of speech are special expressions used in language and poetry to create effect, emphasis, or beauty.

---

### 1.1 Simile

- **Definition:** A comparison between two things using the words **"as"** or **"like"**.
- **Key signal words:** *as…as*, *like*
- **Examples:**
  - *She is as sweet as honey.*
  - *He runs like the wind.*

---

### 1.2 Metaphor

- **Definition:** A **direct** comparison between two things — one thing is said to **be** another. No "as" or "like" is used.
- **Examples:**
  - *You are the apple of my eye.*
  - *Life is a journey.*
  - *He is a lion in battle.*

> **Simile vs Metaphor:** Simile uses "as/like" for comparison; Metaphor makes a direct comparison without those words.

---

### 1.3 Personification

- **Definition:** Giving **human qualities, actions, or feelings** to non-human things (objects, animals, nature).
- **How to identify:** A non-human thing is doing something only humans do — speaking, smiling, whispering, crying, etc.
- **Examples:**
  - *The wind whispered through the trees.* → Wind cannot whisper; whispering is a human action.
  - *The moon looked down at me.*
  - *The flowers smiled at me.*
  - *The tree spoke to me with its branches.*

> **PYQ Example:** "The wind whispered through the trees" → **Personification** ✓

---

### 1.4 Hyperbole

- **Definition:** An extreme **exaggeration** that is not literally true, used for emphasis or dramatic effect.
- **Key clue:** The statement is physically impossible or wildly overstated.
- **Examples:**
  - *I've told you a million times.*
  - *I'm so hungry I could eat a horse.*
  - *I will move mountains for you.*

---

### 1.5 Alliteration

- **Definition:** The **repetition of the same initial consonant sound** in closely connected words.
- **Examples:**
  - *Sun, Sweet, Solon* → all start with the "S" sound → Alliteration
  - *Peter Piper picked a peck of pickled peppers.*
  - *She sells seashells by the seashore.*

---

### 1.6 Onomatopoeia

- **Definition:** Words that **imitate the natural sounds** of things — animals, objects, or actions.
- **Examples:**
  - *Buzz, hiss, roar, moo, sizzle, clang, splash*
  - The word sounds like what it describes.

---

### 1.7 Imagery

- **Definition:** Use of **vivid, descriptive language** that appeals to the senses (sight, smell, sound, touch, taste), creating a mental image in the reader's mind.
- **Examples:**
  - *The golden sun melted into the purple horizon.*
  - *The smell of fresh bread wafted through the house.*

---

### 1.8 Oxymoron

- **Definition:** A figure of speech where two **contradictory or opposite** words are placed together.
- **Examples:**
  - *Deafening silence*
  - *Bittersweet*
  - *Living death*
  - *Dark light*

---

### Quick Reference Table — Figures of Speech

| Figure of Speech | Key Feature | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Simile | Comparison using *as/like* | Sweet as honey |
| Metaphor | Direct comparison (X is Y) | Life is a journey |
| Personification | Human qualities to non-human things | Wind whispered |
| Hyperbole | Wild exaggeration | I've told you a million times |
| Alliteration | Same initial sound repeated | She sells seashells |
| Onomatopoeia | Word sounds like the thing | Buzz, hiss, roar |
| Imagery | Vivid sensory description | Golden sun melting |
| Oxymoron | Two opposites together | Deafening silence |

## 2. Direct and Indirect Speech (Narration)

### 2.1 What is Direct and Indirect Speech?

- **Direct Speech:** Reporting someone's exact words, enclosed in quotation marks.
  - *He said, "I will help you tomorrow."*
- **Indirect Speech (Reported Speech):** Reporting what someone said without using their exact words.
  - *He said that he would help me the next day.*

---

### 2.2 Core Rule — Tense Shift to Past

> **Golden Rule:** When converting Direct Speech → Indirect Speech, the tense **always shifts to the past** because the original statement was made at an earlier point in time.

**Logic:** The words were spoken earlier. By the time you report them, that time has passed. So past tense is used.

#### Tense Conversion Table

| Direct Speech Tense | Indirect Speech Tense |
|---|---|
| Present Simple | Past Simple |
| Present Continuous | Past Continuous |
| Present Perfect | Past Perfect |
| Past Simple | Past Perfect |
| **will** | **would** |
| **can** | **could** |
| **may** | **might** |
| **shall** | **should** |

---

### 2.3 Time and Place Word Changes

Certain time/place expressions also change logically:

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| tonight | that night |

> **Why?** If I report tomorrow's meeting two days later, "tomorrow" no longer means the same day — it becomes "the next day."

---

### 2.4 "Said" vs "Told" — When to Use Which

| Condition | Use |
|---|---|
| No object after the reporting verb | **said that** |
| Object mentioned (said to *someone*) | **told** (+ object) |

- *He said, "I will come."* → *He **said that** he would come.* ✓
- *He said to me, "I will come."* → *He **told me that** he would come.* ✓

**Rule:** If "said to + object" appears in Direct Speech, convert it to "told + object" in Indirect Speech. If just "said" (no object), keep it as "said that."

---

### 2.5 Interrogative Sentences in Indirect Speech

- When the direct speech is a **question**, the indirect speech:
  - Uses **if / whether** for Yes/No questions
  - Uses **WH-word** for WH questions
  - **No question mark** in indirect speech — the sentence ends with a full stop (period).

**Example:**
- Direct: *She said to me, "Are you free tomorrow?"*
- Indirect: *She asked me if I was free the next day.* ✓ (Note: no question mark)

---

### 2.6 PYQ Examples

**Q:** He said, "I will help you tomorrow." → Convert to Indirect Speech.
- ❌ *He said that he will help me tomorrow.* (tense not changed, "tomorrow" not changed)
- ✅ *He said that he would help me the next day.*

**Q:** "Are you free tomorrow?" she said to me. → Convert to Indirect Speech.
- ✅ *She asked me if I was free the next day.*

---

### 2.7 Exam Tip for Direct/Indirect Speech Questions

Since options are given in the exam:
1. **Check if tense has been shifted to past** → Eliminate options where it hasn't.
2. **Check if time/place words have been changed** (tomorrow → next day, etc.) → Eliminate options where they haven't.
3. The remaining option is your answer.

> Most marks are lost when students miss these two checks. Don't overthink — just apply these two filters.

## 3. Prepositions

Prepositions are words that **connect parts of a sentence** and show the relationship between them (time, place, direction, manner, reason, etc.).

---

### 3.1 UNTIL / TILL

- **Meaning:** Up to a certain point in time — used to express **duration**.
- **Use:** Shows how long an action continued.
- **Until** and **Till** are identical in meaning — one is formal, one informal.

**Examples:**
- *I waited for you until morning.*
- *He worked until midnight.*
- *She studied till 2 AM.*

> ❌ Do NOT use *until* for deadlines (see BY below).

---

### 3.2 BY

- **Meaning 1 — Deadline:** Indicates a **deadline** — by a specific time or date.
- **Meaning 2 — Doer (Passive Voice):** Indicates the agent/doer of an action.

**Deadline Examples:**
- *I will arrive by 9 o'clock.*
- *Submit the project by the end of the week.*
- *I will meet you by tonight.*

**Doer Examples:**
- *This book was written **by** her.*
- *The work was done **by** him.*

> **Key Distinction:**
> - *I worked **until** midnight.* → I was doing the work up to midnight (duration)
> - *Complete this **by** morning.* → Finish before morning (deadline)

---

### 3.3 SINCE

- **Meaning 1 — Definite Point in Time:** Used with a **specific, identifiable** time — something you can point to on a clock or calendar.
- **Meaning 2 — Reason:** Used at the beginning of a sentence to give a reason (like "because").

**Definite Time Examples (clock/calendar):**
- *I have been waiting since 8 PM.*
- *She has been learning English since 2006.*
- *He has been working here since 19th February.*

> **Test:** Can you point to this time on a clock or calendar? If YES → use **SINCE**.

**Reason Examples:**
- *Since I was born in a Bengali family, I speak Bengali.*
- *Since I started late, I could not finish.*

---

### 3.4 FOR

- **Meaning 1 — Indefinite Duration:** Used for a period of time that is **not pinpointed** on a clock/calendar.
- **Meaning 2 — Purpose:** Used to express purpose or benefit.
- **Meaning 3 — Phrasal/Fixed Expressions:** Used in fixed collocations (*wait for, look for, taken for granted*).

**Duration Examples (indefinite):**
- *I have been waiting for four hours.*
- *She has been studying for three months.*
- *I have been living here for years.*

> **Test:** Can you NOT point to this time on a clock/calendar? If YES → use **FOR**.

**Purpose Examples:**
- *This is for you.*
- *I bought this for the project.*

**Fixed Phrases:**
- *wait for, look for, taken for granted, care for*

---

### 3.5 SINCE vs FOR — Quick Comparison

| Feature | SINCE | FOR |
|---|---|---|
| Type of time | Definite (specific point) | Indefinite (period/span) |
| Can point on clock/calendar? | ✅ Yes | ❌ No |
| Examples | since Monday, since 5 PM, since 2020 | for 3 days, for years, for a long time |
| Tense used with | Present Perfect / Past Perfect Continuous | Present Perfect / Past Perfect Continuous |

---

### 3.6 FROM

- **Meaning:** Shows **origin or source** — where something starts, comes from, or originates.
- **NOT used for time duration** in the way "since/for" are.

**Examples:**
- *I am coming from Agra.*
- *The Ganga flows from the Himalayas.*
- *This gift is from my mother.*

---

### 3.7 DURING

- **Meaning:** Happening **within** or **throughout** a specific period of time.
- **Examples:**
  - *She fell asleep during the lecture.*
  - *It rained during the night.*

---

### 3.8 IN / ON / AT (Time Prepositions — Quick Reference)

| Preposition | Used for | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **IN** | Months, years, seasons, centuries, long periods | in January, in 2024, in summer, in the morning |
| **ON** | Specific days and dates | on Monday, on 5th March, on Christmas Day |
| **AT** | Specific times, holidays (as a whole), short/precise periods | at 6 PM, at noon, at midnight, at Christmas |

> ❌ Do NOT use "in" with "last month/year/week" — *She joined **last month*** (no preposition needed).

---

### 3.9 WITH vs BY (Tool/Instrument)

- **WITH:** Used when referring to a **tool, instrument, or weapon** a person uses.
  - *She wrote with a Parker pen.*
  - *He hit him with a stick.*
  - *I am teaching with a pen.*
- **BY:** Used for transportation, mode, or large machinery.
  - *Most guests arrived **by** bus.*
  - *She traveled **by** train.*

---

### 3.10 OF

- **Meaning:** Shows **composition, belonging, or what something is made of**.
- **Examples:**
  - *The letter was full **of** mistakes.*
  - *This is made **of** wood.*
  - *A glass of water.*

---

### 3.11 Preposition + Adjective Collocations (Common Exam Patterns)

| Expression | Correct Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| indebted | **to** | I am indebted **to** you. |
| full | **of** | The bag is full **of** books. |
| aware | **of** | Be aware **of** the change. |
| similar | **to** | Her ideas are similar **to** mine. |
| different | **from** | This is different **from** that. |
| disqualified | **from** | He was disqualified **from** holding the post. |
| succeed | **in** | I hope you succeed **in** getting the job. |
| affinity | **with/for** | I feel no affinity **with** his style. |
| indistinguishable | **from** | The copy is indistinguishable **from** the original. |

---

### 3.12 Transport Prepositions

| Situation | Preposition |
|---|---|
| By vehicle (bus, car, train, plane) | **by** |
| Using a tool/instrument | **with** |
| Arriving at a destination | **at** (small places), **in** (cities/countries) |

## 4. Parts of Speech

### 4.1 Noun

- **Definition:** The **name** of any person, place, thing, animal, idea, or feeling.
- **Examples:** *Radha, Delhi, book, student, happiness, team*
- **Rule:** Every named entity in the world is a noun.

---

### 4.2 Pronoun

- **Definition:** A word used **in place of a noun** to avoid repetition.
- **Examples:** *he, she, it, they, we, I, you, him, her, them, us*

**Why we use them:** Instead of saying "Rohan went to market. Rohan bought apples. Rohan came home." we say "Rohan went to market. **He** bought apples. **He** came home."

---

### 4.3 Verb

- **Definition:** A word that shows **action or state of being**.
- **Examples:** *run, eat, think, love, is, are, was, have*

---

### 4.4 Adjective

- **Definition:** A word that **describes or modifies a noun or pronoun** — tells what kind, how many, or which one.
- **Examples:** *tall, small, red, intelligent, beautiful, bright, weak*

**Sentence example:** *The **bright** light hurt his eyes.* → "bright" describes the noun "light" → **Adjective**

---

### 4.5 Degrees of Adjective

Every adjective has three forms (degrees) used for comparison:

| Degree | Formation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Positive** | Base form — no comparison | weak, smart, kind |
| **Comparative** | -er (short adj.) / more (long adj.) | weaker, smarter, more beautiful |
| **Superlative** | -est (short adj.) / most (long adj.) | weakest, smartest, most beautiful |

**Short adjectives** (1-2 syllables): add **-er / -est**
- *weak → weaker → weakest*
- *smart → smarter → smartest*
- *strong → stronger → strongest*

**Long adjectives** (3+ syllables or hard to say with -er): use **more / most**
- *beautiful → more beautiful → most beautiful*
- *intelligent → more intelligent → most intelligent*

> **Trick:** If the word is long and saying "-er" at the end makes it hard to pronounce, use "more/most."

---

### 4.6 Adverb

- **Definition:** A word that **modifies a verb, adjective, or another adverb** — tells *how*, *when*, *where*, or *how much* an action is done.
- **Examples:** *quickly, gently, slowly, always, never, very, here, there*

**Sentence example:** *He spoke to us **gently**.* → "gently" modifies the verb "spoke" — tells **how** he spoke → **Adverb**

> **Adverbs often end in "-ly"** (but not always).

---

### 4.7 Adjective vs Adverb — Distinction

| Feature | Adjective | Adverb |
|---|---|---|
| Modifies | Noun / Pronoun | Verb / Adjective / Another Adverb |
| Question it answers | What kind? Which? How many? | How? When? Where? How much? |
| Example | *bright* light | spoke *gently* |

---

### 4.8 Parts of Speech — Quick Identification Strategy (for MCQs)

1. **Is it a name of something?** → Noun
2. **Is it replacing a name?** → Pronoun
3. **Is it showing an action or state?** → Verb
4. **Is it describing a noun?** → Adjective
5. **Is it describing a verb/adjective/adverb?** → Adverb

## 5. Tenses — Error Detection Strategy

### 5.1 Present Simple (Indefinite)

- **Use:** Habitual actions, routines, universal truths, facts.
- **Signal words:** *always, usually, often, every day, generally, sometimes, never, regularly*
- **Formula (Positive):** Subject + V1 (+ s/es for He/She/It)
- **Formula (Negative):** Subject + do/does + not + V1
- **Formula (Question):** Do/Does + Subject + V1?

**Example:** *She plays with her dog every day.* ✓

---

### 5.2 Present Continuous

- **Use:** Actions happening right now, at the time of speaking.
- **Signal words:** *now, at the moment, currently, at present, look!, listen!*
- **Formula:** Subject + is/am/are + V-ing

---

### 5.3 Present Perfect

- **Use:** Actions completed recently, with present relevance; experiences; actions with *just, already, yet, ever, never*.
- **Signal words:** *just, already, yet, ever, never, recently, lately*
- **Formula:** Subject + has/have + V3 (Past Participle)

> ❌ Do NOT use "yesterday" or other specific past time words with Present Perfect.
> - ❌ *The committee has met yesterday.*
> - ✅ *The committee met yesterday.* (Simple Past)

---

### 5.4 Present Perfect Continuous

- **Use:** Actions that started in the past and are still continuing.
- **Signal words:** *since* (+ specific time), *for* (+ period), *how long*
- **Formula:** Subject + has/have + been + V-ing

**Example:** *She has been working here for five years.*

---

### 5.5 Simple Past (Past Indefinite)

- **Use:** Completed actions at a specific time in the past.
- **Signal words:** *yesterday, last week/month/year, ago, in [past year], then*
- **Formula:** Subject + V2 (Past form)

---

### 5.6 Past Perfect

- **Use:** When two things happened in the past, the one that happened **first** uses Past Perfect.
- **Formula:** Subject + had + V3

**Example:** *When I reached the station, the train had already left.*
→ First: train left (Past Perfect) | Second: I reached (Simple Past)

---

### 5.7 Tense Signal Word Reference Table

| Signal Word | Tense |
|---|---|
| yesterday, ago, last... | Simple Past |
| always, every day, usually | Present Simple |
| now, at the moment | Present Continuous |
| just, already, yet, ever | Present Perfect |
| since [specific time], for [period] | Present Perfect Continuous |
| when [past] + [earlier past] | Past Perfect |
| tomorrow, next... | Future Simple |

---

### 5.8 Error Detection — Tense-Based Errors

**Key Strategy:** Look for **hint words** in the sentence that tell you which tense should be used. Then check if the verb matches that tense.

**Common Errors:**
1. Using Present Perfect with specific past time → Should be Simple Past
   - ❌ *The committee **has met** yesterday.*
   - ✅ *The committee **met** yesterday.*

2. Using wrong helping verb with plural/singular
   - ❌ *She **don't** like cricket.* (She is singular → needs **doesn't**)
   - ✅ *She **doesn't** like cricket.*

3. Using "have" with "each/every" (which are singular)
   - ❌ *Each of the students **have** submitted.*
   - ✅ *Each of the students **has** submitted.*

## 6. Subject-Verb Agreement

### 6.1 Core Rule

- **Singular subject → Singular verb** (is, was, has, does, V+s/es)
- **Plural subject → Plural verb** (are, were, have, do, V without s/es)

---

### 6.2 Each / Every

> **Rule:** "Each" and "Every" are **always singular** — always use a singular verb.

- ❌ *Each of the students **have** submitted.*
- ✅ *Each of the students **has** submitted.*
- ✅ *Every student **is** present.*

**When to use Each vs Every:**
- **Each** → Individual members of a group, one by one (individualistic)
- **Every** → Refers to all members of a group collectively

---

### 6.3 None / Neither (when used alone)

> **Rule:** "None" and "Neither" (when alone, without "nor") take a **singular verb**.

- ✅ *None of the answers **is** correct.*
- ✅ *Neither of the students **has** come.*
- ❌ *Neither of the answers **are** correct.*

---

### 6.4 Neither…Nor / Either…Or

> **Rule (Proximity Rule):** The verb agrees with the **noun closest to it** (the noun just before the verb).

| Structure | Closest Noun | Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Neither A nor **B** (singular) | Singular | Singular verb |
| Neither A nor **B** (plural) | Plural | Plural verb |
| Either A or **B** | Depends on B | Match B |

**Examples:**
- *Neither the professor nor the **students** **were** able to attend.* → "students" is plural → "were" ✓
- *Neither the students nor the **teacher** **was** able to attend.* → "teacher" is singular → "was" ✓
- *Neither the professor nor the **student** **is** coming.* → "student" is singular → "is" ✓

---

### 6.5 One of / One of the

> **Rule:** After "one of," the noun must always be **plural**.

- ❌ *He is one of my friend.*
- ✅ *He is one of my **friends**.*
- ✅ *She is one of those rare **individuals** who...*

**The verb in the relative clause** matches the plural noun, not "one":
- *She is one of those rare individuals **who go** out of their way to help.* ✓
- ❌ *...who goes* (Don't match with "one" — match with "individuals")

---

### 6.6 Collective Nouns

- Usually take **singular** verb in formal/standard usage.
- But if the group is divided in opinion/action → plural may be acceptable.
- *The jury **was** divided in their opinions.* (each member had different opinion — plural accepted)

---

### 6.7 "Who" in Relative Clauses — Agreement

**Key rule:** The verb in a relative clause starting with "who" should agree with the **antecedent** (the noun "who" refers to).

- *She is one of those rare individuals **who go** out of their way.* → "who" refers to "individuals" (plural) → verb is plural "go" ✓

## 7. Articles (A / An / The)

### 7.1 "A" — Indefinite Article

- Used before a **consonant sound**.
- Refers to **any one** thing of a kind (non-specific, general).
- **Examples:**
  - *She is **a** teacher.* (any teacher, not a specific one)
  - *It is **a** very hot day.*
  - *I have **a** book.*

---

### 7.2 "An" — Indefinite Article

- Used before a **vowel sound** (not necessarily a vowel letter).
- **Test:** Say the word aloud — does it start with an **A, E, I, O, U sound**?

**Examples where the letter and sound align:**
- *an apple, an egg, an island, an orange, an umbrella*

**Examples where the letter and sound differ (tricky cases):**

| Word | Sound starts with | Article |
|---|---|---|
| MBBS | "em" sound (vowel) | **an** MBBS doctor |
| hour | "ow" sound (vowel, 'h' is silent) | **an** hour |
| honest | "on" sound (vowel) | **an** honest man |
| university | "yu" sound (consonant "y") | **a** university |
| unique | "yu" sound (consonant "y") | **a** unique idea |
| European | "yu" sound | **a** European country |
| one | "wun" sound (consonant "w") | **a** one-eyed man |

> **Golden Rule:** It's about the **SOUND** not the **SPELLING**.

---

### 7.3 "The" — Definite Article

Used when:

1. **The noun has been mentioned before** (reader already knows which one)
   - *I saw a dog. **The** dog was barking.*
2. **There is only one** of its kind
   - ***The** sun, **the** moon, **the** earth, **the** sky*
3. **Names of rivers, mountain ranges, oceans, seas**
   - ***The** Ganga, **the** Himalayas, **the** Pacific Ocean*
4. **Names of newspapers**
   - ***The** Times of India, **the** Hindu*
5. **Names of groups of countries (unions, states)**
   - ***The** USA, **the** UAE, **the** United Kingdom*
6. **Historical monuments**
   - ***The** Taj Mahal, **the** Eiffel Tower*
7. **Religious books**
   - ***The** Bible, **the** Quran, **the** Gita*
8. **Superlatives**
   - *She is **the** best student.*

---

### 7.4 When NOT to Use "The"

- Before **names of people** → ❌ *the Ravi* (wrong) | ✅ *Ravi*
- Before **names of subjects** → ❌ *the Mathematics* | ✅ *Mathematics*
- Before **names of languages** → ❌ *the English* | ✅ *English*
- Before **names of countries** (single, not a union) → ❌ *the India* | ✅ *India*
- Before **names of continents** → ❌ *the Asia* | ✅ *Asia*
- Before **meals** → ❌ *the breakfast* | ✅ *breakfast*

---

### 7.5 Time Expressions with Articles

- *in **the** morning / in **the** afternoon / in **the** evening*
- *at night* (no article)
- *The sun did not rise till six in **the** morning.* ✓

## 8. Active and Passive Voice

### 8.1 Active vs Passive — Concept

- **Active Voice:** The subject **does** the action.
  - *She wrote the letter.*
- **Passive Voice:** The subject **receives** the action.
  - *The letter was written by her.*

---

### 8.2 Formula for Passive Voice

> **Passive = Object (of active) + Helping Verb (be form) + V3 (Past Participle) + by + Subject (of active)**

The main verb **always** becomes **V3 (past participle)** in passive voice.

---

### 8.3 Tense-wise Passive Conversion

| Tense | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Simple Present | She writes letters. | Letters **are written** by her. |
| Simple Past | She wrote a letter. | A letter **was written** by her. |
| Present Continuous | She is writing a letter. | A letter **is being written** by her. |
| Past Continuous | She was writing a letter. | A letter **was being written** by her. |
| Present Perfect | She has written a letter. | A letter **has been written** by her. |
| Past Perfect | She had written a letter. | A letter **had been written** by her. |
| Simple Future | She will write a letter. | A letter **will be written** by her. |

---

### 8.4 Key Checks for Passive Voice MCQs

**Two things to verify:**
1. **Object of the active sentence comes first** (becomes the subject in passive).
2. **Main verb is in V3 (past participle) form.**

**Additional checks:**
- Singular subject (object of active) → singular be-verb (is/was)
- Plural subject → plural be-verb (are/were)

**Example:**
- Active: *Careless driving causes many accidents.*
- Passive: *Many accidents **are caused** by careless driving.* ✓
- ❌ *Many accidents is caused...* (accidents = plural → are, not is)

---

### 8.5 Converting Active to Passive — Step by Step

1. Identify: **Subject | Verb | Object** in active sentence
2. Move the **Object to the front** (it becomes new subject)
3. Add the correct **be-verb** (matching tense and number)
4. Write the **V3** form of the main verb
5. Add **"by" + original subject** at the end (optional in some cases)

---

### 8.6 Passive with Continuous Tenses

- Present Continuous Passive: *is/am/are + **being** + V3*
- Past Continuous Passive: *was/were + **being** + V3*

### 8.7 Passive with Perfect Tenses

- Present Perfect Passive: *has/have + **been** + V3*
- Past Perfect Passive: *had + **been** + V3*

## 9. Modal Verbs

Modals are special auxiliary verbs that express **mood, possibility, ability, permission, obligation, or advice**.

> **Key grammar rule:** After a modal verb, always use **V1 (base form)** — never V2, V3, or V-ing directly.

---

### 9.1 All Modal Verbs and Their Uses

| Modal | Primary Use | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Can** | Ability (present) | I **can** speak English. |
| **Could** | Ability (past) / Possibility | She **could** run fast when young. |
| **May** | Permission (formal) / Possibility | **May** I come in? |
| **Might** | Possibility (less certain) / Suggestion | It **might** rain today. |
| **Will** | Future / Willingness | I **will** help you. |
| **Would** | Polite request / Hypothetical / Past habit | **Would** you like some tea? |
| **Shall** | Future (formal) / Suggestions (I/We) | **Shall** we begin? |
| **Should** | Advice / Recommendation | You **should** rest. |
| **Must** | Obligation / Strong necessity / Logical deduction | You **must** wear a seatbelt. |
| **Ought to** | Moral obligation / Ideal behavior | You **ought to** respect elders. |
| **Need** | Necessity | You **need** not worry. |
| **Used to** | Past habit / Past state (no longer true) | I **used to** walk every morning. |
| **Dare** | Challenge / Courage | He **dare** not speak. |

---

### 9.2 Detailed Notes on Key Modals

#### CAN
- Present ability: *I **can** swim.*
- Informal permission: ***Can** I use your phone?*

#### COULD
- Past ability: *She **could** speak three languages.*
- Polite request: ***Could** you help me?*
- Possibility: *It **could** be true.*

#### MAY
- Formal permission: ***May** I enter?*
- Possibility: *It **may** rain.*

#### MIGHT
- Less certain possibility: *He **might** come late.*
- Suggestion (polite): *You **might** want to reconsider.*

#### WILL
- Future: *I **will** call you.*
- Willingness: *I **will** do it.*

#### WOULD
- Polite requests: ***Would** you please sit down?*
- Hypothetical situations: *I **would** buy a house if I had money.*
- Past habits (in narration): *She **would** sing every evening.*
- Desire/Preference: *I **would** like some water.*

#### SHALL
- First person future: *I **shall** return.*
- Suggestions with "we": ***Shall** we go?*

#### SHOULD
- Advice: *You **should** exercise daily.*
- Expectation: *She **should** be here by now.*

#### MUST
- Strong obligation: *You **must** submit the form today.*
- Logical deduction: *He **must** be tired — he's been working all day.*

> **Must vs Should:** Both can give advice, but **must** is stronger — it signals there is no real alternative. **Should** is a recommendation.

#### OUGHT TO
- Moral duty/ideal behavior: *We **ought to** respect our parents.*
- Like "should" but more formal.

#### USED TO
- Past habit or state that no longer exists:
  - *I **used to** play cricket when I was in college.*
  - *She **used to** live in Delhi.* (but doesn't anymore)

---

### 9.3 Modals — Exam Quick Reference

**Identifying modals:** *can, could, may, might, will, would, shall, should, must, ought to, need, used to, dare*

**Spotting modal errors:**
- Modal + V3 (past participle) = always wrong in simple usage → correct to Modal + V1
- "Does/Do" rules don't apply to modals — modals don't change for singular/plural

---

## 10. Modal Verbs in Past Tense

### 10.1 Concept — Modals Expressing Regret or Unrealized Actions

When modals are used to talk about the past — especially expressing regret, missed opportunities, or criticism about past actions — a special structure is used:

> **Formula: Modal Verb (past form) + HAVE + V3**

This expresses: "This should/could/would have happened — but it didn't."

---

### 10.2 Structures

| Modal | Past Structure | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| should | should + have + V3 | Was obligated to do (but didn't) |
| could | could + have + V3 | Was able to do (but didn't) |
| would | would + have + V3 | Was willing/likely to do (but didn't) |
| must | must + have + V3 | Was logically necessary (deduction about past) |
| may | may + have + V3 | Possibly happened in the past |
| might | might + have + V3 | Possibly happened (less certain) |

---

### 10.3 Examples

- *You **should have stood** by your brother.* (You had that obligation but didn't do it.)
- *I **could have done** better.* (I had the ability but didn't use it.)
- *She **would have come**.* (She was willing, but something stopped her.)
- *He **must have been** tired.* (Logical deduction about his past state.)
- *You **could have wished** me.* (It was possible — but you didn't.)
- *I **could have loved** you.* (Possibility that was never realized.)

---

### 10.4 How to Identify Modal Past in Questions

**Signal:** A V3 (past participle) appears right after a modal — which normally doesn't happen in simple usage.

- *You ___ stood by your brother.* → "stood" is V3 → This is a modal-past question → Answer: **should have** ✓

---

### 10.5 Passive Voice with Modal Past

Structure: **Modal + have + been + V3**

- *He could have been saved.* (It was possible to save him — but it didn't happen.)
- *The work should have been done.* (It should have been completed — but wasn't.)

---

## 11. Conditional Sentences

Conditional sentences express **"if this, then that"** relationships.

---

### 11.1 Zero Conditional (General Truth)

- **Use:** Universal truths, scientific facts — always true.
- **Formula:** If + Present Simple, Present Simple
- **Example:** *If you heat ice, it melts.*

---

### 11.2 First Conditional (Real Possibility)

- **Use:** Realistic future possibilities.
- **Formula:** If + Present Simple, will + V1
- **Example:** *If it rains, we will stay inside.*

---

### 11.3 Second Conditional (Unreal / Hypothetical Present)

- **Use:** Imaginary or unlikely present/future situations.
- **Formula:** If + Past Simple (V2), would/could/might + V1
- **Key:** Use **"were"** instead of "was" for all subjects (formal/subjunctive)

**Examples:**
- *If I **had** money, I **would buy** a new car.*
- *If I **were** rich, I **would travel** the world.*
- *If I **were** you, I **would** not do that.*

> ❌ *If I have money, I would buy a car.* (wrong — present tense in "if" clause)
> ✅ *If I **had** money, I **would buy** a car.*

---

### 11.4 Third Conditional (Unreal Past — Regret)

- **Use:** Imagining a different past — something did NOT happen; expressing regret.
- **Formula:** If + Past Perfect (had + V3), would/could/might + have + V3
- **Examples:**
  - *If I **had known** about the meeting earlier, I **would have attended** it.*
  - *If she **had studied** harder, she **would have passed** the exam.*
  - *If he **had come** to me, I **would have helped** him.*

---

### 11.5 Inverted Conditionals (No "if" — Formal)

Sometimes the "if" is dropped and the sentence is **inverted**:

| Normal Form | Inverted Form |
|---|---|
| If he had come | **Had he come** |
| If I had known | **Had I known** |
| If she had worked | **Had she worked** |

**Example:**
- *Had I known about the meeting earlier, I would have attended it.* ✓

> This is frequently tested! When you see "Had + Subject + V3" at the beginning → it's a Third Conditional.

---

### 11.6 Quick Summary Table

| Type | Condition Clause | Result Clause | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| Zero | Present Simple | Present Simple | Always-true facts |
| First | Present Simple | will + V1 | Real future possibility |
| Second | Past Simple / were | would/could + V1 | Unreal present |
| Third | Past Perfect (had + V3) | would/could + have + V3 | Unreal past / regret |

---

## 12. Clauses (Noun / Adjective / Adverb)

### 12.1 What is a Clause?

A **clause** is a group of words that contains a **subject** and a **verb**. Unlike a complete sentence, a clause may not express a complete thought on its own.

---

### 12.2 Noun Clause

- **Definition:** A clause that acts as a **noun** in a sentence — it functions as the subject or object.
- **Key signals:** *that, who, what, which, whether, if, how, when, where, why*

**How to identify:** Can you replace this clause with a pronoun (it, something, that)? If yes → Noun Clause.

**Examples:**
- *I want to know **who came here**.* → "who came here" is the object → Noun Clause
- *Please tell me **who came to meet me**.* → "who came to meet me" = object → Noun Clause
- *I think **there must be something wrong**.* → Noun Clause
- ***What she said** surprised everyone.* → "What she said" is the subject → Noun Clause

---

### 12.3 Adjective Clause (Relative Clause)

- **Definition:** A clause that **modifies (describes) a noun or pronoun** — gives information about who, which, when, where.
- **Key signals:** *who, whom, whose, which, that, where, when*

**How to identify:** Does the clause describe or give more detail about a noun that came before it? If yes → Adjective Clause.

**Examples:**
- *The boy **who came here** is my brother.* → "who came here" describes "the boy" → Adjective Clause
- *I know the place **where they will come**.* → "where they will come" describes "place" → Adjective Clause
- *I remember the day **when we met** for the first time.* → "when we met" describes "day" → Adjective Clause
- *I don't know **whose books are kept here**.* → "whose books are kept here" → Adjective Clause

---

### 12.4 Adverb Clause

- **Definition:** A clause that **modifies a verb, adjective, or adverb** — tells when, where, why, how, or under what condition the action happens.
- **Key signals:** *because, since, as, although, though, when, while, if, unless, until, so that, even though, before, after*

**How to identify:** Does the clause answer "when? / why? / how? / under what condition?" about the main action? If yes → Adverb Clause.

**Examples:**
- *I missed the flight **as I was late**.* → "as I was late" explains why → Adverb Clause
- *I had left the room **when my mother came to meet me**.* → "when my mother came" tells when → Adverb Clause
- ***Since it was raining**, we stayed inside.* → Adverb Clause of reason

---

### 12.5 Clause Comparison Table

| Clause Type | Function | Key Words | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Noun Clause** | Subject or Object | that, who, what, whether, how | *I know **what you did**.* |
| **Adjective Clause** | Modifies a Noun | who, which, that, where, when, whose | *The book **that I read** was great.* |
| **Adverb Clause** | Modifies a Verb/Adjective | because, when, if, although, since, until | *She left **because she was tired**.* |

---

### 12.6 Exam Strategy for Clause Questions

1. First, try to identify **Adjective Clauses** — they're easiest to spot (describe nouns; often use who/which/where/when right after a noun).
2. Then, look for **Noun Clauses** — check if the clause is acting as subject or object.
3. Whatever is left is likely an **Adverb Clause**.
4. Use the process of elimination across the options.

## 13. Neither…Nor / Either…Or Agreement

### 13.1 Rule — Proximity (Nearest Noun) Rule

When **neither…nor** or **either…or** connects two subjects, the verb agrees with the **subject closest to the verb** (the second subject).

> **Formula:** Neither A nor **B** + Verb matching **B**

---

### 13.2 Examples

- *Neither the teacher nor the **students were** present.* → "students" is plural → "were" ✓
- *Neither the students nor the **teacher was** present.* → "teacher" is singular → "was" ✓
- *Either the manager or the **employees are** responsible.* → "employees" plural → "are" ✓
- *Either the employees or the **manager is** responsible.* → "manager" singular → "is" ✓

---

### 13.3 Neither (Alone — Without Nor)

When "neither" is used **alone** (not with "nor"), it is treated as **singular**.

- ✅ *Neither of the answers **is** correct.*
- ❌ *Neither of the answers **are** correct.*
- ✅ *Neither of the students **has** come.*

---

### 13.4 Neither…Nor vs Neither (Alone) — Quick Reference

| Structure | Rule | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Neither…nor + [noun A] nor [noun B] | Verb matches noun B (proximity) | Neither A nor **B** → match B |
| Neither (alone) | Always singular | Neither **is** correct |
| Either…or + [noun A] or [noun B] | Verb matches noun B (proximity) | Either A or **B** → match B |

---

## 14. Exclamatory Sentences

### 14.1 What are Exclamatory Sentences?

Sentences that express **strong emotion** — surprise, shock, joy, sorrow, admiration, disgust.

They end with an **exclamation mark (!)**.

---

### 14.2 How to Form Exclamatory Sentences

**Pattern 1: What + a/an + Adjective + Noun + Subject + Verb!**
- *What a wonderful woman she is!*
- *What a terrible accident it was!*

**Pattern 2: How + Adjective/Adverb + Subject + Verb!**
- *How beautiful she looks!*
- *How fast he runs!*

---

### 14.3 Exclamatory Words and Their Emotional Context

| Word | Emotion |
|---|---|
| **Hurray / Hooray** | Joy, celebration |
| **Alas** | Sorrow, regret, misfortune |
| **Oh / Ah** | Surprise, sudden realization |
| **Bravo** | Admiration, praise |
| **What / How** | Used to form exclamatory sentences |

---

### 14.4 Converting Assertive to Exclamatory

- *He met with an accident and died.* (sorrow) → *Alas! He met with an accident and died.* ✓
- *She is a wonderful woman.* (admiration) → *What a wonderful woman she is!* ✓

## 15. Error Detection — Master Strategy

### 15.1 The 6-Point Checklist

When facing an error detection question, check the sentence in this **sequence**:

---

**Step 1 — Subject-Verb Agreement**
- Does the subject (singular/plural) match the verb?
- ❌ *Each of the students **have** submitted.* → Each = singular → should be "has"
- This alone catches **50%+ of errors** in error detection questions.

---

**Step 2 — Tense**
- What is the tense signal word? (yesterday, always, since, just, etc.)
- Does the verb match the expected tense?
- ❌ *The committee **has met** yesterday.* → "yesterday" = Simple Past → should be "met"

---

**Step 3 — Preposition**
- Is the correct preposition used?
- Check common collocations: *indebted to, aware of, full of, different from, succeed in*
- ❌ *I am indebted **for** you.* → should be "indebted **to** you"

---

**Step 4 — Articles (A / An / The)**
- If an article is present, is it correctly used?
- Check vowel sounds for A/An.
- Check if "the" is needed or misused.

---

**Step 5 — Modals**
- Is the modal verb correctly matched with V1 (base form)?
- Or if past modal: Modal + have + V3?

---

**Step 6 — Gerund / Infinitive**
- Is the verb form after another verb correct?
- Some verbs take gerund (V-ing), others take infinitive (to + V1).
- A bare verb form (without to/ing) where one is required = error.

---

### 15.2 Elimination Method

If you cannot find the error directly:
- Go through each underlined part.
- Mark the ones that seem correct using your checklist.
- **The part that remains = the error.**

---

### 15.3 Long Sentences — Special Tip

> **When a sentence is very long, the error is almost always simple.**

Examiners make sentences long to intimidate and distract. They load the sentence with difficult vocabulary, but the actual error is usually a basic subject-verb agreement or tense error. **Don't panic — look for the basics.**

---

### 15.4 "No Error" Option

- Opt for "No Error" only when you've genuinely checked all six categories and found nothing wrong.
- Be careful — students often mark "No Error" when they're unsure. That's a mistake.

## 16. Exam Strategy & Tips

### 16.1 How to Approach MCQ Questions in Grammar

1. **Read the sentence first**, not the options.
2. Predict what kind of answer is needed.
3. Then look at options and **eliminate** wrong ones.
4. In most grammar MCQs, you can eliminate 2-3 options immediately using one rule.
5. The process of elimination is faster and more reliable than trying to construct the answer from scratch.

---

### 16.2 Direct & Indirect Speech — Exam Shortcut

1. Scan options: has the tense been shifted to past? → Eliminate if not.
2. Have time words (tomorrow, now, this) been changed? → Eliminate if not.
3. Is there a question mark where it shouldn't be (in indirect speech)? → Eliminate.
4. What remains = your answer.

---

### 16.3 Passive Voice — Exam Shortcut

1. Does the object of the active sentence come first? → Eliminate if not.
2. Is the main verb in V3 form? → Eliminate if not.
3. Is the be-verb (is/are/was/were) singular/plural correctly matching the new subject?

---

### 16.4 Match the Following — Strategy

For vocabulary match-the-following or sentence-completion matching:

1. Find **one sentence you are 100% sure about** — any one.
2. Pick its answer.
3. Look which options contain that pairing.
4. From the remaining options, verify the others.
5. This shortcut eliminates half the options immediately.

---

### 16.5 Vocabulary (Synonyms/Antonyms) Tips

- Vocabulary questions have the highest frequency in CUET English.
- There is no shortcut — the more words you know, the better.
- Even if you don't know all 4 options, eliminate the ones you *do* know are wrong.
- Probability and elimination work: even 2 known words narrow your answer significantly.
- Keep expanding vocabulary — no upper limit. The more you read, the more you recognize.

---

### 16.6 Reading Comprehension Tips

- RC is entirely based on **common sense and understanding** — not memorization.
- Don't panic; read the passage once calmly.
- Most answers are directly stated or closely implied in the passage.
- Watch the dedicated RC strategy video for approach.
- Vocabulary strength directly improves RC performance.

---

### 16.7 Time Management in Exam

- **Don't give equal time to all questions.**
- Fast questions (direct/indirect, simple error detection) → do quickly, save time.
- Time-consuming questions (match the following, RC) → allocate more time.
- If a question is confusing, use elimination and move on.
- Don't panic if two papers are back-to-back — all students face the same condition.

---

### 16.8 The Day Before the Exam

- **Do not panic.** Panic has ruined more exams than lack of preparation.
- Review the most-missed vocabulary words.
- Go through your mock test errors.
- Keep your mind calm — eat well, rest.
- Trust your preparation.

---

### 16.9 Mindset for the Exam

- Don't compare with others — your score is your own.
- Not everyone will get into top colleges; that doesn't mean your future is decided.
- Every college, every opportunity can be used to build a great career if you work hard.
- Don't take unnecessary drops — each attempt teaches you something.
- Focus on what's in your control: your preparation and your effort on the exam day.

| Rule | Quick Summary |
|---|---|
| Each / Every | Always singular — use has, is, does |
| Neither (alone) | Singular — use is, has |
| Neither…nor / Either…or | Match verb to closest noun (proximity rule) |
| One of | Noun after "one of" must be plural |
| Subject in relative clause | Match verb to the antecedent of "who/which" |
| Modal + V form | Modal + V1 (base form) |
| Modal (past regret) | Modal + have + V3 |
| Passive voice | Object first + be-verb + V3 |
| Direct → Indirect | Tense shifts to past; time words change |
| Third Conditional | If + had + V3, would + have + V3 |
| Inverted Conditional | Had + Subject + V3 (no "if") |
| A vs An | Depends on SOUND (not spelling) |
| The | Used for specific, unique, previously mentioned nouns |
| Since vs For | Since = definite time point; For = duration/period |
| By vs Until | By = deadline; Until = duration up to a point |
| With vs By | With = tool/instrument; By = transport/doer |
