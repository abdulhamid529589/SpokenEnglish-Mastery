## 2. What Is a Conjunction?

A **conjunction** is a **connecting word** that joins two words, phrases, clauses or sentences.

| Two separate sentences | Joined with a conjunction |
|---|---|
| She is my sister. I love her. | She is my sister **and** I love her. |
| I like him. My family does not like him. | I like him **but** my family does not like him. |

The word comes from *conjoin* = to join. Conjunctions make language flow and show the *relationship* between the two ideas (addition, contrast, reason, condition, time, choice).

## 3. The Golden Method for MCQs

Many conjunction options can make a grammatically correct sentence. The examiner wants the **one that fits the meaning best**. Do **not** translate word-for-word into Hindi. Instead:

1. **Read both parts** and ask: what is the relationship between them?
   - Same direction / addition → **and**
   - Opposite / unexpected → **but, although, though, however, yet, even though**
   - Reason → **because, since, as, so, therefore**
   - Condition → **if, unless, once, provided**
   - Time → **when, while, until, before, after, as, once, since**
   - Choice → **either…or, neither…nor, whether…or**
2. **Give yourself a reason** for the option you choose and be able to say why each of the other three fails.
3. **Check position:** is the blank at the **start** or **middle** of the sentence?
4. If two options both work, choose the **more natural / more powerful / more formal** one (e.g. *even though* over *though*; *although* at the start).

## 4. Conjunctions by Purpose

### 4.1 Addition (joining similar ideas)

| Conjunction | বাংলা | Use | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **and** | এবং / আর | Joins similar items or events in sequence | She cooked dinner and washed the dishes. |
| **as well as** | ...এর পাশাপাশি | "in addition to" | She is smart as well as kind. |
| **not only … but also** | শুধু ... নয়, ...ও | Emphasis on both qualities/actions | She is not only smart but also kind. |
| **both … and** | উভয় ... এবং | Two things together | He is both clever and hard-working. |
| **then** | তারপর | Next step in a sequence | We had dinner, then watched a movie. |

*Note (from class):* with **not only … but also**, when the first part begins the sentence a helping verb is used: *Not only **did he win** the race but he also broke a record.* In a plain sentence there is no "did": *She is not only smart but also kind.*

### 4.2 Contrast / Opposite / Unexpected

| Conjunction | বাংলা | Use | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **but** | কিন্তু | Simple contrast, in the **middle** of a sentence | She was tired but she kept working. |
| **although** | যদিও | Contrast, usually at the **start** | Although she was tired, she kept working. |
| **though** | যদিও / তবুও | Like *although*, more informal; can come mid-sentence or at the end | The house was not beautiful; I liked the garden though. |
| **even though** | যদিও (জোরালো) | More **powerful** and **formal** than *though* | Even though he spoke loudly, no one heard him. |
| **however** | তবে / যাই হোক | Contrast; starts a sentence (followed by a comma) or sits mid-sentence between commas | I was late. However, I completed my work. |
| **yet** | তবুও | Contrast or surprise ("still") | We visited the museum yet we didn't have much time. |
| **while / whereas** | অথচ / যেখানে | Contrast between two facts | He is rich while his brother is poor. |
| **instead (of)** | পরিবর্তে | Something opposite happened | She did not complain; instead she looked cheerful. |
| **in spite of / despite / inspite of** | সত্ত্বেও | Followed by a noun/noun-phrase | He could not get the job in spite of his qualification. |
| **rather than** | ...এর চেয়ে বরং | Preference / comparison | I would prefer tea rather than coffee. |

**Important placement rule from class:** *although* and *but* don't go together in the same sentence. If you start with *although*, do not use *but* in the second half.

### 4.3 Reason / Cause

| Conjunction | বাংলা | Use | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **because** | কারণ / কেননা | Gives the reason; usually mid-sentence | We stayed inside because it was raining. |
| **since** | যেহেতু | Reason at the **start** of the sentence | Since I was unwell, I did not get ready. |
| **as** | যেহেতু | Reason; usually at the start | As he was not well, I did not disturb him. |
| **so** | তাই | Result, mid-sentence | I was ill, so I did not go to work. |
| **therefore / thus / consequently** | সুতরাং | Formal result | He missed the bus; therefore he was late. |
| **so that** | যাতে | Purpose ("in order that") | Sanjeev stayed at work so that he could complete the work. |
| **so … that** | এত ... যে | Result of a degree | It was so cold that we could not go out. |

### 4.4 Condition

| Conjunction | বাংলা | Use | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **if** | যদি | Possible/future condition; often at the start | If you leave now, you will be on time. |
| **unless** | যদি না | Negative condition; carries its own "not" | You will fail unless you study. |
| **once** | একবার / যেইমাত্র | As soon as / when a condition is met | We will start once everyone is here. |
| **provided (that)** | শর্তে যে | Only on condition that | You may go provided you finish your work. |
| **even if** | এমনকি যদি | Strong condition | Even if you apologise, she won't forgive you. |

### 4.5 Time

| Conjunction | বাংলা | Use | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **when** | যখন | Time of an event | We will eat when they arrive. |
| **while** | যখন / সময়ে | An action going on at the same time | She was talking on the phone while cooking dinner. |
| **as** | যখন | One event happens while another is in progress | I was sleeping as the phone rang. |
| **until / till** | পর্যন্ত | Time limit up to which something continues | You must wait here until I return. |
| **before** | আগে | Earlier than | Finish this before you go. |
| **after** | পরে | Later than | We left after the movie ended. |
| **since** | থেকে | A definite starting point (with a fixed time) | She has been visiting the bank daily since last Monday. |
| **as soon as** | যেইমাত্র | Immediately after | Call me as soon as you reach. |

### 4.6 Choice / Alternatives (two options)

| Conjunction | বাংলা | Example Sentence |
|---|---|---|
| **either … or** | হয় ... নয়তো | You can either stay here or leave. |
| **neither … nor** | না ... না | Neither he nor his brother came. |
| **whether … or** | কিনা | I didn't know whether she was serious or joking. |
| **or** | অথবা | Do you want tea or coffee? |
| **otherwise / or else** | নাহলে | Hurry up, otherwise you will miss the train. |

## 5. Placement Rules (Start vs Middle)

| Conjunction | Usually appears | Note |
|---|---|---|
| although, even though, since, as, if, once | **Start** of the sentence (also possible mid-sentence) | *Although* + subject + verb, main clause |
| but, because, so, and | **Middle** | Joins two full clauses |
| however | **Start** (with comma) or mid-sentence between commas | Never joins two clauses without punctuation |
| unless | Commonly mid-sentence | Can begin a sentence too: *Unless you apologise, she won't forgive you.* |
| though | Middle or end | More informal than *although* |
| until | Middle | Time limit |

**Fill-in-the-blank tip from class:** *although* and *but* would both make the sentence meaningful, but if the blank is right at the **start**, choose **although**; if it is in the **middle**, choose **but**.

## 6. Tricky Pairs Explained

| Pair | How to tell them apart |
|---|---|
| **If vs Unless** | *If* → both parts point in the **same** direction (positive result). *Unless* → the second part is the **opposite** of what happens when the condition is not met. *Unless you practise, you will fail* = *If you don't practise, you will fail.* Because *unless* already means "if not", don't add "not" after it. |
| **Since vs For** | *Since* + definite starting point (last Monday, 2010). *For* + duration (two hours). |
| **Since (reason) vs Because** | *Since* is placed at the start of the sentence; *because* in the middle. |
| **While vs As** | *While* → one action goes on **while** another is also happening. *As* → a **new event** interrupts an action already in progress (*I was sleeping as the phone rang*). |
| **Though / Although vs Even though** | *Even though* is stronger and more formal. |
| **Yet vs Though** | *Yet* also shows surprise ("still"): *We visited the museum yet we had little time.* |
| **Once vs If** | *Once* = as soon as a condition is fulfilled: *Once you finish, you may go.* |
| **Instead vs Instead of** | *Instead* stands alone: *She didn't complain; instead she looked cheerful.* *Instead of* is followed by a noun. |
| **Either/Neither vs None** | *Either/neither* → **two** options; *none* → more than two. |
| **As well as vs Not only … but also** | Both add; *not only … but also* adds emphasis. |
| **As if / As though** | To show something that is **not real** or only pretended: *He acted as if he knew everything.* |

## 8. Quick Revision Sheet

| Relationship | Use |
|---|---|
| Addition | and, as well as, both…and, not only…but also, then |
| Contrast | but, although, though, even though, however, yet, whereas, instead, in spite of, rather than |
| Reason | because, since, as, so, therefore, so that |
| Condition | if, unless, once, provided that, even if |
| Time | when, while, as, until, before, after, since, as soon as |
| Choice | either…or, neither…nor, whether…or, or, otherwise |

**Five-second method:** (1) What is the link — addition, contrast, reason, condition, time or choice? (2) Start or middle? (3) Which option gives a **logical** meaning, not just a grammatical one? Give yourself a reason for your answer and be ready to say why the other three fail.

**Remember:**
- *Although/since/as/if* → usually **start**; *but/because/so* → **middle**.
- *Unless* = *if … not*; never add another "not".
- *Neither/either* → two options; *none* → more than two.
- *Even though* > *though* in strength and formality.
