## 🔑 The Big Idea: Why Do Tenses Exist?

Every sentence in English has two things that need to be shown:

| Component | What it tells you | How it's shown |
|---|---|---|
| **TIME** | When did the action happen? (Present / Past / Future) | **Helping Verb** |
| **WORK** | How much of the action happened? (Ongoing / Complete / Routine) | **Form of the Main Verb** |

> **Tenses = Time + Work**

This is why tenses were created — to show *when* something happened and *how much* of it happened, using helping verbs and verb forms.

---

## 🔢 The Three Main Times

Every sentence belongs to one of three time categories:

| Time | Meaning | Clue Words |
|---|---|---|
| **Present** | The action is happening now / is a habit | today, usually, always, everyday |
| **Past** | The action happened before | yesterday, two days ago, last week |
| **Future** | The action will happen later | tomorrow, next week, by next month |

---

## 🎭 Forms of the Main Verb (V1 / V2 / V3)

Every verb has **three forms**. These tell you *how much* work was done.

| Form | Name | What it shows | Example (Go) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **V1** | First Form (Base) | Routine / Ongoing action | Go |
| **V2** | Second Form | Past Indefinite only | Went |
| **V3** | Third Form | Completed (Perfect) action | Gone |
| **V1 + ING** | — | Continuous / ongoing action | Going |

### ✅ Key Memory Trick
- **V1 + ING** → shows **continuity** (something is in progress)
- **V3 (Past Participle)** → shows **perfection** (something is complete)
- **V2** → belongs *only* to **Past Indefinite affirmative** — it has no other home

---

## ⚙️ Helping Verbs and What They Show

Helping verbs tell you **which time (tense)** the sentence belongs to.

### Present Tense Helping Verbs
| Helping Verb | Tense |
|---|---|
| do / does | Present Indefinite |
| is / am / are | Present Continuous |
| has / have | Present Perfect |
| has been / have been | Present Perfect Continuous |

### Past Tense Helping Verbs
| Helping Verb | Tense |
|---|---|
| did | Past Indefinite (negative/question) |
| was / were | Past Continuous |
| had | Past Perfect |
| had been | Past Perfect Continuous |

### Future Tense Helping Verbs
| Helping Verb | Tense |
|---|---|
| will / shall | Future Indefinite |
| will be / shall be | Future Continuous |
| will have / shall have | Future Perfect |
| will have been / shall have been | Future Perfect Continuous |

## 📋 Complete Tense Chart

### PRESENT TENSES

#### 1. Present Indefinite (Simple Present)
**Used for:** Habits, routines, schedules, news commentary, universal truths

| Sentence Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + V1 (+s/es) + Object | She speaks English. |
| Negative | Subject + do/does + not + V1 + Object | She does not speak English. |
| Interrogative | Do/Does + Subject + V1 + Object? | Does she speak English? |

**⚠️ Exam Trap:** If the subject is singular (he/she/it/name), add **-s or -es** to the main verb.
- ✅ *She speaks* — NOT *She speak*
- ✅ *She does not speak* — NOT *She does not speaks* (does already carries the -s)

**Hint words:** usually, always, generally, everyday, regularly, often, sometimes

---

#### 2. Present Continuous
**Used for:** Actions happening right now

| Sentence Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + is/am/are + V1+ing + Object | She is cooking food. |
| Negative | Subject + is/am/are + not + V1+ing | She is not cooking. |
| Interrogative | Is/Am/Are + Subject + V1+ing? | Is she cooking? |

**Key:** is → singular, are → plural, am → I only

---

#### 3. Present Perfect
**Used for:** Actions completed at an unspecified time before now

| Sentence Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + has/have + V3 + Object | She has completed her homework. |
| Negative | Subject + has/have + not + V3 | She has not completed her homework. |
| Interrogative | Has/Have + Subject + V3? | Has she completed her homework? |

**Key:** has → singular, have → plural (and I)

---

#### 4. Present Perfect Continuous ⭐ (Most asked in exams)
**Used for:** Actions that started at a specific time in the past and are still continuing

| Sentence Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + has/have + been + V1+ing + since/for + time | He has been learning his lesson for two hours. |
| Negative | Subject + has/have + not + been + V1+ing | She has not been working since morning. |
| Interrogative | Has/Have + Subject + been + V1+ing? | Has she been working since morning? |

---

### PAST TENSES

#### 5. Past Indefinite (Simple Past) ⭐ (Most used in daily life)
**Used for:** Events and actions that happened at a specific time in the past

| Sentence Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + V2 + Object | I went to Agra. |
| Negative | Subject + did + not + V1 + Object | I did not go to Agra. |
| Interrogative | Did + Subject + V1 + Object? | Did you go to Agra? |

**⚠️ Exam Trap — Negative:** *Did* is already V2 of *do*, so the main verb goes back to V1.
- ✅ *She did not eat* — NOT *She did not ate*

**Hint words:** yesterday, last week, two days ago, last Friday, ago (any past time reference)

---

#### 6. Past Continuous
**Used for:** Actions that were happening at a particular time in the past

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + was/were + V1+ing + Object | I was eating food. |

was → singular, were → plural

---

#### 7. Past Perfect ⭐ (Combo with Past Indefinite)
**Used for:** An action completed *before* another past action

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + had + V3 + Object | The train had left before I arrived. |

**⭐ Exam Pattern — Past Perfect + Past Indefinite Combo:**
> When two past actions are described together, the earlier one uses **Past Perfect (had + V3)** and the later one uses **Past Indefinite (V2)**.

- *The train **had left** when I **arrived**.*
- *Rohan **had watched** the movie before he **read** the review.*
- *I **had already eaten** before she **came**.*

Keywords that signal this combo: **before, after, when, by the time**

---

#### 8. Past Perfect Continuous ⭐
**Used for:** Actions that were continuously happening before a point in the past; always includes a time duration

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + had + been + V1+ing + since/for + time | I had been waiting for you for two hours. |
| Subject + had + not + been + V1+ing + object | My brother had not been playing since evening. |
| Wh word + had + sub (not) + been + V1+ing + object | Since when had the monkeys been climbing the tree |

---

### FUTURE TENSES

#### 9. Future Indefinite (Simple Future)
**Used for:** Actions that will happen in the future

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + will/shall + V1 + Object | She will go to the market. |
| Will + Subject + V1 + Object | Will he come today? |
| Wh word + will + sub + v1 + Object | What will rohan eat? |

- When he comes back, he will return your money (do not use *will* two time in this situation like "when i will come back, I will return your money")

**Will vs. Shall:**
- Traditionally: **shall** with I/We, **will** with all others
- In modern usage: **will** is used for all persons
- **Rules reverse** when showing promise, threat, or determination:
  - *I will do this.* (promise/determination — will with I)

---

#### 10. Future Continuous
| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + will be/shall be + V1+ing | He will be waiting for me. |

---

#### 11. Future Perfect ⭐
**Used for:** An action that will be completed by a certain point in the future

| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + will have/shall have + V3 | We shall have completed the project by next month. |

**Hint word:** by (deadline signal — *by next month*, *by tomorrow*)

---

#### 12. Future Perfect Continuous
| Structure | Example |
|---|---|
| Subject + will have been/shall have been + V1+ing | They will have been working for hours by then. |

---

## 🔍 SINCE vs. FOR — The Most Tested Rule

This appears in every exam. Know the difference well.

| Word | When to use | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **SINCE** | When the starting point is a **definite, specific time** visible on a clock or calendar | since 8 PM, since Monday, since 1996, since morning, since childhood |
| **FOR** | When a **duration** (unspecified period) is given | for two hours, for many days, for a long time, for three months |

**Test yourself:**
- *I have been studying _____ morning.* → **since** (morning = specific time)
- *I have been studying _____ three hours.* → **for** (three hours = duration)
- *She has been here _____ January.* → **since** (January = specific)
- *She has been here _____ a month.* → **for** (a month = duration)

## 🎯 Exam Strategy: How to Find Tense Errors

### Step 1 — Find the Hint Word
Every tense question has a hint hidden in the sentence. Find it first.

| Hint Word | Tells You |
|---|---|
| yesterday, ago, last week | → **Past Indefinite** |
| usually, always, everyday | → **Present Indefinite** |
| since + specific time | → **Perfect Continuous** |
| for + duration | → **Perfect Continuous** |
| before, after, when (two past actions) | → **Past Perfect + Past Indefinite combo** |
| by next month / by evening | → **Future Perfect** |

### Step 2 — Check the Previous Sentence
If the previous sentence is in the **past**, the next sentence should also follow past tense. Never randomly switch tenses unless a hint word tells you to.

### Step 3 — Check Singular/Plural Agreement
| Subject | Helping Verb |
|---|---|
| He / She / It / Name | has, is, does, was |
| I / We / You / They | have, are, do, were |
| I (special case) | have, am, was |

### Step 4 — Check V1 / V2 / V3 Form
Look at the main verb. Ask yourself:
- Is this **V1+ing**? → Continuous tense
- Is this **V3**? → Perfect tense
- Is this **V2**? → Past Indefinite affirmative
- Is this **V1**? → Present Indefinite, or after did/do/does/will/shall

## ✅ Most Important Exam Points (Quick Revision)

### Present Indefinite
- Singular subject + **V1+s/es**
- After do/does (negative/question) → always **V1** (no -s/-es again)
- Hint words: usually, always, everyday

### Past Indefinite
- Affirmative → **V2** only
- Negative/Question → **did + V1** (V2 never repeats after did)
- Hint words: yesterday, last week, ago

### Perfect Continuous (Any)
- Must have a **time reference** (since/for)
- has been / have been → Present Perfect Continuous
- had been → Past Perfect Continuous
- will have been → Future Perfect Continuous

### Past Perfect Combo
- **had + V3** for the earlier action
- **V2** for the later action
- Triggered by: before, after, when, by the time

### Has vs. Have
- Singular subject → **has**
- Plural subject / I → **have**

## 📝 Practice Question Types (PYQ Examples)

**Q1:** She _____ with her dog everyday. *(plays / played / has been playing)*
→ "everyday" = routine = **Present Indefinite** → *plays* ✅

**Q2:** The war _____ in 1934. *(will start / is starting / started)*
→ Historical event = **Past Indefinite** → *started* ✅

**Q3:** I am tired as I _____ since 7 o'clock in the morning. *(have been working / had been working)*
→ "since" + "I am" (present) = **Present Perfect Continuous** → *have been working* ✅

**Q4:** As the Rath Yatra festival approaches, the number of pilgrims in Puri _____. *(is increasing / will be increasing)*
→ "approaches" = present indefinite → next clause also present → *is increasing* ✅

**Q5:** Rohan _____ the movie before he read the review. *(watches / had watched / has watched)*
→ "read" = past; action before it = **Past Perfect** → *had watched* ✅

**Q6:** I _____ this book since morning. *(have been reading / had been reading)*
→ "since morning" = specific time; current reading = **Present Perfect Continuous** → *have been reading* ✅

**Q7:** By the next month, we shall _____ the project. *(complete / have completed / completing)*
→ "by next month" = deadline = **Future Perfect** → *have completed* ✅

**Q8:** He _____ in the States but he still does not have a command over the English language.
→ "does not have" = Present; "States" with continuity = **has been living** ✅

## 🧠 Deep Dive: Each Tense — Full Explanation

### 1. Present Indefinite — The Habit Tense

This is the most commonly asked tense in CUET and competitive exams.

**When exactly do we use it?**
- Daily habits and routines: *I wake up at 6 AM.*
- General truths and facts: *The sun rises in the east.*
- Scheduled/timetabled events: *The train leaves at 9 PM.*
- News commentary and live narration: *He passes the ball to Rohit, who shoots!*
- Instructions: *You add salt and stir.*

**Complete Structure Table:**

| Type | Singular Subject | Plural / I / You / We / They |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | He **reads** books. | They **read** books. |
| Negative | He **does not read** books. | They **do not read** books. |
| Question | **Does** he **read** books? | **Do** they **read** books? |
| WH Question | Why **does** he **read** books? | Why **do** they **read** books? |

**The S/ES Rule — Detailed:**

Add **-s** to most verbs: *eat → eats, run → runs, speak → speaks*
Add **-es** to verbs ending in -s, -sh, -ch, -x, -o: *go → goes, watch → watches, wash → washes*
Verbs ending in consonant + y: change y → ies: *study → studies, fly → flies*

**Why no -s/-es after does/do?**
Because *does* is already the modified form of *do* (it already carries the singular marker). Adding -es again to the main verb would be double-marking. So after *does/do*, the main verb always stays in **V1**.
- ✅ *She does not go* (go = V1)
- ❌ *She does not goes* (wrong — double marking)

---

### 2. Present Continuous — The "Right Now" Tense

**When exactly do we use it?**
- Actions happening at the moment of speaking: *I am writing this note.*
- Temporary actions happening around now: *She is staying at a hotel this week.*
- Planned future arrangements (informal): *I am meeting him tomorrow.*
- Annoying repeated habits (with always): *He is always interrupting me!*

**Complete Structure:**

| Type | I | He/She/It/Name | We/You/They |
|---|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | I **am** working. | She **is** working. | They **are** working. |
| Negative | I **am not** working. | She **is not** working. | They **are not** working. |
| Question | **Am** I working? | **Is** she working? | **Are** they working? |

**The ING Rule:**
- Most verbs: simply add -ing → *eat → eating, go → going*
- Verbs ending in -e: drop the e, add -ing → *write → writing, take → taking*
- Short verbs ending in consonant-vowel-consonant: double the last consonant → *run → running, sit → sitting, swim → swimming*
- Verbs ending in -ie: change ie → y, add -ing → *lie → lying, die → dying*

**Stative Verbs — Never use in Continuous:**
Some verbs describe states, not actions, and are NOT used with -ing:
*know, believe, understand, like, love, hate, want, need, prefer, seem, appear, belong, contain, mean, own, possess*
- ❌ *I am knowing the answer.* → ✅ *I know the answer.*
- ❌ *She is loving him.* → ✅ *She loves him.*

---

### 3. Present Perfect — The "Just Completed" Tense

**When exactly do we use it?**
- Actions completed at an unspecified time before now: *I have visited Delhi.*
- Recent actions with present relevance: *She has just finished her work.*
- Life experiences (ever/never): *Have you ever eaten sushi?*
- Actions starting in the past that are still ongoing (with since/for — though this usually triggers Perfect Continuous): *I have lived here for ten years.*
- With words like *already, yet, just, recently, ever, never*

**Complete Structure:**

| Type | Singular Subject | Plural / I |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | He **has done** his work. | They **have done** their work. |
| Negative | He **has not done** his work. | They **have not done** their work. |
| Question | **Has** he **done** his work? | **Have** they **done** their work? |

**Key Signal Words:** just, already, yet, recently, ever, never, so far, till now, up to now

**Has Been vs. Have Been (Special Use):**
Apart from Perfect Continuous, *has been / have been* is also used to describe a state that has existed:
- *He has been a great friend of mine.* (= He has been / remained a great friend)
- *It has been a wonderful time with you.*
- *She has been living in the States.* (= She has been a resident — continuity sense)

---

### 4. Present Perfect Continuous — The "Still Going On Since" Tense

**When exactly do we use it?**
- An action that started at a specific point in the past and is still continuing NOW
- Always paired with *since* (specific time) or *for* (duration)

**Complete Structure:**

| Type | Singular | Plural / I |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | She **has been working** since morning. | They **have been working** for hours. |
| Negative | She **has not been working** since morning. | They **have not been working** for hours. |
| Question | **Has** she **been working** since morning? | **Have** they **been working** for hours? |

**The Since / For Rule (Detailed):**

| SINCE — Definite / Specific Time | FOR — Indefinite / Duration |
|---|---|
| since 8 PM | for two hours |
| since Monday | for many days |
| since January | for a long time |
| since 1996 | for several months |
| since morning | for ages |
| since childhood | for a while |
| since last year | for three years |

**How to decide:** Ask yourself — *Can I see this time on a clock or calendar?*
- "8 PM" → yes, clock shows it → **since**
- "two hours" → no, which two hours? → **for**
- "morning" → yes, it's a named time → **since**
- "long time" → no, vague → **for**

### 5. Past Indefinite — The Storytelling Tense

This is the most used tense in everyday conversation and storytelling.

**When exactly do we use it?**
- Completed actions at a definite past time: *I went to Agra yesterday.*
- A series of past events (narrating a story): *She woke up, got dressed, and left.*
- Past habits (with used to or would — though Past Indefinite also works): *He walked to school every day.*

**Complete Structure:**

| Type | All Subjects (Same Form) |
|---|---|
| Affirmative | She **went** to school. |
| Negative | She **did not go** to school. |
| Question | **Did** she **go** to school? |
| WH Question | Why **did** she **go** to school? |

- V1 is used everywhere (present, after do/does/did/will/shall)

### 6. Past Continuous — The "Was Happening" Tense

**When exactly do we use it?**
- Action in progress at a specific past time: *At 8 PM, I was cooking dinner.*
- Background action when another action interrupted it: *I was reading when she called.*
- Two parallel past actions: *While he was cooking, she was studying.*

**Complete Structure:**

| Subject | Helping Verb | Example |
|---|---|---|
| I / He / She / It / Name | was | She **was reading** a book. |
| We / You / They | were | They **were playing** cricket. |

---

### 7. Past Perfect — The "Had Already Done" Tense

**When exactly do we use it?**
- To show which of two past actions happened first
- Always used with Past Indefinite in the same sentence
- The action that happened EARLIER uses had + V3

**Complete Structure:**

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + had + V3 | She **had completed** her work. |
| Negative | Subject + had + not + V3 | She **had not completed** her work. |
| Question | **Had** + Subject + V3? | **Had** she **completed** her work? |

**The Before/After Rule:**

When *before* connects two clauses:
- The action in the *before* clause happened SECOND (use Past Indefinite)
- The other clause happened FIRST (use Past Perfect)

> *She had eaten [first] before he came [second].*
> *He had left [first] before I arrived [second].*

When *after* connects two clauses:
- The action in the *after* clause happened FIRST (use Past Perfect)
- The other clause happened SECOND (use Past Indefinite)

> *After she had finished [first] her work, she went [second] out.*

**When + Two Past Actions:**
The action already completed at the time of the other = Past Perfect

> *When I reached the station [Past Indefinite], the train had already left [Past Perfect].*

---

### 8. Past Perfect Continuous

**When exactly do we use it?**
- A past continuous action that was happening for a period before another past point
- Always includes a time duration (since/for)

**Structure:** Subject + had + been + V1+ing + since/for + time

Examples:
- *I had been waiting for you for two hours when you finally arrived.*
- *She had been studying since morning before the lights went out.*
- *They had been playing cricket for an hour when it started to rain.*

---

### 9. Future Indefinite

**Will vs. Shall:**

| Traditional Rule | Modern Usage |
|---|---|
| shall → I / We | will → all persons (most common today) |
| will → He/She/It/They/You | shall → I/We (formal/legal documents) |

**When rules REVERSE (Emphatic Future):**
When expressing **promise, threat, determination, or willpower**, the rule flips:
- *I **will** do this.* (promise — will with I/We)
- *You **shall** pay for this.* (threat — shall with You/They)

**Complete Structure:**

| Type | Structure | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Affirmative | Subject + will/shall + V1 | She will go to the market. |
| Negative | Subject + will/shall + not + V1 | She will not go. |
| Question | Will/Shall + Subject + V1? | Will she go? |

---

### 10. Future Perfect

**When exactly do we use it?**
- An action that will be COMPLETED by a specific future time
- Key signal word: **by** (by tomorrow, by next month, by 5 PM, by then)

**Structure:** Subject + will have / shall have + V3

Examples:
- *By next month, we shall have completed the project.*
- *By the time you arrive, I will have finished cooking.*
- *By 2030, scientists will have found a cure.*

## 🔄 Tense Consistency — A Critical Exam Rule

Once a tense is established in a passage or sentence, all related clauses should follow the same tense unless a specific hint word changes it.

**Wrong:** The story tells us that the king was brave and he **protects** his people.
**Right:** The story tells us that the king was brave and he **protected** his people.

**Wrong:** She went to the market and **buys** vegetables.
**Right:** She went to the market and **bought** vegetables.

## 🔁 Has Been / Have Been — All Uses

This helping verb combination appears in three different contexts:

| Use | Example | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Present Perfect Continuous** | She has been working since morning. | She started working in the morning and is still working. |
| **State of being** | He has been a great teacher. | He has remained a great teacher (over time). |
| **Passive of Present Perfect** | The file has been submitted. | Someone submitted the file (passive — agent not mentioned). |

## 📊 Error Detection in Tenses — Master Framework

When you see an error detection question involving tenses, run through this mental checklist:

```
STEP 1: Find the HINT WORD
         ↓
         yesterday / ago      → Past Indefinite
         always / usually     → Present Indefinite  
         since + specific     → Perfect Continuous
         for + duration       → Perfect Continuous
         by + future time     → Future Perfect
         before / after       → Past Perfect combo

STEP 2: Check SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT
         ↓
         Singular → has, is, was, does + V1+s/es
         Plural   → have, are, were, do + V1

STEP 3: Check MAIN VERB FORM
         ↓
         V1+ing   → Continuous?  ✓ or ✗
         V3       → Perfect?     ✓ or ✗
         V2       → Past Indef.  ✓ or ✗
         V1       → after did/do/does/will? ✓ or ✗

STEP 4: Check DID/DOES rule
         ↓
         did + not + V1 (NOT V2)
         does + not + V1 (NOT V1+s/es)

STEP 5: Check SINCE vs. FOR
         ↓
         Clock/calendar time → since
         Period/duration     → for
```

## 🧩 Tricky Questions From Exams — With Explanations

**Q: Every boy and girl _____ in the class today. (is / are present)**
→ "Every" = singular verb → **is present** ✅
(Every boy and girl = treated as a singular unit)

**Q: 2/3 of the food supply _____ for the month. (has been used / have been used)**
→ "food supply" = singular → **has been used** ✅

**Q: As the Rath Yatra festival approaches, the number of pilgrims in Puri _____.**
→ "approaches" = Present Indefinite → next verb also Present → **is increasing** ✅

**Q: I did not know Rahul was in the hospital. If I _____, I _____ him.**
→ Conditional sentence (unreal past) → **If I had known, I would have visited** ✅

**Q: The war _____ in 1934.**
→ Historical event + specific year → **started** (Past Indefinite) ✅

**Q: 2 dozen _____ ₹100 according to the prevailing rate.**
→ "2 dozen" = plural → **cost** (Present Indefinite, plural) ✅

**Q: The council _____ its decision.**
→ "council" = singular + completed action → **has made** ✅

**Q: He _____ in the States but still does not have a command over English.**
→ "does not have" = present; continuous life experience → **has been living** ✅

## 💡 Real-Life Memory Anchors

The instructor used powerful real-life analogies to anchor these concepts:

**"Today's moments become tomorrow's memories"**
→ This is why *Present Indefinite* habits (I study daily) become *Past Indefinite* events (I studied) when you look back years later.

**"V2 is loyal to only one master"**
→ The second form of a verb (V2) is exclusively used in *Past Indefinite affirmative*. V1 goes everywhere, V3 has multiple uses — but V2 belongs only to Past Indefinite affirmative.

**"The helping verb shows the clock, the main verb shows the meter"**
→ Helping verb = what time is it? Main verb form = how much work is done?

**"Since = calendar / clock. For = vague duration"**
→ If you can point to it on a clock or calendar, use *since*. If it's a floating period of time, use *for*.

**"Longer sentence = simpler error in error detection"**
→ When an error detection sentence looks very long and scary, the actual grammatical mistake is almost always something basic — subject-verb agreement or wrong verb form.
