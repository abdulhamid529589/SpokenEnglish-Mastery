# Direct & Indirect Speech — Complete CUET Notes

---

## PART 1: FOUNDATION

### What Is Direct & Indirect Speech?

Every time someone speaks, there are two ways to report it:

**Direct Speech** — the exact words, inside quotation marks.
**Indirect Speech** — reporting what was said, no quotation marks, tense shifts.

Real-life example:
- Morning: X says to Y → "I am going to Delhi tonight."
- Night: Y tells Z → X said that he was going to Delhi that night.

Notice what changed:
- "I" became "he" (pronoun change)
- "am going" became "was going" (tense change)
- "tonight" became "that night" (time word change)
- Quotation marks removed
- "said" added before the reported clause

---

### Why Does Tense Change?

Because time has passed. When Y tells Z about X's words at night, X's statement (made in the morning) is now in the past. So everything shifts back in time. This is called **backshift**.

This logic is the foundation of the entire topic. Once you understand this, the rules feel natural rather than arbitrary.

---

## PART 2: THE EXAM TRICK

For MCQ-based exams like CUET, you do not need to memorize all rules mechanically.

**3-Step Method:**

**Step 1** — Identify the tense of the verb inside the Direct speech.
**Step 2** — Find the option where that tense has correctly shifted to past.
**Step 3** — Among matching options, check pronouns, time words, and sentence structure.

This eliminates 2–3 wrong options instantly without reading the entire sentence.

## PART 3: COMPLETE RULES

### Rule 1 — Tense Change Table

| Direct Speech Tense | Indirect Speech Tense | Example (Direct → Indirect) |
|---|---|---|
| Present Indefinite | Past Indefinite | "I go" → he went |
| Present Continuous | Past Continuous | "I am going" → he was going |
| Present Perfect | Past Perfect | "I have gone" → he had gone |
| Present Perfect Continuous | Past Perfect Continuous | "I have been going" → he had been going |
| Past Indefinite | Past Perfect | "I went" → he had gone |
| Past Continuous | Past Perfect Continuous | "I was going" → he had been going |
| Past Perfect | Past Perfect (no change) | "I had gone" → he had gone |
| Past Perfect Continuous | Past Perfect Continuous (no change) | "I had been going" → he had been going |
| Future Indefinite (will) | Would | "I will go" → he would go |
| Future Indefinite (shall) | Would / Should | "I shall go" → he would/should go |
| Can | Could | "I can do it" → he could do it |
| May | Might | "I may come" → he might come |
| Must | Had to / Must (for strong obligation) | "I must go" → he had to go |
| Need | Needed | "I need help" → he needed help |

**Memory shortcut:** Everything moves one step back into the past. Present → Past. Past → Past Perfect. Will → Would.

---

### Rule 2 — Pronoun Changes

Pronouns change based on who said what and who is being spoken to. Use the **1-2-3 rule**:

**1st person pronouns** (I, me, my, mine, we, us, our, ours) — change according to the **subject** of the reporting verb.

**2nd person pronouns** (you, your, yours) — change according to the **object** of the reporting verb.

**3rd person pronouns** (he, she, it, they, him, her, his, hers, its, their, them) — **no change**.

| Pronoun | Changes to |
|---|---|
| I | he / she (based on who spoke) |
| me | him / her |
| my | his / her |
| mine | his / hers |
| we | they |
| us | them |
| our | their |
| you | I / me / we (based on who was spoken to) |
| your | my / our |

**Worked examples:**

He said, "**I** am happy." → He said that **he** was happy.
*(I → he because the speaker is "he")*

She said to me, "**You** are late." → She told me that **I** was late.
*(you → I because the person spoken to is "me")*

He said to her, "**I** will help **you**." → He told her that **he** would help **her**.
*(I → he; you → her)*

They said, "**We** are leaving." → They said that **they** were leaving.
*(we → they)*

| Direct Speech | Indirect Speech |
|---|---|
| now | then |
| today | that day |
| tonight | that night |
| yesterday | the previous day / the day before |
| last night | the previous night |
| last week | the previous week |
| tomorrow | the next day / the following day |
| next week | the following week |
| next month | the following month |
| here | there |
| this | that |
| these | those |
| ago | before |

### Rule 4 — Reporting Verb Changes

The word "said" changes depending on the type of sentence:

| Sentence Type | Direct Reporting Verb | Indirect Reporting Verb |
|---|---|---|
| Statement | said / said to | said that / told + object + that |
| Question (WH) | said to | asked / inquired / wanted to know |
| Question (Yes/No) | said to | asked / inquired whether / if |
| Request | said to | requested ... to |
| Order / Command | said to | ordered / commanded ... to |
| Advice | said to | advised ... to |
| Suggestion | said to | suggested that ... should |
| Proposal | said to | proposed that ... should |
| Exclamation | said | exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise that |
| Wish / Blessing | said | wished / prayed / blessed that ... might |

---

### Rule 5 — Said vs. Told

This is a very common exam question.

**Said that** → used when the person being spoken to is NOT mentioned in the reporting clause.
> He said that he was tired.

**Told + object + that** → used when the person spoken to IS mentioned.
> He told me that he was tired.
> She told her friend that she would come.

Pattern:
- "He said to me..." → "He told me that..."
- "She said to him..." → "She told him that..."
- "He said..." (no object) → "He said that..."

## PART 4: TYPES OF SENTENCES — DETAILED

### Type 1: Assertive (Statement) Sentences

The most common type. Someone makes a statement.

**Structure (Direct → Indirect):**
Subject + said/said to + object + "Statement" → Subject + said/told + object + that + (tense shifted sentence)

**Examples:**

Direct: He said, "I am busy."
Indirect: He said that he was busy.

Direct: She said to me, "I have finished my work."
Indirect: She told me that she had finished her work.

Direct: The teacher said to the students, "You must work hard."
Indirect: The teacher told the students that they had to work hard.

Direct: He said, "I was sleeping when you called."
Indirect: He said that he had been sleeping when I called.

Direct: She said, "I will come tomorrow."
Indirect: She said that she would come the next day.

**Negative statements:**
Direct: He said, "I don't know the answer."
Indirect: He said that he didn't know the answer.

Direct: She said, "I have not eaten anything."
Indirect: She said that she had not eaten anything.

---

### Type 2: Interrogative (Question) Sentences

Two subtypes — WH questions and Yes/No questions.

**2A: WH Questions (What, Where, When, Why, Who, Which, How)**

Rules:
- Reporting verb: asked / inquired / wanted to know
- WH word stays
- Sentence becomes affirmative (Subject + Verb order, not Verb + Subject)
- Question mark removed
- "That" is NOT used after asked/inquired with WH words

Wrong: She asked me that where I was going?
Right: She asked me where I was going.

**Examples:**

Direct: He said to me, "Where are you going?"
Indirect: He asked me where I was going.

Direct: The teacher said to Sheila, "Why are you late today?"
Indirect: The teacher asked Sheila why she was late that day.

Direct: She said to him, "What have you done?"
Indirect: She asked him what he had done.

Direct: He said, "Who broke the window?"
Indirect: He asked who had broken the window.

Direct: My friend said to me, "Which play have you seen recently?"
Indirect: My friend asked me which play I had seen recently.

Direct: She said, "How long have you been waiting?"
Indirect: She asked how long I had been waiting.

**2B: Yes/No Questions**

Rules:
- Reporting verb: asked / inquired
- Add "if" or "whether" before the clause
- Sentence becomes affirmative
- Question mark removed

**Examples:**

Direct: She said to him, "Did you eat?"
Indirect: She asked him if he had eaten.

Direct: He said to me, "Will you lend me your book?"
Indirect: He asked me if I would lend him my book.

Direct: She said, "Are you feeling better now?"
Indirect: She asked if I was feeling better then.

Direct: He said to her, "Can you cook?"
Indirect: He asked her if she could cook.

Direct: The doctor said, "Have you taken your medicine?"
Indirect: The doctor asked if I had taken my medicine.

---

### Type 3: Imperative Sentences

Sentences that give orders, commands, requests, advice, or suggestions. These do NOT shift tense — instead the reporting verb tells you what kind of imperative it was.

**Structure:** Subject + [reporting verb] + object + to + V1 (base form)

**3A: Requests (Please / Kindly)**

Reporting verb: **requested ... to**

Direct: He said to me, "Please help me."
Indirect: He requested me to help him.

Direct: She said to him, "Please don't leave."
Indirect: She requested him not to leave.

Direct: He said, "Kindly sign this form."
Indirect: He requested me to sign that form.

**3B: Orders / Commands**

Reporting verb: **ordered / commanded ... to**

Direct: The officer said, "Stand at ease."
Indirect: The officer ordered them to stand at ease.

Direct: She said to me, "Get out of this room."
Indirect: She ordered me to get out of that room.

Direct: He said, "Don't make noise."
Indirect: He ordered us not to make noise.

**Note for negatives:** "Don't + V1" → "not to + V1"

**3C: Advice**

Reporting verb: **advised ... to**

Direct: The doctor said, "Take rest for a week."
Indirect: The doctor advised me to take rest for a week.

Direct: She said, "You should exercise daily."
Indirect: She advised me to exercise daily.

**3D: Suggestions (Let's / Let us)**

Reporting verb: **suggested that ... should**

Direct: He said, "Let's go for a walk."
Indirect: He suggested that they should go for a walk.

Direct: She said to her friend, "Let's go for a picnic."
Indirect: She suggested to her friend that they should go for a picnic.

**3E: Proposals**

Reporting verb: **proposed that ... should**

Direct: He said, "Let us wait for the results."
Indirect: He proposed that they should wait for the results.

**3F: Exclamations**

Reporting verb: **exclaimed with + emotion + that**

| Word in Direct | Emotion in Indirect |
|---|---|
| Hurrah / Hooray / Bravo | joy |
| Alas / Oh no | sorrow |
| Oh / Ah / What | surprise |
| What a / How | admiration |
| Shame / Fie | disgust |

Rule: The exclamation word itself disappears. Only the emotion is carried into the reporting verb.

Wrong: He exclaimed with alas that he had lost.
Right: He exclaimed with sorrow that he had lost.

Direct: She said, "Hurrah! We have won!"
Indirect: She exclaimed with joy that they had won.

Direct: He said, "Alas! I am ruined."
Indirect: He exclaimed with sorrow that he was ruined.

Direct: She said, "What a beautiful dress!"
Indirect: She exclaimed with admiration that it was a very beautiful dress.

Direct: He said, "How clever she is!"
Indirect: He exclaimed with admiration that she was very clever.

**3G: Requests in Literary Style (Beseeched / Implored / Pleaded)**

These mean "begged" — very strong requests.

Direct: He said, "Please forgive me."
Indirect: He implored / beseeched / pleaded with me to forgive him.

---

### Type 4: Optative Sentences (Wishes, Blessings, Curses, Prayers)

These express wishes, hopes, or prayers.

Reporting verb: **wished / prayed / blessed / cursed + that + subject + might + V1**

Direct: She said, "May you live long."
Indirect: She wished / prayed that I might live long.

Direct: He said, "May God bless you."
Indirect: He prayed that God might bless me.

Direct: She said, "May you rot in hell."
Indirect: She cursed that he might rot in hell.

Direct: He said, "I wish I were rich."
Indirect: He wished that he were rich.

## PART 5: SPECIAL RULES

### Special Rule 1 — Universal Facts

If the Direct speech states something that is always true (a scientific fact, proverb, religious truth, or law of nature), the tense does NOT change.

Direct: The teacher said, "The earth revolves around the sun."
Indirect: The teacher said that the earth revolves around the sun.

Direct: He said, "Water boils at 100 degrees."
Indirect: He said that water boils at 100 degrees.

Direct: The saint said, "God is great."
Indirect: The saint said that God is great.

Direct: She said, "Honesty is the best policy."
Indirect: She said that honesty is the best policy.

---

### Special Rule 2 — When Reporting Verb Is in Present/Future Tense

If the reporting verb (said, told, asked...) is in the present tense or future tense, the tense of the reported speech does NOT change.

Direct: He says, "I am happy." → He says that he is happy.
Direct: She will say, "I am tired." → She will say that she is tired.

*(Backshift only happens when the reporting verb is in past tense)*

---

### Special Rule 3 — Past Perfect Stays Unchanged

If the Direct speech is already in Past Perfect tense, it stays in Past Perfect in Indirect speech. It cannot go further back.

Direct: He said, "I had finished my work."
Indirect: He said that he had finished his work. *(no change)*

---

### Special Rule 4 — Conditional Sentences

If the Direct speech contains conditional sentences with "would," "could," "might," "should" — these do not change.

Direct: He said, "If I had money, I would buy a car."
Indirect: He said that if he had money, he would buy a car. *(no change in the conditional)*

---

### Special Rule 5 — Modal Verbs

| Direct (Modal) | Indirect (Modal) |
|---|---|
| will | would |
| shall | would / should |
| can | could |
| may | might |
| must | had to (or must, for permanent obligation) |
| need | needed |
| ought to | ought to (no change) |
| used to | used to (no change) |
| could | could (no change) |
| would | would (no change) |
| might | might (no change) |
| should | should (no change) |

## PART 6: COMMON EXAM TRAPS

### Trap 1 — Question Mark in Indirect
Wrong: She asked me where was I going?
Right: She asked me where I was going.

### Trap 2 — "That" with WH Questions
Wrong: She asked me that where I was going.
Right: She asked me where I was going.

### Trap 3 — Inverted Word Order in Indirect
Wrong: He asked me where was she going.
Right: He asked me where she was going.
*(In Indirect, WH clause becomes Subject + Verb, never Verb + Subject)*

### Trap 4 — "Did" + Main Verb Together
Wrong: She asked me did I had eaten.
Right: She asked me if I had eaten.
*(Did disappears; verb takes its past/past perfect form)*

### Trap 5 — Not Changing Time Words
Wrong: He said that he would come tomorrow.
Right: He said that he would come the next day.

### Trap 6 — Changing Universal Facts
Wrong: The teacher said that the sun rose in the east.
Right: The teacher said that the sun rises in the east.

### Trap 7 — Keeping Exclamation Words in Indirect
Wrong: He exclaimed with alas that he had failed.
Right: He exclaimed with sorrow that he had failed.

### Trap 8 — Wrong Pronoun
Wrong: She told me that I was happy. (when she said "I am happy")
Right: She told me that she was happy.

### Trap 9 — Wrong Said/Told Usage
Wrong: He said me that he was tired.
Right: He told me that he was tired. / He said that he was tired.
*("said" never takes a direct object — you cannot "say someone"; you "tell someone")*

### Trap 10 — "Let's" = Suggestion, Not Order
Wrong: He ordered that they should go for a walk.
Right: He suggested that they should go for a walk.
*(Let's always = suggest, never order)*

## PART 10: SUMMARY CARD

**5 Golden Rules:**
1. Tense shifts to past (Present → Past; Past → Past Perfect; Will → Would)
2. Imperative sentences — no tense change; use correct reporting verb + TO + V1
3. Questions — no question mark; affirmative order; if/whether for Yes/No questions
4. Universal facts — tense never changes
5. Said (no object) vs. Told + object — always check if person spoken to is mentioned

**Quick Reporting Verb Guide:**
- Statement → said / told
- WH Question → asked where/when/why/what/how
- Yes/No Question → asked if/whether
- Request → requested to
- Order → ordered to / commanded to
- Advice → advised to
- Suggestion → suggested that ... should
- Proposal → proposed that ... should
- Exclamation → exclaimed with joy/sorrow/surprise/admiration that
- Wish/Blessing → wished/prayed that ... might
