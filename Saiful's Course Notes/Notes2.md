# My Daily Routine — Presentation Script (v2)

_Tip: Don't read this word-for-word in class. Read it 3-4 times at home until you know the FLOW (not the exact sentences), then speak naturally, like you're just telling a friend about your day._

---

## Opening

Good morning everyone. Today I want to talk about something simple, but personal — my daily routine.

A little about me first — I recently completed (com-PLEET-id) my diploma in Computer Science, and right now I'm preparing (pri-PAIR-ing) for a developer job. So most of my day is actually built around learning, practicing, and slowly getting closer to that goal.

## Morning

My day usually starts with breakfast. After that, I like to walk around in our garden for a few minutes — honestly, it just helps me feel fresh (FRESH) before I sit down to work.

Then I open my laptop and get started. Right now, my main project is a multi-vendor (MUL-ti VEN-dor) e-commerce (ee-KOM-mers) website — it has multiple sellers, products, and orders, all working together, so it's a fairly complex system. Alongside (uh-LONG-side) that, I'm learning system design (SIS-tem di-ZINE), which, honestly, I find quite tough. But I think struggling with hard topics like this is just part of becoming a better developer.

## Mid-morning

After working for some time, I usually take a short break — maybe a YouTube podcast (POD-cast) or a movie, just something to relax my mind before going back to work.

## Afternoon

In the afternoon, I have a light snack and rest a bit. Then I move on to cybersecurity (CY-ber-si-KYOOR-i-tee) — I watch tutorials (TOO-tor-ee-uhls) and practice hands-on alongside them.

After lunch, I usually take a short nap, just to recharge (ree-CHARJ) before continuing the day.

## Evening

Later, I go out for a walk, mainly to refresh (ri-FRESH) my mind. In the evening, after a snack, I sit with a few non-academic (non-a-ka-DEM-ik) books — right now that includes my spoken English materials, since I'm attending this course.

After that, I go back to my laptop and continue working for a while.

## Weekly rhythm

It's not just the same thing every day, though. Every now and then, I go out with friends to different places, just to keep my mind fresh. And on Fridays, I take a proper rest day — I scroll through Facebook, watch funny videos, documentaries (dok-yoo-MEN-ta-rees), and interesting blogs on YouTube. It's my way of recharging before the week starts again.

## Closing

At night, after dinner, I spend a little time on my phone, and then I go to sleep.

If I'm honest, my day doesn't follow a strict clock — and that's something I'm still working on. But it does follow a rhythm: every day, I try to touch three things — my e-commerce project, system design, and cybersecurity practice. It's still a work in progress, but it's slowly shaping me into the developer I want to become.

Thank you.

## How to sound natural (not memorized)

- **Pause slightly** after each section (Morning, Afternoon, Evening) — like you're thinking of what comes next, not reciting.
- Use small natural fillers occasionally: _"So,"_ _"Actually,"_ _"Honestly,"_ _"You know,"_ — but don't overuse them.
- Vary your speed — slow down on important lines (your goal, the "work in progress" line), speed up slightly on small details (breakfast, snack).
- Look up from your notes on the **Opening** and **Closing** lines especially — those two matter most for impression.
- If you forget an exact sentence, just say the idea in simpler words. The teacher wants to hear you speak, not recall.
- The "honestly, I find it tough" and "still working on" lines are your strongest moments — say them slightly slower and more sincerely. That's what makes it feel real, not rehearsed.

## Handling questions in the middle (mid-presentation)

This is actually a good sign — it means the teacher is listening and testing your **spoken** fluency, not just your memory. Here's how to handle it:

- **Pause, don't panic.** Take one second before answering — it's normal, not weak.
- **Answer in 1-3 sentences**, then naturally return to your topic: _"...and after that, as I was saying, I go back to my project."_
- If you don't understand the question, it's completely fine to say: _"Sorry, could you repeat that, please?"_ — this itself is good spoken English, not a failure.
- If you don't know how to answer, don't go silent — say something like: _"That's a good question, let me think..."_ — buys you a second and sounds natural.

## Likely questions + answers

**Q: Why don't you have a fixed time for your routine?**
A: "Actually, I'm still working on that. Right now I follow whatever feels productive in the moment, but I know a fixed schedule would help me focus better — it's something I'm trying to improve."

**Q: What is a multi-vendor e-commerce website?**
A: "It's an online shopping website where multiple sellers can list and sell their own products, instead of just one company selling everything — like how Daraz or Amazon works."

**Q: Why do you find system design tough?**
A: "Because it's not just about writing code — it's about planning how a large application should be structured so it can handle many users and stay reliable. It needs a different way of thinking, which I'm still developing."

**Q: What is backend development?**
A: "It's the part of a website that users don't see — the server, database, and logic that handle data and make everything work behind the scenes."

**Q: Why are you learning cybersecurity if you're a developer?**
A: "Because I believe a good developer should also understand security — how to protect an application from attacks. It also helps me write safer code from the start."

**Q: How do you manage your time between so many things — projects, cybersecurity, and now spoken English?**
A: "Honestly, it's a challenge. I don't follow strict hours, I just try to give time to each area every day, even if it's a small amount. It's not perfect, but I'm improving."

**Q: What do you want to become in the future / what's your goal?**
A: "My goal is to become a job-ready developer, and eventually grow into someone who understands both development and systems deeply — not just writing code, but understanding how everything works together."

**Q: Do you rest on weekdays too, or only Friday?**
A: "Mostly just Friday. On other days I try to keep working, but I do take small breaks, like watching a podcast or a short video, to keep my mind fresh."

**Q: Why are you learning spoken English now?**
A: "Because good communication is just as important as technical skill, especially for interviews and working with a team. I want to be confident when I speak, not just when I write code."

## Tip for unexpected questions

If the teacher asks something totally unrelated (like "what did you eat for breakfast?" or "what's your favorite subject?"), don't overthink it — these are usually just fluency checks, not knowledge checks. Answer honestly, in simple sentences, and smile. That's exactly what they want to see.
