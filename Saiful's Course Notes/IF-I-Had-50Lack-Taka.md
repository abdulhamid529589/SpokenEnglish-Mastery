# If I Had 50 Lakh Taka — Presentation Script
### 5–8 Minutes | Fun, Punchy, Natural | Simple Words, Big Personality

*Don't memorize sentences. Memorize the STORY. Then just tell it like you're talking to a friend.*

---

## Opening — Hook First, Introduce Second

Good morning, everyone.

Today I am going to talk about something that I think everyone in this room has thought about at least once — what would you do if you suddenly had a lot of money, and you had to spend all of it in just three days?

Three days ago, in my head, I became a millionaire. Well — a lakhpati, to be exact. Fifty lakh taka, sitting right in front of me, with one strange condition — spend all of it in just three days, or it disappears forever.

*(pause)*

For the first hour, I felt like the luckiest person alive. I was already imagining myself walking around like a king, nodding at people, maybe wearing sunglasses indoors for no reason.

*(small smile)*

By the second hour... I checked my imaginary pocket. Empty. Already. In my head, I had somehow spent 50 lakh taka faster than I finish a plate of biryani.

*(pause, look at audience)*

So I had to stop, take a breath, and actually think like an engineer instead of a Bollywood hero throwing money in the air. I built a real plan. A proper, three-day plan — because apparently, even my imaginary fortune needed a project timeline.

Let me take you through it.

---

## Day 1 — Building My Entire World
### Part One: The Setup. The Dream. The Lab.

Okay. So before I tell you about Day 1, I need to give you a little background — because without the background, you will not feel how much Day 1 matters to me.

I am someone who works with computers. Not just using computers — I mean building things with computers. Websites, systems, apps. If you have ever used a website to order food, buy something, or check information — someone like me built that. That is what I do, and what I am training myself to become better at, every single day.

Now. Imagine a painter who loves painting more than anything. His dream. His passion. His whole life.

But he only has one broken brush.

*(pause)*

That is me. Right now. Every day.

I have a basic laptop that tries its best — God bless it. A basic desk. And a chair that, I believe, was designed by someone who has never sat down in their life. Every single day, my back and that chair have a disagreement. And every single day — my back loses.

*(pause, look around)*

So. Day 1 — I fix ALL of that.

First — a powerful desktop computer. Think of it like upgrading from a small kitchen stove to a full professional chef's kitchen. Same idea, completely different experience. Everything becomes faster, smoother, more powerful. Things that used to take 10 minutes now take 10 seconds.

Then — not one screen, but multiple screens side by side. Because when you are working on something complex, having one screen is like trying to cook a big meal with one tiny plate. You keep moving things around, losing track, starting over. With multiple screens, everything is visible at once. It changes the way you think.

A desk that I can raise or lower whenever I want — so I can sit when I feel relaxed, and stand when I need to think hard. And the chair. Oh, the chair. The kind that has been designed specifically for people who sit for long hours — comfortable, supportive, perfect. My back has been waiting for this chair for years. Day 1 is for you, back.

*(small smile)*

Now — the part I am most excited about. I want to build what I call a smart lab. Here is the simplest way I can explain it.

You know how in movies, someone walks into a room and says — "lights on," and the lights turn on? Or their front door unlocks automatically when they arrive home? Or their air conditioner starts running ten minutes before they wake up, so the room is already perfect?

That is not science fiction anymore. That is real technology. And I want to build it myself. From scratch. With my own hands and my own code.

I want sensors that detect when I enter a room and automatically turn things on. I want a system where I press one button on my phone and my whole room responds — lights change, fan adjusts, music starts. I want to build a plant watering system that waters my plants automatically, because I love plants but I always forget to water them. And they always die. And I always feel terrible about it.

*(pause, light smile)*

I want to build all of these things. Not buy them ready-made — build them. Because the joy is not just in having the result. The joy is in understanding exactly how it works, and knowing you made it happen.

I also want to learn how to protect these kinds of systems — because any connected system can be attacked. Think of it like this: if you build a house, you also need to know how to lock the doors properly. That is cybersecurity (CY-ber-si-KYOOR-i-tee). And I want to understand it deeply.

So — a complete, proper tech setup, plus a home lab where I can build and experiment with smart systems. That is Part One of Day 1.

**But wait — Day 1 is not finished yet.**

---

### Part Two: The Music Corner
### *(AKA: The Other Half of My Soul)*

Now. Here is something most people do not know about me when they first meet me — I love music. Not just listening to it. I love to sing. I love to play musical instruments (myoo-ZI-kul IN-stroo-ments). And specifically, I love Qawwali (kuh-WAH-lee) and Naat (NAHT) — the kind of soulful, spiritual music you feel in your chest, not just your ears.

My playlist is basically a small list of legends. Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan (noos-RAT fuh-TEH AH-lee KHAN) — the man who made Qawwali known across the entire world. Atif Aslam (aa-TIF as-LUM), for that raw, emotional voice that just cuts straight through you. A.R. Rahman (AY AR ruh-MAAN), simply a genius who can make any kind of music feel magical. And Maher Zain (MAH-her zayn), along with Sami Yusuf (SAH-mee yoo-SOOF), who both beautifully blend that Eastern, spiritual style with modern, Western sound. That mix — old soul, new sound — is exactly the kind of music I want to create myself one day.

*(pause, small smile)*

Basically, if my music taste were a road, it would go straight from a Sufi shrine to a modern recording studio, with no stops in between.

But right now, I do not have proper instruments. And anyone who loves music without proper instruments knows exactly how frustrating (frus-TRAY-ting) that feels. It is like a chef who loves cooking but only has one broken pan.

*(pause)*

Day 1, Part Two — I fix that too.

First — a proper harmonium (har-MOH-nee-um). If you know Qawwali or Naat, you know the harmonium is basically the heart of it — that warm, breathing sound behind every powerful voice. I want the best one money can buy.

Then — a tabla (TUB-lah) and a dholak (DOH-luk), the rhythm instruments that give Qawwali its pulse, its heartbeat, the sound that makes people clap along without even realizing it.

And since I admire how Sami Yusuf blends traditional sounds with modern instruments — I would also get a full-size piano keyboard (pyuh-NO KEE-bord) with weighted keys, so I can experiment with that same East-meets-West style myself.

*(smile)*

But the part I am most excited about — a proper recording (ri-KOR-ding) corner. A professional microphone — the kind that captures every detail of your voice, exactly as it sounds, with all its emotion. Studio speakers that play music back exactly as it really is — no filters, no adjustments, just honest, clear sound. And soundproofing (SOUND-proof-ing) on the walls, so the room absorbs the sound and makes everything feel like a real studio.

Because I do not just want to play music. I want to record my own Naats and Qawwali-inspired pieces. Properly. And maybe one day — share them with the world.

*(short pause, genuine smile)*

So by the end of Day 1 — one half of my room looks like a serious tech lab. The other half sounds like a small Qawwali gathering. And I am standing right in the middle of it, wondering which one to go to first.

Honestly? I will probably sit at the harmonium for five minutes, then open the laptop. Then close the laptop and start singing instead. Then tell myself I need to code. Then start singing again.

It will be a beautiful, chaotic, completely perfect day.

**Day 1 budget: 22 lakh taka. Worth every single taka.**

## Day 2 — The Trip That Actually Happens
### *(With Everyone I Love)*

Quick question for everyone — do you have a friend group that has been planning a trip for over a year?

*(pause for reaction)*

Yes. So do I.

We have been planning the big trip for a very long time. Every few months someone says — okay, this time we actually do it. And then life happens. And the trip does not happen. At this point, the trip is basically a legend. A story we tell but have never lived.

But here is my version of Day 2 — and it is a little different from what most people would do.

I am not going on this trip alone, or just with friends. I am taking everyone. My full family. All my friends. Every person who matters to me — all of them, together, one trip, one destination (des-ti-NAY-shun).

*(pause)*

Think about that for a second. When was the last time your entire family and all your friends went somewhere together, without anyone worrying about cost, without anyone saying "I cannot afford it this time"? For most of us — never. Because somebody always has to stay behind.

Not on Day 2.

I cover everything for everyone. Hotels, transport (TRANS-port), food, entry fees — every single cost, for every single person. Nobody calculates anything. Nobody feels awkward. For once, the only thing everyone needs to do is show up and enjoy.

We pick one destination and experience it properly — not rushed, not squeezed into a tight schedule, but actually explored. Good food together, real memories made together. The kind of day that people talk about for years after.

And of course — somewhere in that trip, if there is any flat ground available, there will absolutely be a cricket match. Because what is a big group trip without a cricket match? That is not a trip. That is just a very organized picnic.

*(smile)*

Honestly, of all three days — this one makes me feel the best. Because the lab and the gadgets and the music studio are all for me. But Day 2 is for everyone else. And that feels different.

**Day 2 budget: 12 lakh taka. For the trip that everyone finally gets to go on.**

## Day 3 — Full Life Automation
### *(AKA: I Want My Life to Run Itself)*

Day 3 is not just about buying gadgets (GAD-jets). Day 3 is about automating (AW-toh-may-ting) my entire life. From the moment I wake up, to the moment I sleep.

Let me walk you through what a fully automated day looks like.

**Morning:**
My alarm is a smart alarm that tracks my sleep cycle (SLEEP SY-kul) and wakes me up at the lightest sleep stage — so I do not feel like I was hit by a bus. The moment I wake up, automated (AW-toh-may-tid) curtains (KER-tenz) slowly open. Natural light comes in. My smart coffee maker already started five minutes ago because it knows my schedule. By the time I reach the kitchen, coffee is ready. I touched nothing. I made zero decisions. The morning just... runs itself.

**During the day:**
Smart lights (SMART LYTS) adjust automatically (aw-toh-MAT-ik-lee) based on time of day — soft in the morning, bright and focused in the afternoon. A robot vacuum (ROH-bot VAK-yoom) quietly cleans the floor while I sit at my desk. I am coding. The floor is getting clean. I am doing nothing about it. This is a beautiful thing.

I wear the best noise-cancelling (NOYZ KAN-sel-ing) headphones available — the moment they go on, the world disappears. A smartwatch (SMART-woch) handles all notifications (no-ti-fi-KAY-shunz) on my wrist — quick glance, no phone, no distraction. Because picking up the phone to check one message and ending up watching videos for 20 minutes — that is a very real problem that I have, and I am sure some of you can relate.

*(pause, look at the audience)*

My high-speed mesh WiFi (MESH WY-fy) means everything downloads instantly. My mechanical keyboard (meh-KAN-i-kul KEE-bord) and wireless mouse feel like luxury tools, not basic equipment. A portable power station (PORT-uh-bul POW-er STAY-shun) means I can work from the garden, the rooftop, a trip — power always with me, everywhere.

**At night:**
Smart home security (SMART HOME si-KYOOR-i-tee) — motion sensors (MOH-shun SEN-serz), cameras, automated door locks. I do not check if the door is locked. The system already knows. A smart air purifier (AIR PYOOR-i-fy-er) has been running all day quietly. Wireless charging (WY-er-les CHAR-jing) pads on every surface — desk, side table, bed — my devices charge the moment I put them down. No cables. No thinking.

And now — here is where Day 3 connects back to Day 1. I walk into my music corner. Everything is already set up — microphone, studio speakers, all connected. I sit down, press one button, and I am recording. No setup time, no cable hunting, no frustration. Just music.

*(pause)*

So basically — after Day 3, the only thing I still do manually in my life... is breathe. And honestly, if technology gets better, I might automate that too.

*(straight face — pause — let the audience react)*

Oh, and a drone (DROHN). Because I travel, I love photography, and a drone is just incredible. There is no deeper reason. I simply want one.

**Day 3 budget: 8 lakh taka. Manual tasks remaining: zero.**

## Closing — The Line That Sticks

So. Dream lab, music studio, one real trip with everyone I love, and a fully automated life. That is my 50 lakh taka in 3 days.

Here is what I realized while planning all of this. If you give someone 50 lakh taka and say "spend it on your dreams" — what they spend it on tells you more about them than any introduction ever could.

I spent mine on tools to build things I love — both in code and in music. On a trip I have been postponing for too long. And on a life that runs so efficiently that my brain is always free for the things that actually matter.

The 50 lakh taka is imaginary. But the vision of what I want my life to look like? That part is very real.

Thank you.

*(pause — smile — done)*

## Delivery Secrets — How to Make This Land

**Lines to say SLOWLY, with full eye contact:**
- *"I do not."* — your biggest laugh moment, pause before and after
- *"The trip is basically a legend."*
- *"I simply want one."* — after the drone, deadpan delivery
- *"The only thing I still do manually... is breathe."*
- *"The 50 lakh taka is imaginary. But the vision — that part is very real."*

**Lines to deliver with a small smile, slightly faster:**
- *"My back usually loses."*
- *"Day 1 is for you, back."*
- *"That is not a trip. That is just a very organized picnic."*
- *"I might automate that too."* — straight face, maximum effect
- *"Trying to decide which one to use first."* — at the end of Day 1, light smile

**Filler words — one per section, not one per sentence:**
- *"So"* — opening a new idea or section
- *"Honestly"* — before anything personal
- *"Actually"* — before surprising the audience
- *"You know"* — before a relatable observation

**Looking up from notes:**
Opening, Closing, and every *(pause)* moment — always look at the audience.

## Handling Mid-Presentation Questions

- One breath. Answer in 2 sentences. Return: *"Anyway, as I was saying..."*
- Did not understand: *"Sorry, could you repeat that please?"*
- Need time: *"That is a good question, let me think..."*

## Likely Questions + Your Answers

**Q: Why no family expenses or charity?**
A: "Actually, Day 2 is entirely for my family and friends — I cover the whole trip for everyone. That is my way of including the people I love in this plan."

**Q: Why take everyone on one trip instead of giving them money individually?**
A: "Because an experience we share together is worth more than money given separately. A trip creates a memory. Money just gets spent."

**Q: What instrument would you buy first?**
A: "The harmonium. In Qawwali and Naat, it is really the heart of the sound — I have wanted a proper one for a long time."

**Q: Why do you like Qawwali and Naat specifically?**
A: "Honestly, it is the emotion. This kind of music feels like it comes from the chest, not just the throat. Artists like Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, Sami Yusuf, and Maher Zain mix that traditional soul with modern sound, and I find that combination beautiful."

**Q: You code AND play music — how do you have time?**
A: "Music is actually what keeps me sane during long coding sessions. When my brain is full of code, music empties it again. They balance each other."

**Q: Is buying cybersecurity courses not investing?**
A: "Investing in myself is spending — it does not generate business profit. It is knowledge I use personally, not money I earn from."

**Q: What would you build first with your smart home lab?**
A: "Probably a system where one button controls my lights, fan, and music together. And definitely that plant watering system — my plants deserve better than my memory."

**Q: Why only one travel destination?**
A: "I would rather experience one place properly than rush through five and enjoy nothing. Quality over quantity (KWON-ti-tee)."

**Q: Is all this automation not lazy?**
A: "Actually the opposite — automation removes small, repetitive tasks so my brain has more energy for creative ones. The best engineers in the world automate everything they can, so they can focus on what needs human thinking."

**Q: What gadget would you buy first on Day 3?**
A: "Noise-cancelling headphones. When I am deep in a problem — coding or music — silence is the most valuable thing in the world."

**Q: What if you still had money left over?**
A: "That is the buffer. Something always goes wrong — that is just how life works. And if nothing goes wrong? I will just buy more gadgets."
*(straight face — it lands better that way)*
