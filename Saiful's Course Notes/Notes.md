## 2. Grammar Point: "There is" / "There are"

Used to say that something **exists** in a place.

### Rule

| Structure                    | When to Use                                               | Example                 |
| ---------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------- |
| **There is** + singular noun | For one (singular) countable thing, or uncountable things | There **is** a pen.     |
| **There are** + plural noun  | For more than one (plural) countable thing                | There **are** two cats. |

### 📌 Examples with "There is"

1. **There is a pen in the bag.**
   - বাংলা: ব্যাগে একটি কলম আছে।

2. **There is a pen on the table.**
   - বাংলা: টেবিলের উপর একটি কলম আছে।
   - _(Note: same structure repeated, only the preposition/place changes — "in the Bag" → "on a table")_

### 📌 Examples with "There are"

1. **There are two cats at home.**
   - বাংলা: বাসায় দুটি বিড়াল আছে।

2. **There are many clothes on the rooftop.**
   - বাংলা: ছাদে অনেক জামা আছে।

3. **There are (some clothes) in the cupboard/almirah.**
   - বাংলা: আলমারিতে (কিছু জামা) আছে।

### 🧠 Quick Tip

Ask yourself: _"Is it one thing, or more than one?"_

- One / uncountable → **There is**
- Many / plural → **There are**

## 3. Grammar Point: "Can" / "Can not (Can't)"

Used to express **ability** (what someone is able to do) or **tolerance** (what someone can bear/put up with).

### Rule

| Structure                                                 | Meaning                                    | Example                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------------------------- |
| **Subject + can + verb (base form)**                      | Positive ability                           | I **can** play cricket.     |
| **Subject + can not / cannot / can't + verb (base form)** | Negative ability / inability / intolerance | I **can not** play cricket. |

> Note: "Can" is always followed by the **base form of the verb** (no "to", no "-ing", no "-s").
> ✅ I can play. ❌ I can to play. ❌ I can playing.

### 📌 Examples with "Can"

1. **I can play cricket.**
   - বাংলা: আমি ক্রিকেট খেলতে পারি।

### 📌 Examples with "Can not"

1. **I can not stand Rahim.**
   - বাংলা: আমি রহিমকে সহ্য করতে পারি না।
   - _(Here "stand" means "tolerate," not "stand up physically")_

2. **I can not play cricket.**
   - বাংলা: আমি ক্রিকেট খেলতে পারি না।

### 🧠 Quick Tip

- **Can** = ability/permission (পারা)
- **Can not / Can't** = inability/refusal/intolerance (পারা না)
- "Can not stand [someone/something]" is a common idiomatic use meaning "cannot tolerate."

## 7. Grammar Point: "Have to" / "Has to"

Used to express **obligation or necessity** — something a person **must** do (compulsion, not choice).

### Rule

| Subject             | Structure                      | Meaning        |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------- |
| I / We / You / They | **have to** + verb (base form) | must / need to |
| He / She / It       | **has to** + verb (base form)  | must / need to |

> 🧠 **Key Rule:** Use **"has to"** only with third-person singular subjects (He, She, It, or a singular name). Use **"have to"** with everyone else (I, We, You, They).

### 📌 Examples

1. **I have to speak English.**
   - বাংলা: আমার ইংরেজি বলতে হবে।

2. **I have to go to Dhaka.**
   - বাংলা: আমার ঢাকা যেতে হবে।

3. **We have to go to Dhaka.**
   - বাংলা: আমাদের ঢাকা যেতে হবে।

4. **You have to go to Dhaka.**
   - বাংলা: তোমার/আপনার ঢাকা যেতে হবে।

5. **They have to go to Dhaka.**
   - বাংলা: তাদের ঢাকা যেতে হবে।

6. **He has to go.**
   - বাংলা: তার যেতে হবে।
   - _(Notice "has to" is used here because the subject "He" is third-person singular)_

### 🧠 Quick Tip

Think of "have to / has to" as expressing something **forced by circumstances** — a duty, requirement, or necessity — not a personal choice.

## 8. Grammar Point: "Should" / "Should not"

Used to give **advice, suggestions, or express what is morally/practically right** (obligation of a softer kind than "have to").

### Rule

| Structure                         | Meaning                                         | Bangla   |
| --------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | -------- |
| **Should** + verb (base form)     | It is advisable / the right thing to do         | উচিত     |
| **Should not** + verb (base form) | It is NOT advisable / not the right thing to do | উচিত নয় |

> Note: "Should" does not change with subject (no "should to," no "should-s" for he/she/it).
> ✅ He should go. ❌ He should to go. ❌ He shoulds go.

### 📌 Examples with "Should"

1. **I should go to the Mosque.**
   - বাংলা: আমার মসজিদে নামাজ আদায় করা উচিত।

2. **I should sleep.**
   - বাংলা: আমার ঘুমানো উচিত।

### 📌 Examples with "Should not"

1. **I should not curse.**
   - বাংলা: আমার গালি দেওয়া উচিত নয়।

2. **I should go.**
   - বাংলা: আমার যাওয়া উচিত।

### 🧠 Quick Tip

- **Have to / Has to** → strong obligation (a rule, requirement, necessity — you often have no choice)
- **Should / Should not** → advice or moral suggestion (recommended, but you technically have a choice)

**Compare:**
| Sentence | Strength | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| I have to go to Dhaka. | Strong (necessity) | I am required/forced to go |
| I should go to the Mosque. | Softer (advice) | It's the right/good thing to do |

## 12. Grammar/Speaking Point: Introducing Yourself — Casual vs. Formal

When introducing yourself in English, the **structure changes** depending on whether the situation is casual (informal) or formal.

### Rule

| Style      | Structure           | Example                  |
| ---------- | ------------------- | ------------------------ |
| **Casual** | This is + [Name].   | This is Touhidul Alam.   |
| **Formal** | Myself is + [Name]. | Myself is Touhidul Alam. |

> 🧠 **Tip:** Use the casual form with friends or in relaxed settings. Use the formal form in interviews, official meetings, or when speaking to someone senior/unfamiliar.

### 📌 Model Sentences

1. **I go by the name Touhidul Alam.**
   - _(Introducing your full/official name)_

2. **This is Touhidul Alam.** → _(Casual)_

3. **Myself is Touhidul Alam.** → _(Formal)_
   - **But you can call me Touhid.**
   - _(Giving a shorter nickname people can use)_

## 16. Grammar Point: "There was / There were / There is / There are / There will be"

This page expands the "There is / There are" concept from Page 1 across **all three tenses**.

### Rule Table

| Tense       | Singular          | Plural                              |
| ----------- | ----------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Past**    | There **was**     | There **were**                      |
| **Present** | There **is**      | There **are**                       |
| **Future**  | There **will be** | There **will be** _(same for both)_ |

### 📌 Examples

| Tense              | Example                                  |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------- |
| Past (singular)    | There **was** a cat in the room.         |
| Past (plural)      | There **were** many people at the event. |
| Present (singular) | There **is** a pen in the bag.           |
| Present (plural)   | There **are** two cats at home.          |
| Future             | There **will be** a meeting tomorrow.    |

> 🧠 **Key Insight:** "Will be" works for both singular and plural in the future — no need to change it!

## 17. Speaking Section: Hobbies / Free Time (অবসরে তুমি কী করো?)

When asked **"What do you do in your free time?"** in English, use this structure:

### Structure

> **In my free time, I love to + [verb/activity].**

### 📌 Model Sentences

1. **In my free time, I love to play football.**
   - বাংলা: আমার অবসরে খেলতে ভালোবাসি (ফুটবল ভালোবাসি)।

2. **In my free time, I love to listen to music.**
   - বাংলা: আমার অবসরে সঙ্গীত শুনতে ভালোবাসি।

3. **In my free time, I love to travel and listen to music.**
   - _(Combining two hobbies with "and")_

### 🧠 How to Talk About Multiple Hobbies

Join activities using **"and"**:

> In my free time, I love to **[hobby 1]** and **[hobby 2]**.

### Common Hobby Phrases

| Activity                | Expression                 |
| ----------------------- | -------------------------- |
| Play football / cricket | I love to play football.   |
| Listen to music         | I love to listen to music. |
| Travel                  | I love to travel.          |
| Read books              | I love to read books.      |
| Watch movies            | I love to watch movies.    |
| Cook                    | I love to cook.            |

## 18. Speaking Section: Aim / Future Goal

When asked **"What is your aim in life?"** or **"What do you want to be?"**, use this structure:

### Structure

> **In my future, I would like to be a/an + [profession/goal].**

### 📌 Model Sentence

1. **In my future, I would like to be an Engineer.**
   - _(Note: use "an" before a word starting with a vowel sound — "an Engineer")_

### 🧠 Alternative Structures

| Structure                              | Example                        |
| -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| I would like to be a/an + [profession] | I would like to be a Doctor.   |
| I want to become a/an + [profession]   | I want to become a Teacher.    |
| My aim is to + [verb]                  | My aim is to serve my country. |

## 19. Speaking Section: Ending / Closing a Self-Introduction

Always **close your self-introduction** politely. Use the standard closing line:

### 📌 Closing Line

> **That's all about myself. Thank you.**

> 🧠 This signals to the listener that you have finished your introduction. It is polite, professional, and commonly expected in interviews and spoken English tests.

## 20. Vocabulary: "I understand" vs. "I understood"

A very common and important distinction in everyday spoken English:

| Expression       | Bangla     | Tense                             | When to Use                                                                |
| ---------------- | ---------- | --------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **I understand** | আমি বুঝি   | Present (ongoing/general ability) | When you currently understand something, or understand things in general   |
| **I understood** | আমি বুঝেছি | Past (something just understood)  | When you just understood something right now, OR understood it in the past |

### 📌 Examples

1. **I understand** = আমি বুঝি
   - _"I understand what you are saying."_ (I follow your point right now)

2. **I understood** = আমি বুঝেছি
   - _"I understood the lesson."_ (I grasped it — it clicked for me)

> ⚠️ In Bengali conversation, people often say "আমি বুঝেছি" to mean "I get it now" — this maps to **"I understood"** in English (past tense indicating a completed act of understanding). Don't say "I understand" if you mean "I just got it" — use "I understood."

## 28. Important Note on Verbs

> 📌 **Understand, Like** — এগুলোর সাথে **(ing)** হবে না।
> Meaning: Verbs like **"understand"** and **"like"** do NOT take the **-ing** form in simple/general sentences.
> ✅ I understand you. ❌ I am understanding you.
> ✅ I like music. ❌ I am liking music.
> These are called **stative verbs** — they describe a state, not an action, so they don't use continuous (-ing) forms.

## 30. Vocabulary: Verbs of Movement / Direction

Different verbs are used depending on **how** you move:

| Verb     | Bangla                                    | Example Sentence                            |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- |
| **Go**   | যাওয়া (moving away from current place)   | I go to Dhaka. (আমি ঢাকা যাই)               |
| **Come** | আসা (moving toward current place/speaker) | I come to Dhaka. (আমি ঢাকায় আসি)           |
| **Walk** | হেঁটে যাওয়া                              | I walk to School. (আমি হেঁটে স্কুলে যাই)    |
| **Run**  | দৌড়ানো                                   | I run to Khulsi. (আমি দৌড়িয়ে খুলশিতে যাই) |

> 🧠 **Go vs Come:**
>
> - Use **"go"** when moving **away** from where you currently are.
> - Use **"come"** when moving **toward** where the speaker/listener is.
> - Example: _"I go to Dhaka"_ (I leave here for Dhaka). _"I come to Dhaka"_ (I arrive/move to Dhaka — context dependent).

## 31. Grammar Point: Positive / Negative / Question — Simple Present Tense (with "go")

This page introduces the **three core forms** of any sentence: Positive (affirmative), Negative, and Question.

### Structure: Simple Present (I / We / You / They)

| Form            | Structure                       | Example (Bangla → English)  |
| --------------- | ------------------------------- | --------------------------- |
| ✅ **Positive** | Subject + verb (base)           | আমি যাই → **I go**          |
| ❌ **Negative** | Subject + do not / don't + verb | আমি যাই না → **I don't go** |
| ❓ **Question** | Do + subject + verb?            | আমি কি যাই? → **Do I go?**  |

### 📌 Model Examples

1. **আমি যাই** → **I go**
   - _(Subject: I / We / He / Rahim / They — can replace depending on sentence)_

2. **আমি যাই না** → **I don't go**
   - _(Use: do not / don't for negation with I/We/You/They)_

3. **আমি কি যাই?** → **Do I go?**
   - _(Use: Do at the beginning for a Yes/No question)_

## 32. Grammar Point: Positive / Negative / Question — Present Continuous Tense (with "go")

Present Continuous = something happening **right now** or around this time.

### Structure

| Form            | Structure                            | Example                            |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------- |
| ✅ **Positive** | Subject + am/is/are + verb-ing       | আমি যাচ্ছি → **I am going**        |
| ❌ **Negative** | Subject + am/is/are + not + verb-ing | আমি যাচ্ছি না → **I am not going** |
| ❓ **Question** | Am/Is/Are + subject + verb-ing?      | আমি কি যাচ্ছি? → **Am I going?**   |

### 📌 Model Examples

1. **আমি যাচ্ছি** → **I am going**

2. **আমি যাচ্ছি না** → **I am not going**

3. **আমি কি যাচ্ছি?** → **Am I going?**

## 36. Grammar Point: WH Questions — "Where"

Building on Page 6's question structure, this page introduces **WH questions** (questions that ask for information, not just yes/no).

### Structure

> **WH word + do/does/did + subject + verb (base)?**

| Bangla           | English              | Notes                            |
| ---------------- | -------------------- | -------------------------------- |
| তুমি কি যাও?     | Do you go?           | Yes/No question (from Page 6)    |
| তুমি কোথায় যাও? | **Where do you go?** | WH question — asking about place |

### 📌 Examples

1. **Where do you go?**
   - তুমি কোথায় যাও?

2. _(Implied full form)_: Where do you go? → I go to school / I go to Dhaka / etc.

> 🧠 **Key Rule:** In WH questions, "Do/Does" still comes after the WH word, and the main verb stays in **base form**.
> ✅ Where do you **go**? ❌ Where do you **goes**?

> **I feel like** = মনে হচ্ছে / ইচ্ছে করছে

Used to express a **desire, urge, or vague feeling** in the moment.

### Structure

> **I feel like + verb-ing / noun**

### 📌 Examples

## 38. Vocabulary: "-ever" Words (কিন্তু / তথাপি / যাই হোক)

## 39. Grammar Point: "I am supposed to" (বর্তমান — কারো কথা)

**"Supposed to"** expresses something that is expected, planned, or meant to happen — often based on someone else's instruction, a rule, or an agreement.

### Present Form

> **I am supposed to + verb (base form)**
> Meaning: আমার [কাজটি] করার কথা (এখন/এই মুহূর্তে প্রত্যাশিত)

### 📌 Examples (Present — "am supposed to")

1. **I am supposed to play football.**
   - বাংলা: আমার ফুটবল খেলার কথা।

2. **I am supposed to write.**
   - বাংলা: আমার লেখার কথা।

### Past Form — "I was supposed to" (অতীত — কারো কথা ছিল)

> **I was supposed to + verb (base form)**
> Meaning: আমার [কাজটি] করার কথা ছিল (কিন্তু হয়নি — implied)

### 📌 Examples (Past — "was supposed to")

1. **I was supposed to play football.**
   - বাংলা: আমার ফুটবল খেলার কথা ছিল।

2. **I was supposed to write.**
   - বাংলা: আমার লেখার কথা ছিল।

> 🧠 **Key Insight:**
>
> - **"I am supposed to"** → the expectation is **current** (it's supposed to happen now/soon)
> - **"I was supposed to"** → the expectation was in the **past** — and it is **often implied that it did NOT happen**
>   - Example: "I was supposed to call you." (কিন্তু আমি করিনি — but I didn't.)

## 43. Speaking Skill: Body Language & Intonation

### Body Language

When speaking English, your **body language** matters as much as your words:

- Make eye contact with your listener
- Stand/sit straight with confidence
- Use natural hand gestures to support your speech
- Avoid looking down or away — it signals lack of confidence

### Intonation (কণ্ঠের tone)

> **Intonation = Intonation** _(the note crossed out "Invlation" and corrected it)_ → means **the rise and fall of your voice (কণ্ঠের tone)**

Intonation makes your English sound natural and expressive:

| Type                       | When to Use               | Voice Direction            |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| **Rising intonation** (↗)  | Yes/No questions          | Voice goes UP at the end   |
| **Falling intonation** (↘) | Statements & WH questions | Voice goes DOWN at the end |

> 🧠 **Why it matters:** If you speak in a flat, monotone voice, English sounds unnatural and robotic. Native speakers use intonation constantly to show emotion, emphasis, and meaning.

---

## 44. Grammar Point: "I told you to..." (Reported Instruction)

Used when you are telling someone what instruction or request you gave earlier.

### Structure

> **I told + [person] + to + verb (base form)**

### 📌 Model Sentence

> **I told you to bring me a bunch of red roses this morning.**
> বাংলা: আজ সকালে তোমাকে আমি কতগুলো লাল গোলাপ ফুল দিয়ে আসতে বলেছিলাম।

### Breakdown of the sentence

| Part                 | Role                                       |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| I told you           | The instruction was given in the past      |
| to bring me          | What was asked to do (bring = action verb) |
| a bunch of red roses | Object — what to bring                     |
| this morning         | Time reference                             |

> 🧠 **"A bunch of"** = একগুচ্ছ / কতগুলো → used for flowers, keys, grapes, etc.
> "Bring" → to carry something **toward** the speaker (opposite of "take" — which moves **away** from the speaker).

## 45. Speaking Skill: Extension with Beautification

This is one of the **most important spoken English skills** — giving a longer, more natural, and interesting answer instead of a short one.

### The Concept

When someone asks you a question, don't just give a one-word or one-phrase answer. **Extend your answer** by adding:

- **Reason** (because...)
- **Time** (especially on weekends / throughout the week...)
- **Details** (what, where, how...)

### Question & Extended Answer Example

**Question:** What do you do in your free time?

**Short (weak) answer:** I like sleeping.

**Extended (beautified) answer:**

> **Well, I like sleeping in my free time, especially on weekends, because I am busy throughout the week.**

### Breakdown of the Extended Answer

| Element                                   | Part of Sentence    | Purpose                                    |
| ----------------------------------------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| **Well,**                                 | Gap filler / opener | Sounds natural, buys thinking time         |
| **I like sleeping**                       | Core answer         | Direct response to the question            |
| **in my free time**                       | Time context        | Clarifies when                             |
| **especially on weekends**                | Extra detail        | Makes answer more specific and interesting |
| **because I am busy throughout the week** | Reason              | Explains why → beautifies the answer       |

## 46. Speaking Skill: Gap Fillers

**Gap fillers** are words or phrases used at the **beginning of a spoken answer** to sound natural and fluent while you gather your thoughts. They are completely normal and used by native speakers too.

| Gap Filler             | When to Use                            |
| ---------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **Well,**              | General opener — the most common       |
| **Toh,**               | Informal transition (similar to "So,") |
| **Honestly speaking,** | When giving a sincere/personal opinion |

### 📌 Examples in use

1. **Well,** I like sleeping in my free time...
2. **Honestly speaking,** I prefer staying home on weekends.
3. **Toh,** I usually go for a walk in the evening.

> ⚠️ **Don't overuse gap fillers.** Use one at the start of your answer, then speak clearly. Repeating "Well... well... well..." sounds unnatural.

## 49. Speaking Skill: Gap Fillers / Beginnings (expanded)

This page expands the gap filler list from Page 8. These are **sentence starters** that make your spoken English sound more fluent and natural.

| #   | Gap Filler / Beginning              | Use / Notes                                  |

> 🧠 **"As far as [topic] is concerned"** is particularly useful in spoken tests and interviews. It signals that you are about to address a specific topic directly and thoughtfully.

### 📌 Examples

1. **As far as free time is concerned, I love spending time with my friends.**
   - _(Introducing the topic "free time" formally before answering)_

2. **As far as my address is concerned, I reside in Kotwali.**
   - _(Introducing the topic "address" before stating it)_

### Template

> **As far as [topic] is concerned, [your answer].**

| my hobby   | As far as my hobby is concerned, I love playing cricket. |
| my family  | As far as my family is concerned, there are 4 members.   |

## 50. Vocabulary: Frequency Adverbs (সাধারণত)

These words describe **how often** something happens. Use them to make your sentences richer and more natural.

| Word          | Bangla            | Strength (most → least frequent) |
| ------------- | ----------------- | -------------------------------- |
| **Normally**  | সাধারণত           | Very common / usual              |
| **Usually**   | সাধারণত / প্রায়ই | Most of the time                 |
| **Generally** | সাধারণভাবে        | As a general rule                |
| **Basically** | মূলত              | Fundamentally / at the core      |

> 🧠 **Normally / Usually / Generally** are nearly synonymous and interchangeable in most spoken contexts.
> **Basically** is slightly different — it means "at the most fundamental level" or "in simple terms."

### 📌 Examples

| Sentence                                 | Meaning                               |
| ---------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| I **normally** wake up at 6 AM.          | আমি সাধারণত সকাল ৬টায় উঠি।           |
| I **usually** go to bed early.           | আমি সাধারণত তাড়াতাড়ি ঘুমাই।         |
| I **generally** prefer home-cooked food. | আমি সাধারণভাবে ঘরের রান্না পছন্দ করি। |
| **Basically**, I am a simple person.     | মূলত, আমি একজন সাধারণ মানুষ।          |

### Position in a Sentence

Frequency adverbs usually go:

- **Before the main verb:** I **usually** eat rice.
- **After "am/is/are":** I am **generally** busy on weekdays.

## 51. Speaking Skill: যোগের শব্দ (Joining / Linking Words)

These are **connectors** that join ideas within a sentence or between sentences. They make speech flow naturally and show the relationship between ideas.

| Joining Word     | Type              | Bangla             | Use                                                        |
| ---------------- | ----------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **because**      | Reason            | কারণ               | Explains why something happens                             |
| **but**          | Contrast          | কিন্তু             | Introduces an opposing idea                                |
| **however**      | Contrast (formal) | তবুও / কিন্তু      | More formal version of "but" — often starts a new sentence |
| **nevertheless** | Contrast (strong) | তবুও / তা সত্ত্বেও | Despite what was said — strong contrast                    |

### 📌 Examples

1. **because:**

   > I like sleeping in my free time **because** I am busy throughout the week.

2. **but:**

   > I love football, **but** I don't have time to play these days.

3. **however:**

   > I am very tired. **However**, I will finish the work tonight.

4. **nevertheless:**
   > It was raining heavily. **Nevertheless**, we went to the game.

### 🧠 Difference between "but" and "however"

| Word        | Position in sentence                        | Formality                    |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **but**     | In the middle of a sentence                 | Informal / everyday          |
| **however** | At the start of a new sentence (with comma) | More formal / written/spoken |

## 52. Putting It All Together — Extended Answer Model

Combining gap fillers + frequency adverbs + joining words + extension:

**Question:** What do you do in your free time?

> **Well, as far as free time is concerned, I love spending time with my friends. I normally go out on weekends, but during the week I am usually very busy. Honestly speaking, spending time with friends refreshes my mind. However, I also enjoy reading books whenever I get the chance.**

Notice how this answer uses:

- ✅ Gap filler: _Well_
- ✅ "As far as... is concerned"
- ✅ Frequency adverb: _normally, usually_
- ✅ Joining words: _but, However_
- ✅ Extension: reason + detail

## 55. Speaking Practice: Extended Answers with Joining Words

This page shows **full model answers** using all the tools learned so far — gap fillers, joining words, extension, and beautification. These answer two very common spoken English questions.

---

### Question 1: What is your favourite colour?

**Short (weak) answer:** Blue.

**Extended (beautified) model answer:**

> **Honestly speaking, all colours are my favourite. But nevertheless, blue is my favourite one. Because blue is something special.**

### Breakdown

| Element         | Part                                | Purpose                               |
| --------------- | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Gap filler      | _Honestly speaking,_                | Natural opener, sounds sincere        |
| Broad statement | _all colours are my favourite_      | Shows appreciation, sounds thoughtful |
| Contrast        | _But / Nevertheless_                | Narrows down to the real favourite    |
| Core answer     | _blue is my favourite one_          | Direct answer                         |
| Reason          | _Because blue is something special_ | Beautification — explains why         |

> 🧠 **"Nevertheless"** here is used right after "But" for extra emphasis: _"But nevertheless..."_ = তবুও / কিন্তু তা সত্ত্বেও
> This is a strong, impressive connector to use in spoken English tests.

---

### Question 2: What is your favourite food?

**Short (weak) answer:** Biryani.

**Extended (beautified) model answer:**

> **Well, many foods are my favourite. But Biryani is my most favourite food. Because Biryani is very delicious and** _(the sentence continues — e.g., "it makes me feel happy")_

### Breakdown

| Element         | Part                                       | Purpose                               |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| Gap filler      | _Well,_                                    | Natural opener                        |
| Broad statement | _many foods are my favourite_              | Sounds thoughtful, not too narrow     |
| Contrast        | _But_                                      | Shifts to the specific favourite      |
| Superlative     | _my most favourite food_                   | Emphasises the top choice             |
| Reason          | _Because Biryani is very delicious and..._ | Beautification — always add a reason! |

> 🧠 **Tip:** Always structure favourite-type answers as:
>
> 1. Open broadly ("many X are my favourite")
> 2. Narrow down ("But Y is my most favourite")
> 3. Give a reason ("Because...")

## 56. Speaking Skill: Tongue Twisters (উচ্চারণ অনুশীলন)

**Tongue twisters** are sentences that are deliberately difficult to pronounce quickly. Practising them improves:

- **Pronunciation clarity** — making each sound distinct
- **Mouth muscle control** — training your lips and tongue
- **Fluency and speed** — speaking faster without stumbling
- **Confidence** — if you can say these, normal sentences feel easy!

> 🧠 **How to practise:** Start slowly, saying each word clearly. Then gradually increase your speed. Repeat 5–10 times daily.

---

### Tongue Twister 1

> **She sells sea-shells by the sea-shore.**

- **Focus sound:** /s/ and /sh/ — practises distinguishing between "s" (সি) and "sh" (শ) sounds
- **Tip:** "sells" and "sea" use /s/; "shells" and "shore" use /sh/. Keep them separate!

---

### Tongue Twister 2

> **Red Lorry – Yellow Lorry.**

- **Focus sound:** /r/ and /l/ — two sounds that Bengali speakers often mix up
- **Tip:** Repeat alternately, faster and faster: _Red Lorry, Yellow Lorry, Red Lorry, Yellow Lorry..._

---

### Tongue Twister 3

> **New York – New Unique – New Yolk.**

- **Focus sound:** /y/ sound at the start of words (York, Unique, Yolk)
- **Tip:** Each word starts with a /y/ or /n/ + /y/ blend — practise the transition carefully

---

### Tongue Twister 4

> **Susee is sitting in a shoe-shine shop while eating chicken in the kitchen.**

- **Focus sounds:** /s/, /sh/, /ch/ — three similar but distinct sounds
- **Breakdown:**
  - **Susee / sitting** → /s/ sound
  - **shoe-shine / shop** → /sh/ sound
  - **chicken / kitchen** → /ch/ sound
- **Tip:** The hardest part is switching quickly between /sh/ and /ch/ — go slowly at first

---

| What do you do in your free time? | Well, I love [hobby], especially [when/where], because [reason].                                     |

## 59. Grammar: Tense Practice — Past / Present / Future

This section practises the **same verb across three tenses**, showing how the form changes.

### Key Verbs Introduced

| Bangla         | English Base Verb |
| -------------- | ----------------- |
| বলা/চেষ্টা করা | try               |
| চলমান          | (ongoing action)  |

### Tense Forms of "try" and "pay"

| Tense                 | Structure                   | Example                                   |
| --------------------- | --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Past** (অতীত)       | verb + -ed / was + verb-ing | 1. I tried. / 2. I was paying.            |
| **Present** (বর্তমান) | verb / am + verb-ing        | 1. I try. / 2. I am trying / I am paying. |
| **Future** (ভবিষ্যৎ)  | will + verb                 | 1. I will try.                            |

### 📌 Examples from notes

**Past:**

1. I **tried**. _(অতীতে চেষ্টা করেছিলাম — completed action)_
2. I **was paying**. _(অতীতে পরিশোধ করছিলাম — was in the middle of paying)_

**Present:**

1. I **try**. / I **am trying**. _(এখন চেষ্টা করছি)_
2. I **am paying**. _(এখন পরিশোধ করছি — চলমান)_

**Future:**

1. I **will try**. _(ভবিষ্যতে চেষ্টা করব)_

> 🧠 **Simple vs Continuous:**
>
> - Simple past ("I tried") = action is **completed**
> - Past continuous ("I was trying/paying") = action was **in progress** at a point in the past

## 60. Daily Life Conversations (দৈনন্দিন কথোপকথন)

### Situation 1: Asking Why Someone Didn't Come

### Situation 2: Leaving for Home

### Situation 3: Describing How You Slept

> বাংলা: গতরাতে আমি খুব ভালো ঘুমিয়েছি।

### Situation 4: Asking About a Bus

> **Where is this bus going / heading?**
> বাংলা: বাসটি কোথায় যাচ্ছে?

> 🧠 **"Heading"** = going in the direction of. "Where is this bus heading?" is a very natural, common expression at bus stops.

---

### Situation 5: Asking Someone to Change Money

> **Would you have change of a 500 taka?**
> বাংলা: আপনার কাছে ৫০০ টাকার ভাঙতি আছে?

> 🧠 **"Change"** here means **small/broken currency** (ভাঙতি), not the action of changing.
> **"Would you have..."** = a very polite way to ask — more polite than "Do you have...?"

---

### Situation 6: Describing Weather / Visibility

> **My glasses are foggy due to rain.**
> বাংলা: বৃষ্টির কারণে আমার চশমা ঝাপসা হয়ে গেছে।

> 🧠 **"Due to"** = কারণে / জন্য — used to give a reason.
> **"Foggy"** = ঝাপসা / কুয়াশাচ্ছন্ন (here, misty/blurred because of rain or steam)

### Situation 7: Going to Buy Something

> **I am going Bananas.**
> বাংলা: আমি কলা কিনতে যাচ্ছি।

> ⚠️ **Note:** In everyday speech this means literally "I am going (to buy) bananas." However, be aware that **"going bananas"** is also an English idiom meaning "going crazy/mad." Context makes the meaning clear.

### Situation 8: Attending a Meeting

> বাংলা: আমি মিটিংয়ে যাচ্ছি।

> 🧠 **"Participate in"** = অংশগ্রহণ করা — a more formal way of saying you are taking part in something.

## 63. Vocabulary: Commonly Confused Words

### Compliment vs Complement

| Word           | Bangla Meaning             | What it means                                            |
| -------------- | -------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Compliment** | কোনো কিছুর প্রশংসা         | A nice thing you say to someone — praising them          |
| **Complement** | কোনো কিছুর জোড়া / মানানসই | Something that goes well with / completes something else |

### 📌 Examples

1. **Compliment** (প্রশংসা):

   > "You look great today!" ← This is a **compliment**.
   > "She gave me a compliment about my English."

2. **Complement** (মানানসই/পরিপূরক):
   > "Sugar and spice **complement** each other." ← They go well together.
   > "Salt complements food." ← Salt enhances/completes the taste of food.

> 🧠 **Memory trick:**
>
> - **complIment** → I like it! (praise) → the "I" reminds you it's about a **person**
> - **complEment** → complEte → it **completes** or matches something

## 69. Grammar Point: Modal Verbs — Expressing "Going" with Different Meanings

This is one of the most important pages in the course. It shows how the **same action ("go")** can be expressed with completely different **meanings, strengths, and tones** by changing the modal verb.

> 🧠 **Modal verbs** are helping verbs that express ability, possibility, permission, obligation, intention, etc. They always come before the **base form** of the verb (no -s, no -ing, no -ed).

---

### Complete Table: All Ways to Say "আমি যাব / যাওয়ার কথা"

| Bangla                          | English                      | Modal Used             | Strength / Meaning                    |
| ------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| আমি যাই                         | **I go**                     | — (simple present)     | Habit / general fact                  |
| আমি যাচ্ছি                      | **I am going**               | — (present continuous) | Currently happening / planned         |
| আমি যাচ্ছিলাম                   | **I was going**              | — (past continuous)    | Was in the middle of going            |
| আমার যাওয়া উচিত                | **I should go**              | should                 | Mild advice / moral duty              |
| আমি যেতে পারি                   | **I can go**                 | can                    | Ability / possibility                 |
| আমি যেতে সক্ষম                  | **I am able to go**          | able to                | Formal ability                        |
| আমার অবশ্যই যাব                 | **I must go**                | must                   | Strong obligation (self-imposed)      |
| আমার যেতে হবে                   | **I have to go**             | have to                | External obligation / necessity       |
| আমার যেতে হয়েছিল               | **I had to go**              | had to                 | Past obligation (it was necessary)    |
| আমি যেতে চাই                    | **I want to go**             | want to                | Desire / wish                         |
| আমার যাওয়ার কথা                | **I am supposed to go**      | supposed to            | Expected / planned by someone else    |
| আমার যাওয়ার ইচ্ছা              | **I would (like) to go**     | would                  | Polite desire / conditional wish      |
| আমার যেতে পারতাম                | **I might go** _(I may go)_  | might / may            | Uncertain possibility (হয়তো যাব)     |
| আমার যাওয়া উচিত ছিল            | **I should have gone**       | should have            | Regret — should have done but didn't  |
| আমার যেতে পারতাম (কিন্তু যাইনি) | **I should could have gone** | should/could have      | Missed possibility + regret           |
| তুমি গেলে আমিও যেতাম            | **If you go, I would go**    | would (conditional)    | Conditional — depends on someone else |
| আমার ভবিষ্যতে যাব               | **I will go**                | will                   | Definite future intention             |

---

## 70. Detailed Breakdown — Each Modal Explained

### 1. I go / I am going

- **I go** = simple habit (আমি সাধারণত যাই)
- **I am going** = currently happening or near future plan (আমি এখন যাচ্ছি)

### 2. I want to go (আমি যেতে চাই)

> Expresses **desire** — you wish to go, but it's not confirmed.

### 3. I will go (আমার ভবিষ্যতে যাব)

> Expresses **definite future intention** — you are certain you will go.
> ✅ Use "will" for promises, decisions made at the moment, and strong intentions.

### 4. I have to go / I had to go

> - **Have to go** = must go NOW (external necessity — বাধ্য)
> - **Had to go** = was forced/necessary to go in the PAST (অতীতে বাধ্য ছিলাম)

### 5. I would (like) to go (আমার যাওয়ার ইচ্ছা)

> Expresses a **polite wish or conditional desire**.
> "I would go" often implies "...if conditions were right."

### 6. I might go / I may go (আমার যেতে পারতাম — সম্ভাবনা)

> Expresses **uncertainty** — maybe yes, maybe no.
>
> - **Might** = slightly less certain than "may"
> - Both mean "হয়তো যাব"

### 7. I am supposed to go (আমার যাওয়ার কথা)

> Someone else expects you to go — a prior plan or expectation.

### 8. I should go (আমার যাওয়া উচিত)

> **Advice or moral duty** — the right thing to do.

### 9. I can go / I am able to go

> - **Can go** = I have the ability/permission (যেতে পারি)
> - **Am able to go** = more formal version of "can"

### 10. I must go (আমার অবশ্যই যেতে হবে)

> **Strong obligation** — often self-imposed or very urgent. Stronger than "have to."

### 11. I should have gone (আমার যাওয়া উচিত ছিল — কিন্তু যাইনি)

> Expresses **regret about the past** — you didn't go but you should have.
> Structure: **should have + past participle (gone)**

### 12. I should/could have gone

> Expresses a **missed opportunity** — it was possible AND advisable, but it didn't happen.

### 13. If you go, I would go (শর্তসাপেক্ষ)

> **Conditional** — your action depends on someone else's action.
> Structure: **If + [condition], I would + [action]**

```
Weakest ←————————————————————————→ Strongest

might / may → would → should → can → want to → will → have to → must
(হয়তো)    (ইচ্ছা)  (উচিত)  (পারি) (চাই)    (যাব) (যেতে হবে) (অবশ্যই)
```

## 74. Vocabulary: Words for RICH and POOR

An important speaking skill is having **variety in vocabulary** — instead of always saying "rich" or "poor," use more expressive words.

### Words for RICH (ধনী)

| Word         | Bangla Meaning      | Level / Notes                                                  |
| ------------ | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Loaded**   | অনেক ধনী (informal) | Very informal/slang — "He is loaded" = he has a lot of money   |
| **Well-off** | সচ্ছল               | Comfortably wealthy — not extreme, but financially comfortable |
| **Opulent**  | বিলাসবহুল ধনী       | Very rich, with lavish lifestyle — grand, luxurious            |
| **Solvent**  | আর্থিকভাবে সক্ষম    | Able to pay all debts — financially stable                     |
| **Wealthy**  | ধনী / সম্পদশালী     | Standard formal word for rich                                  |

### Words for POOR (গরিব)

| Word             | Bangla Meaning            | Level / Notes                                                   |
| ---------------- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Broke**        | একদম কপর্দকহীন (informal) | Temporarily having no money — "I am broke" = আমার কাছে টাকা নেই |
| **Needy**        | অভাবী                     | In need — lacking basic necessities                             |
| **Destitute**    | নিঃস্ব / সর্বহারা         | Extremely poor — having nothing at all                          |
| **Impoverished** | দারিদ্র্যপীড়িত           | Formal word for made poor, often by circumstances               |

> 🧠 **Usage Tips:**
>
> - **Broke** is casual/everyday: "I can't come, I'm broke this week."
> - **Destitute** and **Impoverished** are more formal/written/news language.
> - **Well-off** is polite — a gentle way to say someone is rich without sounding blunt.

## 75. Grammar Point: "A Few" vs "Few"

A very common and important distinction in English:

| Expression | Bangla              | Meaning                             | Feeling                      |
| ---------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **A few**  | কয়েকজন / কয়েকটি   | Some — a small number (positive)    | Positive — I DO have some    |
| **Few**    | বন্ধু নেই বললেই চলে | Hardly any — very little (negative) | Negative — I barely have any |

### 📌 Model Sentences

1. **আমার অল্প কয়েকজন বন্ধু আছে।**
   → **I have a few friends.**
   _(I have some friends — not many, but some. Positive tone.)_

2. **আমার বন্ধুবান্ধব নেই বললেই চলে।**
   → **I have few friends.**
   _(I barely have any friends. Negative/sad tone.)_

### 🧠 Memory Trick

- **A few** = **A** is there → there IS something → **positive**
- **Few** = alone, no "a" → almost nothing → **negative**

### More Examples

| Sentence                            | Meaning                                             |
| ----------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------- |
| I have **a few** minutes.           | আমার কিছুটা সময় আছে। (positive — some time exists) |
| I have **few** minutes.             | আমার প্রায় সময় নেই। (negative — barely any time)  |
| She has **a few** friends in Dhaka. | ঢাকায় তার কয়েকজন বন্ধু আছে।                       |
| She has **few** friends in Dhaka.   | ঢাকায় তার বন্ধু নেই বললেই চলে।                     |

> ⚠️ This applies to **countable nouns** (friends, minutes, books, etc.)
> For uncountable nouns: use **"a little"** (positive) and **"little"** (negative).
> Example: I have **a little** money. (কিছু টাকা আছে) vs I have **little** money. (টাকা নেই বললেই চলে)

## 76. Grammar Point: "Until" vs "Till"

| Word      | Meaning             | Formality                                        |
| --------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Until** | যতক্ষণ না / পর্যন্ত | More formal — used in writing and speech         |
| **Till**  | যতক্ষণ না / পর্যন্ত | More informal/casual — common in everyday speech |

> 🧠 Both **until** and **till** mean the same thing and are interchangeable in most situations. "Until" is preferred in formal writing; "till" is more common in casual spoken English.

### Structure

> **I will + [action] + until/till + [condition/time]**

### 📌 Model Sentences

1. **তুমি না ঘুমানো পর্যন্ত আমি অপেক্ষা করব।**
   → **I will wait until you sleep.**

2. **তুমি না ঘুমানো পর্যন্ত আমি অপেক্ষা করব।**
   → **I will wait till you don't sleep.**

> ⚠️ **Important note on negatives with "until/till":**
> In English, "I will wait until you sleep" and "I will wait till you don't sleep" express the same meaning in common spoken use. However, strictly grammatically:
>
> - ✅ **I will wait until you sleep.** (standard)
> - The version with "don't" is a common spoken pattern but grammatically the negative is redundant — the meaning of "until" already implies "not yet."

### More Examples

| Sentence                           | Meaning                              |
| ---------------------------------- | ------------------------------------ |
| I will study **until** midnight.   | আমি মধ্যরাত পর্যন্ত পড়ব।            |
| Wait **till** I come back.         | আমি ফিরে না আসা পর্যন্ত অপেক্ষা করো। |
| She won't stop **until** she wins. | সে জেতার আগ পর্যন্ত থামবে না।        |

## 79. Common English Mistakes by Bengali Speakers (and How to Fix Them)

These are errors that Bengali speakers make very frequently. Knowing them helps you avoid them.

| ❌ Wrong                       | ✅ Correct                                     | Why                                                                 |
| ------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| I am understanding you.        | I understand you.                              | "Understand" is a stative verb — no -ing                            |
| I am liking this.              | I like this.                                   | "Like" is stative — no -ing                                         |
| She is having a car.           | She has a car.                                 | "Have" (possession) is stative                                      |
| I went to home.                | I went home.                                   | No "to" before "home"                                               |
| I am come from Dhaka.          | I am from Dhaka. / I came from Dhaka.          | "Am come" is never correct                                          |
| I have done this yesterday.    | I did this yesterday.                          | Past simple with "yesterday," not present perfect                   |
| She is more beautiful than me. | She is more beautiful than I am. / ...than me. | "than me" is acceptable in spoken English                           |
| He do not go.                  | He does not go.                                | He/She/It → does (not "do")                                         |
| I can to go.                   | I can go.                                      | Modal verbs never take "to"                                         |
| My all friends.                | All my friends.                                | "All" comes before "my"                                             |
| I have 25 years.               | I am 25 years old.                             | Age uses "am," not "have"                                           |
| She is very genius.            | She is a genius. / She is very intelligent.    | "Genius" is a noun, not an adjective                                |
| Please do one thing.           | Please do something.                           | "Do one thing" is a direct Bangla translation — not natural English |
| I am agree.                    | I agree.                                       | "Agree" is a verb, not an adjective — no "am" needed                |
| What is your good name?        | What is your name?                             | "Good name" is a Bangla-influenced phrase — not used in English     |

## 80. Essential Prepositions — In / On / At

Prepositions of **place** and **time** are among the most confusing for Bengali speakers. Here is the complete rule:

### Prepositions of PLACE

| Preposition | Use                                   | Examples                                         |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **in**      | Inside an enclosed space / large area | in the bag, in the room, in Dhaka, in Bangladesh |
| **on**      | On a surface                          | on the table, on the rooftop, on the wall        |
| **at**      | A specific point/location             | at home, at school, at the bus stop, at the door |

### Prepositions of TIME

| Preposition | Use                                  | Examples                                       |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------- |
| **in**      | Months, years, seasons, parts of day | in January, in 2024, in summer, in the morning |
| **on**      | Specific days and dates              | on Monday, on 15 August, on my birthday        |
| **at**      | Specific times, holidays             | at 6 AM, at noon, at night, at Eid             |

> 🧠 **Easy memory rule:**
> Think of a funnel — **in** is the biggest (countries, cities, rooms), **on** is medium (surfaces, days), **at** is the smallest/most specific (exact point, exact time).

### 📌 Practice Sentences

1. I study **\_\_** the morning. (in)
2. The pen is **\_\_** the table. (on)
3. I will meet you **\_\_** 5 PM. (at)
4. She lives **\_\_** Chattogram. (in)
5. I was born **\_\_** Friday. (on)

## 81. Question Tags — Adding a "Tail" to a Statement

Question tags turn a statement into a question. They are used to confirm information or seek agreement.

### Rule

- **Positive statement + negative tag?**
- **Negative statement + positive tag?**

| Statement              | Tag           | Full Sentence                     |
| ---------------------- | ------------- | --------------------------------- |
| You are a student,     | aren't you?   | You are a student, aren't you?    |
| She can speak English, | can't she?    | She can speak English, can't she? |
| They have gone,        | haven't they? | They have gone, haven't they?     |
| He doesn't like rice,  | does he?      | He doesn't like rice, does he?    |
| You can't swim,        | can you?      | You can't swim, can you?          |
| It is raining,         | isn't it?     | It is raining, isn't it?          |
| I am late,             | aren't I?     | I am late, aren't I?              |

> 🧠 **Key rule:** The tag uses the **same auxiliary verb** as the main sentence, but **reversed** (positive ↔ negative).

### 📌 Common Question Tags Quick Reference

| Main Verb     | Positive Tag       | Negative Tag              |
| ------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------- |
| is / are / am | is it? / are they? | isn't it? / aren't they?  |
| was / were    | was it?            | wasn't it?                |
| do / does     | do you?            | don't you? / doesn't she? |
| did           | did he?            | didn't he?                |
| can           | can you?           | can't you?                |
| will          | will you?          | won't you?                |
| have / has    | have you?          | haven't you?              |
